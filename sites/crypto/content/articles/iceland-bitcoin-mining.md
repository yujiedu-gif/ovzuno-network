Bitcoin mining in Iceland uses the same Bitcoin proof-of-work mechanism as mining elsewhere: specialized operations search for work that lets them propose candidate blocks. What makes an Icelandic operation feasible, however, cannot be determined from that protocol description alone. Energy access, grid connection, site conditions, and operating arrangements are local questions that require current evidence tied to the specific facility.

## What Bitcoin mining does in the network

Proof of work is a computational process that takes time and resources to find but is comparatively straightforward for other participants to verify. In Bitcoin, mining uses this process to propose candidate blocks. When competing histories exist, Bitcoin’s design prefers the valid chain with the most accumulated proof of work. The word “valid” matters: computational work does not make a block that violates network rules acceptable. ([source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/Proof_of_work_consensus_model>); [source](<https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf>))

Mining is the block-proposal role, not the entire process by which the network establishes its shared history. A candidate block must be considered under Bitcoin’s applicable rules, and the chain-selection description applies to valid histories. So it is inaccurate to frame an individual miner as having unilateral authority to approve transactions or change the rules followed by the network. ([source](<https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf>))

In pooled mining, a pool can measure contributed work using “shares.” A share meets a pool target but usually does not meet the harder network target. It is a way to measure work within a pool arrangement, not proof that a miner has proposed a network block. Pool payout methods vary by operator, so the use of shares alone does not establish how or when a participant is paid. ([source](<https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html>))

These are general Bitcoin mechanisms, not an Iceland-specific operating guide. They explain what mining does at the protocol level; they do not establish the electricity, facility, commercial, or regulatory conditions for a particular location.
