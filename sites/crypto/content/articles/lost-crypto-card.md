If you have lost a crypto-linked payment card, contact its issuer through a verified official channel and ask how to block it; then review account activity and report transactions you do not recognize. Card controls, disputes, and replacement depend on the issuer and program. Losing the physical card alone does not establish that your crypto account or wallet is compromised.

## Secure a lost crypto card immediately

Start with the card issuer’s official app or website. If it offers a lock, freeze, or block control, use the instructions shown there and confirm the card’s status. If you cannot access a control, or believe the card was stolen, contact the issuer using contact details you can verify independently. Ask whether the card is blocked and whether the issuer requires any additional action.

Do not assume that every crypto-linked card has the same controls or that an app option is available. The label “cryptocurrency card” describes a card linked in some way to digital assets and a traditional card-payment network; it does not establish where the crypto is held or how a purchase is funded. For that reason, the card program’s own instructions are more relevant to the immediate response than assumptions based on the word “crypto.”

When seeking help, do not share passwords, one-time codes, recovery phrases, or private keys with anyone claiming to be support. Use official channels you reach independently rather than contact details supplied in an unsolicited message or call. If you cannot access the issuer’s account interface, tell the issuer through a verified support route and ask how to report the loss without signing in.

## Report the loss and check recent activity

Note when and where you last remember having the card, when you discovered it was missing, and what steps you have taken so far. Keep a record of the time you contacted the issuer and any instructions or reference information it provides. A clear, consistent timeline can help you explain what happened, but do not wait to establish every detail before reporting a suspected theft.

After securing the card or while following the issuer’s instructions, review recent account activity through the official interface. Record transactions you do not recognize and note how they are labeled, such as pending or completed. Report suspected unauthorized activity promptly through the issuer’s documented dispute or fraud-reporting process. Ask what information it needs and whether the card or account needs any further protective action.

Do not treat a transaction display as proof that a dispute has been accepted or resolved. Card procedures and outcomes depend on the issuer, product, and applicable location. The evidence available here does not establish a universal reporting deadline, investigation process, refund, or liability rule for crypto-linked cards. Ask the issuer how it handles each transaction you contest, and keep copies of relevant records and communications.

A card dispute should also be kept distinct from a blockchain transaction. As one protocol-specific example, a confirmed Ethereum transaction ordinarily cannot be cancelled or returned by the sender through a wallet, although a recipient may voluntarily send a separate return transaction. That fact does not determine how a card issuer handles a disputed card charge; use the issuer’s process for the card transaction. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/guides/how-to-use-a-wallet>))

## Request a replacement crypto card

Once the loss is reported, ask the issuer whether replacement is available and what identity or account verification it requires. Confirm that the missing card is blocked, and ask whether any additional step is needed before a replacement is issued. Do not assume that a card can be replaced automatically, or that replacement eligibility is the same across programs.

Ask about any replacement fee, delivery method, and expected timing before relying on the card for a particular need. Also ask whether a virtual card or other temporary access is offered. These are issuer-specific details; no general replacement fee, delivery estimate, or temporary-card availability can be inferred for all crypto-linked cards.

Check whether replacement changes the card details and whether recurring payments or saved payment credentials need to be updated. Verify the answer with the issuer rather than assuming the old details will continue to work, or that every recurring payment will transfer. If a payment is time-sensitive, ask the issuer and the merchant separately what steps are needed; the issuer determines its card procedures, while a merchant manages its own payment arrangements.

Before ending the replacement conversation, make sure you understand what happens to the old card, how you will receive the replacement, and where to check its status. If the issuer gives instructions through the app, website, or support, use the current instructions for your specific account and program. A general article cannot establish the issuer’s current availability, verification requirements, fees, or delivery schedule.

## Protect the linked account and wallet

A missing physical card is not, by itself, evidence that someone has accessed the account or wallet associated with it. Treat suspected card misuse and suspected account takeover as related but distinct concerns. If you see unfamiliar account activity, receive unexpected access notices, or cannot sign in, contact the relevant provider through its official channel and ask how to secure the account. Review available account-security settings and follow the provider’s documented steps.

A crypto wallet is generally used to manage, store, or use asymmetric keys and blockchain addresses; it does not necessarily store the crypto assets themselves. Wallet designs vary, so card controls do not tell you how a separate wallet is secured. ([source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/wallet>))

If wallet keys or recovery material may also have been exposed, treat that as a separate, potentially serious incident and consult official guidance appropriate to that wallet type. Do not disclose recovery material to a card issuer or anyone claiming to be support. For a self-custody arrangement, the user controls and is responsible for the private keys and recovery material needed to access the crypto assets. ([source](<https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/crypto-asset-custody-basics-retail-investors-investor-bulletin-0>))

Key management refers to protecting and handling cryptographic keys across functions such as generation, storage, distribution, use, recovery, revocation, and destruction. The relevant steps depend on the wallet’s design and custody arrangement. This guide does not establish a universal wallet-recovery procedure, and blocking a payment card should not be treated as a way to recover wallet access. ([source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/Projects/Key-Management/Key-Management-Guidelines>))

## Know what depends on the issuer and location

Card-lock options, loss reporting, dispute handling, replacement availability, and fees can vary by issuer and card program. The process and any rights or liability rules can also depend on location. Check the current cardholder agreement and official issuer support for instructions that apply to your particular card. Do not assume a specific refund, recovery outcome, investigation period, or response time.

If you need information about local consumer protections, legal rights, taxes, or compliance obligations, consult authoritative material specific to the relevant jurisdiction and product. This guide does not determine those rules. Keep the response steps separate: ask the issuer to secure and, if available, replace the card; report transactions you contest through its process; and investigate possible account or wallet exposure through the provider responsible for that account or wallet.
