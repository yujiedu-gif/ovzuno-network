## How WAX-based NFT listings relate to blockchain records

A listing and a token record serve different roles. A listing presents an offer under a marketplace’s process. A token record reflects information maintained according to the applicable contract or network rules. Seeing an offer does not, by itself, demonstrate that a transfer has happened or establish who controls a token after a transaction.

For example, under ERC-721, an NFT is uniquely identified on an Ethereum chain by its contract address together with its token ID. That is an Ethereum standard example, not evidence that a WAX-based NFT uses ERC-721 or follows the same identification scheme. The platform’s documentation should identify the relevant chain and token details rather than leave readers to assume them from an image, collection name, or ticker.

## What NFT ownership records do—and do not—show

An NFT ownership record and the rights associated with the referenced item are separate questions. A token record can identify a token and a recorded holder under its system’s rules, but it does not by itself establish copyright, a license, exclusive use, or ownership of a physical or digital item. Those claims require supporting terms or other documentation. The name of a marketplace, the image shown beside a listing, and the fact of a token transfer do not settle them.

When a listing makes a rights claim, find the issuer’s terms and identify what they say the holder receives. A rights-management or license reference can be associated with a token, but a technical reference alone does not determine legal enforceability or the scope of the rights.
