## What the CoinGlass RSI heatmap represents

RSI is a momentum indicator derived from price changes over a selected window. A heatmap presents data through visual encoding, often using color to help readers scan multiple displayed values. For CoinGlass specifically, however, the evidence available here does not establish which assets, markets, or timeframes its current RSI heatmap displays, or how it arranges them. Those details should not be inferred from the product name alone.

The core distinction is between the value and its presentation. The RSI reading is the indicator value calculated from a price series and lookback. A cell, tile, or color is a way of displaying that value, not a separate measure of market activity. The exact labels and layout are product-specific and should be checked in the current display.

An RSI heatmap is also different from a liquidity heatmap. Market liquidity concerns the time and expense of buying or selling, including spread, transaction cost, price impact, and immediacy. Those are not what an RSI value represents. A color encoding RSI therefore does not, by itself, show how much liquidity is available or how easily an asset can be traded. [^1]

## How to read RSI values and colors

Start with the numeric reading, if the display provides one. RSI describes relative price momentum within its calculation window; a higher or lower reading is an observation about that selected period, not a statement about what price will do next. The color is a visual shorthand for the reading, and its meaning depends on the legend attached to that particular view.

Do not assume that a color has a universal meaning across products, charts, or even different views in the same product. A red cell, for example, cannot be assigned a particular interpretation without seeing the display’s legend and labels. The evidence available here does not verify CoinGlass’s current colors, value bands, labels, or whether it uses named threshold categories. Keep two observations separate. “This cell displays a particular RSI value” describes what the interface shows. “The asset is about to rise or fall” is a prediction that the value alone does not establish. Colors can make comparisons easier to scan, but visual intensity does not add predictive certainty to the underlying reading.

## Limits of using an RSI heatmap alone

RSI describes price movement over a selected past window. It does not establish what happens next, explain every factor affecting an asset, or independently verify the completeness or quality of the underlying market data. A heatmap can organize readings visually, but it cannot remove the need to understand their calculation and context.

A single indicator view also cannot establish whether a market is liquid, whether an order could be executed at a particular price, or what an asset’s future value will be. Those questions are different from what an RSI value measures. Avoid treating a color or conventional threshold label as an automatic buy, sell, overbought, or reversal instruction. Such labels do not turn a historical-window indicator into a guaranteed outcome or a standalone decision rule.

This distinction keeps interpretation within the evidence. A reader can report what the displayed value and legend say, while withholding conclusions about future price direction or execution conditions that the indicator does not establish. This is an explanation of the visualization, not a trading recommendation.
