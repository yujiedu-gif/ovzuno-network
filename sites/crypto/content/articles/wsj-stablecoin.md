## How stablecoins fit into settlement

A stablecoin aims to maintain a stable value relative to a specified asset, currency, or basket through a stated stabilization mechanism. “Settlement” broadly means completing and recording a transfer between parties. In a token-based system, a stablecoin transfer can be recorded on a blockchain, which is the transaction-recording network used by the token.

That on-network record is one part of the picture. It does not automatically establish that a holder can redeem the token, that a bank transfer has taken place, or that every legal obligation between parties has been discharged. Those outcomes depend on the relevant issuer, service, agreement, and process—not on the phrase “WSJ stablecoin.” The token’s stated value objective also should not be mistaken for proof that its market value or redemption value always matches its reference.

Stablecoins do not all use the same design. For example, a crypto-collateralized stablecoin can depend on smart-contract positions, price oracles, collateral-ratio rules, and liquidation processes. The existence of that model does not tell a reader which design an unspecified “WSJ stablecoin” might use. Since the phrase does not identify a token, no particular reserve arrangement, redemption path, or settlement procedure can be inferred from it.

A useful way to read the term is to separate three questions: what token is being discussed, what record or transfer is completed on a network, and what additional steps—if any—are required outside that network. Keeping those questions distinct avoids treating a recorded token movement as proof of a broader banking or legal outcome.
