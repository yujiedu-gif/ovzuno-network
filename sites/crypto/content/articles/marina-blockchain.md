## Evidence that can establish a blockchain’s identity

Start with documentation attributable to the entity that claims to operate, govern, or maintain the network. It should explicitly name the network and explain the relationship between that name and any organization, application, or token. If the material does not make those relationships clear, do not assume them.

Then look for technical material that describes the network itself. Useful identifying details include its ledger records, how participants validate or agree on updates, and the identifiers that distinguish it from other networks. A source that merely uses the word “blockchain” or describes a product as blockchain-based may not answer whether it is naming a separate network.

A practical verification sequence is:

1. **Find an attributable primary source.** Identify who published the documentation and whether that source speaks for the claimed network or project.
2. **Check the stated relationship.** Confirm whether “Marina” names a network, an operator, a product, an application, or something else.
3. **Seek technical identifiers.** Look for documentation that identifies the relevant network and explains its validation process and ledger records. Do not substitute a token name or brand for these details.
4. **Compare independent references carefully.** Third-party mentions can corroborate a claim, but check whether they point back to the same primary documentation and the same entity.
