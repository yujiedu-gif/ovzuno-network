## What “mining” means in Pi Network

“Mining” can be used in different ways, so the label is not enough to identify what a network participant does. In a technical comparison, distinguish at least three questions: Does the participant perform computational work to propose blocks? Does the participant take part in another protocol-defined consensus process? Does the participant perform an app action associated with a network or token-distribution feature? Evidence for one answer does not automatically answer the others.

For Bitcoin, proof-of-work chain selection has a specific meaning: when competing histories exist, nodes treat the valid chain with the greatest cumulative proof of work as the preferred history. The word “valid” matters. Work does not make an invalid block valid; nodes still apply the network’s rules. This is a description of Bitcoin’s design, not a general definition of every activity called mining.

To tell the difference, look for technical documentation that explains what participants actually do, how their actions enter the protocol, and how the network accepts or rejects competing proposed histories. If those steps are absent, the word “mining” should not be treated as proof of proof-of-work operation.
