## Questions to verify before relying on a service

For any service described as a crypto-payment option, separate the research into specific questions. A positive answer to one question does not resolve the others.

- **Operator identity:** What entity is named as the operator? Is there attributable evidence linking that entity to the product, rather than merely a similar name or an unrelated record?
- **Payment function:** Does current product documentation explicitly describe a payment service and its intended users? Does it distinguish payment processing from wallet functions, token activity, or other crypto services?
- **Asset and network scope:** Does the documentation name the precise assets and networks supported? Check the exact terminology and limitations in the source; do not infer broad compatibility from a general reference to crypto.
- **Payment completion:** What does the documentation say counts as completion or settlement, and which party determines that status? A transaction reference or an announcement that a transaction occurred may not answer the service’s operational settlement question. If the source does not define the relevant status, leave it unresolved.
- **Fees and availability:** Are fees and geographic availability stated in current official materials, with conditions or limitations? If the evidence is missing, unclear, or not current, do not represent a fee or service location as confirmed.
- **Security and recovery:** Are specific security controls and account or asset recovery procedures documented? Identify what the documentation actually covers and what it leaves open; a broad security slogan does not establish the controls or their limits.
- **Responsibilities and support:** Which duties does the documentation assign to the provider, the business, or another party? What attributable evidence describes customer support? Do not infer that a party handles a responsibility merely because it is mentioned in a product description.
- **Claims about yield or compliance:** Is there specific, current evidence for the exact claim? Do not treat a yield statement or a broad assertion of compliance as established without evidence that addresses its scope and basis.

This checklist is a way to identify unresolved questions, not a provider ranking or endorsement. It also does not establish whether a service is safe or suitable for a particular business. Those judgments would require evidence specific to the entity, product, operating context, and relevant risks.
