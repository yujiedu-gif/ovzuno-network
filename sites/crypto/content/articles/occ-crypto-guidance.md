“OCC crypto guidance” is a shorthand for communications from the U.S. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency about digital-asset-related matters involving banks within its remit. It is not the name of one standalone crypto rule, and the phrase alone does not tell you whether a particular activity is permitted or what legal effect a particular document has.

## What “OCC crypto guidance” means

The phrase groups together agency materials that discuss digital assets in a banking context. It may be used broadly in conversation, so readers should identify the specific document being referenced rather than assume that every mention points to the same policy or requirement.

“Digital asset” can describe different things depending on the source and context. For example, a digital token may be a ledger record about an asset and its owner, with programmable rules governing use or transfer. That technical description does not, by itself, establish legal title, enforceability, or the quality of the represented asset. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/tokenisation-context-money-and-other-assets-concepts-and-implications-central-banks>)

The practical scope of “OCC crypto guidance” therefore depends on the communication’s wording, audience, and subject. A document may address a particular institution type, activity, or set of circumstances; the label does not establish broader coverage.

## What forms OCC crypto guidance can take

The phrase is not limited to a single document format. Readers may encounter agency communications with different names and functions, including interpretive materials, bulletins, notices, or rules. Those labels should not be treated as interchangeable: the document’s form and text matter when evaluating what it says.

Nor does the word “guidance” settle a document’s legal character. The phrase may be used informally for materials with different purposes or effects. To discuss a particular communication accurately, verify its full text and official publication details, including its issuing office, document type, date, intended audience, and stated scope. Do not assume that a document remains current or applies beyond the circumstances it describes.

This distinction matters because a summary can blur what an agency explicitly says with a writer’s interpretation. If a specific document is named, its status and any later developments need to be checked in official sources before describing its present effect.

## What activities the guidance may address

At a broad level, OCC crypto guidance concerns banking-related questions involving digital assets. Depending on the source, those questions may relate to activities such as custody, payments, or other services connected to digital assets. This is a description of the topic area, not a statement that any particular institution can conduct any particular activity.

The boundary of a document’s coverage depends on the institution, the activity, the applicable law, and the document itself. A communication about one activity or set of circumstances should not be stretched into a general conclusion about every bank, every digital asset, or every service. Likewise, mentioning a subject does not establish that the agency has approved a product or that all legal requirements have been met.

Keep the roles in view when reading broad summaries. For example, an exchange operator may combine trading, proprietary activity, lending, token issuance, and custody functions, but not every operator performs every function. That example illustrates why a reference to “crypto services” can hide materially different activities; it does not describe an OCC determination or establish what a bank may do. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2022/future-monetary-system>)

## Guidance is not automatically a binding rule or approval

The word “guidance” alone does not establish whether a document is binding, advisory, interpretive, superseded, or otherwise limited. Nor does a general agency communication, by itself, answer whether a particular institution may conduct a specific activity. Those questions require attention to the actual source and applicable current law.

A careful reader should keep three things separate: what the document expressly states, what other legal requirements may apply, and what conclusion someone draws about a specific institution or proposed activity. The first is a matter of reading the source; the others cannot be settled merely by repeating the phrase “OCC crypto guidance.”

This article does not assess the current legal status of any particular OCC document or determine whether any specific activity is permissible. Those questions call for current primary sources and, where needed, qualified legal advice tailored to the relevant institution and facts.

## How to read an OCC crypto document responsibly

When you encounter a reference to OCC crypto guidance, start by locating the cited document in an official source. Confirm who issued it, what kind of document it is, when it was published, who it addresses, and which activity or issue it discusses. If a summary omits those details, treat its description as incomplete rather than filling in the gaps by assumption.

Next, check whether there have been later amendments, rescissions, clarifications, or other developments. A document’s publication date and current status matter, and a secondary summary may not capture later changes. The available evidence in this article does not establish a complete inventory of OCC materials or their present status, so it would be misleading to treat the examples of document types or subject areas above as a current list of operative positions.

Finally, separate the source’s own language from commentary. Note any conditions or limits in the text, and avoid extending a statement about one institution or activity to others. For an institution-specific question, consult current official sources and seek qualified legal advice rather than treating a general description of OCC crypto guidance as a permission, approval, or compliance conclusion.
