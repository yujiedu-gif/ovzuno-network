An **NFT company** is a broad label for an organization whose products, services, or operations involve non-fungible tokens or related services. The phrase does not name one standard business model: the organization might provide software, run a marketplace, or use NFTs as part of a product or service. To understand what a particular organization does, look at its actual activities rather than the label alone.

## What does “NFT company” mean?

An NFT, or non-fungible token, is a distinct token recorded by a smart contract on a blockchain. In the ERC-721 standard, a token is identified by the combination of its contract address and token ID on an Ethereum chain. An NFT company, by contrast, is an organization: it may create or use NFT-related technology or services, but it is not itself a token.

“Non-fungible” indicates that the token is distinguished from other tokens under its contract’s record. It does not, by itself, describe the organization associated with it or establish what rights a token holder has. The phrase “NFT company” therefore needs context. It could refer to a business building tools for NFTs, a service that supports their use, or an organization whose own product incorporates them.

The term is descriptive rather than a precise business category. Two organizations called NFT companies may do very different work. The relevant question is what each organization builds, operates, provides, or uses.

## What work might an NFT company do?

NFT-related work can take several forms. These examples are illustrative, not a formal or exhaustive industry classification:

- **Build software or infrastructure.** An organization may develop systems that create, identify, transfer, or display NFTs. In an ERC-721 system, the token’s contract address and ID provide its technical identifier; applications may use that record as part of a service.
- **Provide tools or services.** A business may offer interfaces or other services that help users interact with NFT-related systems. The organization’s role is the service it provides, not the tokens users may encounter through it.
- **Operate a marketplace.** A marketplace is a service where NFTs can be listed or exchanged. It may be operated by an NFT company, but “marketplace” describes a service, while “company” describes an organization. One company could have a marketplace among its offerings, or the marketplace could be one part of a broader service.
- **Use NFTs in a product or service.** An organization may incorporate tokens into its own offering. In that case, it is using NFT technology; that does not mean its main business is building NFT infrastructure or operating a marketplace.

These roles can overlap, but the label alone does not tell you which ones apply. A company’s product description and the actual functions it provides are more informative than a general claim that it is “in NFTs.”

## How an NFT company differs from an NFT, project, or marketplace

An **NFT** is a token record. On Ethereum under ERC-721, its contract address and token ID distinguish it within that chain and contract. The organization that develops, issues, supports, or uses a token-related service is a separate thing from the token itself.

An **NFT project** is a particular initiative or undertaking involving NFTs. It is not necessarily the name of a company, and the label alone does not establish which organization is responsible for its technology or services. When a project and a company have different names, identify the organization behind the specific activity being described rather than assuming they are interchangeable.

A **marketplace** is a service for listing or exchanging NFTs. A company may operate one, but the terms are not synonyms: a marketplace is a service, and a company is an organization. A business might provide other NFT-related services as well, while a marketplace’s name does not by itself explain its operator or the full scope of that operator’s work.

The token should also be distinguished from the content or rights associated with it. An NFT record does not automatically establish ownership of an associated image, intellectual property, or other off-chain rights. In U.S. federal securities-law context, SEC staff materials also describe third-party tokenized arrangements that may represent a custodial entitlement, contractual claim, or synthetic exposure rather than direct ownership of a referenced asset. That is a specific distinction in tokenized-security arrangements, not a universal description of NFTs; it illustrates why the token’s technical record and the rights described by an arrangement should not be assumed to be the same.

## How to interpret the phrase in context

When a business calls itself an NFT company, identify the activity behind that description. Does it build software, provide a service, operate a marketplace, or use tokens in a product? Those are different roles, and a broad label does not resolve which one applies.

Next, separate descriptions of the organization from descriptions of a token or project. A company name, project name, marketplace, and token identifier may refer to different things. In ERC-721, for example, the contract address and token ID identify a token within the stated technical scope; a project or company name is not a substitute for that identifier.

Finally, treat the label as a starting point, not proof of legal status, reliability, compliance, or token-holder rights. This definition does not establish whether a particular organization meets any legal or regulatory requirements, nor does it determine what rights attach to a particular token. Those questions require information specific to the organization, service, token, and relevant terms. For a basic interpretation, keep the categories separate: the NFT is a token, the project is an initiative, the marketplace is a service, and the NFT company is an organization whose actual role must be identified from what it does.
