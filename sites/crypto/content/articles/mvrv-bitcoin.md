Bitcoin MVRV is commonly described as a ratio comparing Bitcoin’s market value with its realized value. ## What MVRV means for Bitcoin

MVRV expands to “market value to realized value.” In Bitcoin analysis, it refers to comparing a market-value measure with a realized-value measure. The basic idea is a ratio: one quantity divided by another. The term alone does not tell readers exactly how either quantity is constructed.

This distinction matters because a metric name is not a full methodology. A specific chart or report may define its inputs and data treatment, but this article’s permitted evidence does not establish those details. Nor does the evidence establish that all sources use the same definitions. A reported MVRV figure therefore needs to be read in the context of the method that produced it.

MVRV is discussed as an on-chain valuation metric, but that label should not be taken to mean it measures every dimension of Bitcoin activity or condition. It does not, by itself, identify holders, explain their decisions, or establish what Bitcoin is worth. The evidence available here also does not support a claim that MVRV predicts future price behavior.

## How the MVRV ratio is calculated

The conceptual formula is:

**MVRV = market value ÷ realized value**

This expresses the comparison, but the formula is not a complete calculation specification. To reproduce or assess a published reading, a reader would need documentation identifying the input definitions, data source, time represented, and any assumptions used. ## How to interpret an MVRV reading

If a source’s figures are defined and the denominator is positive, a ratio above 1 means its market-value figure exceeds its realized-value figure. A ratio below 1 means the market-value figure is smaller. These are mathematical descriptions of the selected inputs. They do not establish that Bitcoin is overvalued or undervalued, nor do they indicate what price will do next.

Interpretation depends on consistent definitions. Comparing readings across time or between reports requires knowing whether the inputs and assumptions remain comparable. Without that information, a change in the ratio cannot automatically be attributed to a change in underlying economic conditions; it could also reflect different data choices. ## What MVRV leaves out

A ratio of value measures does not reveal the identity, intentions, or financial circumstances of Bitcoin holders. It also does not establish what a coin’s movement means to the person or organization associated with it. Those questions require information beyond the ratio itself, and the evidence authorized for this article does not document how any particular MVRV methodology handles them.

The same caution applies to assumptions about coins that may be inaccessible or about the effect of custody arrangements. These may be relevant when evaluating how on-chain records are interpreted, but the available claims do not specify how such circumstances enter a Bitcoin realized-value calculation. Readers should not infer that a reported figure has resolved those issues unless its methodology says so.

MVRV alone does not establish fair value, future returns, or an appropriate buy-or-sell decision. It is a comparison between two measures, not a complete explanation of an asset’s value or of future market conditions. Treating a single ratio as a decision rule would go beyond what the metric’s conceptual formula can show—and beyond the evidence available here.
