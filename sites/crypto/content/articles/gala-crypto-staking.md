## How to verify Gala’s staking mechanics

Start with current official Gala technical documentation and network-specific announcements. Look for an explicit description of the mechanism, its purpose, and the network or product it applies to. A marketing phrase, community discussion, or provider page may point to a claim worth investigating, but does not by itself establish protocol behavior.

Then verify the particulars rather than inferring them from the label. A useful source review asks:

- Which Gala network, token, or other resource is involved, and what version or date does the documentation cover?
- Does Gala describe the mechanism as part of its protocol, or does an intermediary offer a separate product?
- What role does a participant perform? Does the source identify a node, validator, delegate, or another role, and what does that role actually do?
- What resource, if any, must be committed, and who controls the relevant keys or assets?
- How are rewards calculated and distributed, if the documentation establishes that rewards exist?
- Are lockups, withdrawal conditions, fees, penalties, or other loss mechanisms specified? If not, record them as unknown rather than borrowing rules from another network.

A reward detail should be considered established only when a current, applicable source states it. A general statement that a network or service offers rewards is not enough to determine the calculation, timing, eligibility, or conditions. For example, Solana documentation describes a validator commission deducted as a percentage of rewards earned by delegated stake. That is a Solana-specific detail, not a basis for assuming that Gala has validator commissions or the same reward structure. ([source](<https://solana.com/docs/references/staking>))

For an outside offer, identify who operates it and what the provider says happens to the asset. Check whether the provider describes user custody, a contract, delegated participation, or another arrangement, and compare those statements with Gala’s own materials. The provider’s terms should be treated as provider-specific, not as Gala protocol rules.

Nor does the word “staking” establish who holds the asset, what activity generates a stated reward, or what happens when a withdrawal is requested.
