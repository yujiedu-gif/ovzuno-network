A DAO considering an AVAX digital asset treasury should define its purpose, decision rights, custody responsibilities, and review process before authorizing any action. The evidence available for this explainer does not establish AVAX-specific technical, market, legal, tax, or custody details. Those points require current, authoritative verification; this article does not determine whether AVAX is suitable for a particular DAO.

## What an AVAX treasury mandate should define

A treasury mandate is the DAO’s documented boundary for a treasury decision: its purpose, permitted uses, approval authority, and conditions for review. For an AVAX holding, the mandate should say what objective it is intended to serve and which actions are permitted. It should also identify actions that are outside scope, so a general authorization is not mistaken for permission to take any related action.

Keep the mandate focused on treasury decisions. A DAO should distinguish deciding whether to hold an asset from deciding whether to participate in any network governance process. The evidence available here does not establish AVAX-specific governance rights or participation procedures. A DAO should verify any proposed network participation against current authoritative documentation, rather than assume that holding AVAX automatically provides a particular right or role.

The mandate should identify the decision boundaries contributors need to apply: who may propose a change, who reviews it, who approves it, and who may execute it. It should also specify limits and what kinds of new evidence, operational problems, or policy changes would trigger reconsideration. These are matters for the DAO’s own rules; this explainer does not prescribe a universal structure.

Finally, separate the governance mandate from questions it cannot answer by itself. A policy document does not establish the DAO’s legal status, tax treatment, custody arrangement, or contributors’ rights. Those matters require review based on the DAO’s circumstances and relevant jurisdictions.

## How DAO decision rights apply to AVAX holdings

Before authorizing an action, map its decision path: proposal, review, approval, execution, and recordkeeping. Name the body or role responsible at each stage under the DAO’s actual rules. A general statement that “the community approved it” may not, by itself, explain which procedure was followed or who is authorized to carry out the decision.

The decision record should describe the proposed action, the approval mechanism used, the authorized execution path, and the rationale. It should also note any conditions or limits attached to approval and identify how a later change to the mandate must be authorized. This keeps the policy decision distinct from the operational act that implements it.

Do not assume that one governance model applies to all DAOs. The relevant procedure is the one set by the DAO’s own contracts, policies, and other governing arrangements. For an illustrative case, a DAO might require one process to approve a treasury action and a separate authorized role to execute it. That example is not a universal rule: the DAO must verify that its own rules permit the arrangement and explain how the two steps relate.

A record of on-chain activity may help contributors follow transactions, but it should not be treated as a complete record of authorization, rationale, or accounting. Define how the DAO will connect transaction records to approvals and identify any activity or information that the record does not cover.

## Operational controls for an AVAX digital asset treasury

Approval and custody are separate questions. A crypto wallet generally stores the private keys or passcodes used to access and authorize crypto assets; it does not store the assets themselves. This distinction makes it important to document both the DAO’s decision process and the arrangement through which authorized actions are carried out.

The DAO should state who or what role is responsible for custody access, how a proposed action is checked against the relevant approval, and how execution is verified and recorded. If proposal, approval, and execution are assigned separately, document how those responsibilities connect and how changes to access authority are approved. The evidence available here does not establish a tested AVAX custody configuration or a recovery procedure, so do not describe a particular setup as proven or recoverable without separate evidence.

Operational policy should also explain how records are compared with approved actions, how activity requiring attention is raised, and who receives an escalation. State who may take any permitted response and which approvals are needed to resume or change operations. These are control questions for the DAO to resolve; naming a procedure does not prove that it prevents incidents or guarantees recovery.

The policy and the operational process should remain aligned. If an authorized decision changes the mandate, the DAO should specify how that change reaches the roles responsible for custody and execution. Conversely, records of executed actions should allow contributors to check whether the action matched the approved scope. The precise process depends on the DAO’s own rules and architecture; no particular AVAX-specific system is established by the evidence used here.

Treat network, custody, governance, and financial questions as distinct review areas. ## A decision record and review checklist

A decision record should connect the DAO’s stated purpose to the authority and controls needed to carry out an approved action. Before adopting or changing an AVAX treasury mandate, contributors can check that it answers the following questions:

1. **Purpose and boundaries:** What objective is the holding intended to serve, which uses are permitted, and what is outside scope?
2. **Decision rights:** Who may propose, review, approve, execute, and amend the decision under the DAO’s own rules?
3. **Custody and operations:** Who is responsible for access, how are actions checked against approvals, and how are execution and records reviewed?
4. **Evidence:** Which current authoritative sources support any AVAX-specific technical, market, availability, or operational assumptions?
5. **Open issues:** Which legal, regulatory, tax, or compliance matters need jurisdiction-specific review?
6. **Reconsideration:** What limits, new evidence, incidents, or changes in the DAO’s objectives require another decision?

Record the rationale, approvals, limits, and authorized execution path together. The DAO should set its own review schedule or triggers; no universal cadence is established here. If an important assumption remains unverified, label it unresolved instead of presenting it as fact. This keeps the decision process clear while avoiding unsupported claims about AVAX suitability, outcomes, or operational guarantees.
