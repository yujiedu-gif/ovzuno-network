## What to verify before relying on a listing

Use a few distinct checks so that a page’s presentation is not mistaken for evidence about an asset or service:

1. **Match the asset identity.** Compare the displayed name and identifier with primary documentation. Similar names alone do not establish that two assets are the same.
2. **Confirm the network and representation.** A tokenized security may use a crypto network as part of its master ownership record, or onchain transfers may trigger updates to an authoritative offchain record. In those issuer-sponsored models, governing documents, applicable law, and transfer mechanics determine which record is legally effective. A page label by itself cannot resolve that distinction.
3. **Review time and data context.** Check the timestamp, units, and any source or delay information disclosed on the page. If these are absent or unclear, do not infer freshness or completeness.
4. **Check service claims with the responsible provider.** A listing does not establish that an asset can be traded, what fees apply, or how security, custody, or recovery work. Verify those details in the current documentation of the provider responsible for the relevant service.
5. **Separate a token record from claims about rights or backing.** For an issuer-sponsored tokenized security, the legally effective ownership record depends on the governing documents, applicable law, and transfer mechanics. Do not infer legal effect from the mere presence of a token or a market page.

In short, Yahoo Finance crypto is a label for crypto-related information presented through the service, not evidence of a particular exchange, custody, or other operational offering. Confirm which information is currently available, read its context, and verify asset identity and service claims with the relevant primary sources before relying on them.
