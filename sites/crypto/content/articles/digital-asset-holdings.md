“Digital asset holdings” is a descriptive category for digital assets attributed to a person or organization. To make the phrase useful, state what kind of asset is involved, how access is controlled, and what record supports the amount. The phrase itself does not establish legal ownership, value, accounting treatment, or tax or regulatory status.

## What does “digital asset holdings” mean?

“Holdings” is a way to describe assets reported or treated as associated with a person or organization. It does not name one technical asset type. Its scope depends on the context, so a useful description should say whether it covers crypto-assets and related protocol-based assets or uses “digital asset” more broadly.

A crypto wallet illustrates why the asset and its record should not be confused. A wallet generally stores private keys or passcodes used to access and authorize crypto assets; it does not store the assets themselves. A displayed balance is information supplied by a wallet or platform, not, on its own, proof of the asset’s nature, legal status, or the rights associated with it. [source](<https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/crypto-asset-custody-basics-retail-investors-investor-bulletin-0>)

The phrase “digital asset” can also refer to a digital representation of something else. In a U.S. federal securities-law context, SEC staff describe tokenization as creating a digital representation of a tangible or intangible asset using distributed ledger technology. This scoped description does not determine what rights attach to a particular token. [source](<https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/corp-fin-statement-tokenized-securities-012826-statement-tokenized-securities>)

For an inventory, define the boundary first. A list limited to crypto-assets and related protocol-based assets is different from a broader list of digital representations. Stating the boundary helps readers avoid treating unlike items as interchangeable simply because they share a general label.

## Which assets may be included?

A practical classification can start with three broad categories: native blockchain assets, fungible tokens, and non-fungible tokens. These labels describe different technical forms, not a guarantee about economic or legal substance.

A native asset is associated with a blockchain’s own protocol. A token, by contrast, may be implemented through a smart contract on a network. Some tokens are designed for interchangeable units; others have identifiers that distinguish one token from another. Those descriptions help classify records but do not, by themselves, establish what a token represents or what rights a holder has.

Tokens can be designed to support or represent different functions or things, including financial assets, access-related uses, and governance-related uses. The examples describe possible designs; they do not establish legal ownership, an enforceable claim, backing, redemption rights, or value. Those questions require information about the specific design and arrangements.

A familiar interface label should not substitute for that investigation. For example, an ERC-20 interface or token label does not specify the complete project-specific rights, service conditions, current availability, or legal treatment associated with a token. A reader needs project documentation and terms to understand those matters; the interface name alone cannot answer them.

As a result, avoid recording only a name or symbol when more precise identification is available. Include the relevant network or protocol and token or account identifier. The point is to make clear which record is being described, not to infer rights from a technical label.

## Separate the asset from control and custody

The asset category and the control arrangement answer different questions. “What is recorded?” concerns the asset and its identifier. “Who can authorize access or a transfer?” concerns control. Neither question, by itself, settles legal ownership.

In a self-custody arrangement, the user controls the private keys and recovery material needed to access the crypto assets. In a third-party custody arrangement, a provider manages access to private keys on behalf of customers. Custody structure, customer rights, segregation, insurance, regulation, and insolvency treatment vary by provider and jurisdiction, so the general label does not establish those details.

A platform-recorded balance should also be identified as a platform record rather than silently presented as a self-managed wallet balance. The records may be useful for describing an account, but the wording should make clear which system reports the number and what control arrangement applies. Do not use “held in a wallet” as a shortcut for an arrangement that has not been established.

For a neutral inventory, use plain descriptive language: keys managed by the organization, assets accessed through a third-party custodian, or a balance recorded on a platform. These descriptions distinguish arrangements without making a legal conclusion. The implications depend on the documented terms and applicable law, which this general framework does not determine.

## How to describe and document digital asset holdings

A clear holdings description separates identification, quantity, control, and evidence. It should also say when the information applies and what the inventory is meant to show.

- **Asset and identifier:** Name the asset category and, where relevant, its network or protocol and token or account identifier. Avoid relying only on a display name or symbol.
- **Recorded amount:** State the quantity and the unit or measurement basis. Keep a token quantity distinct from any separate valuation figure; this framework does not supply a method for valuing it.
- **Control arrangement:** Identify whether access is managed through organization-controlled keys, a third-party custodian, or a platform account. Use wording that describes the arrangement rather than asserting legal title.
- **Record source:** Name the record used, such as wallet data, a custodian statement, a platform record, or an internal ledger. A key-management process covers functions such as generation, storage, distribution, use, recovery, revocation, and destruction; identifying the relevant process can clarify how access is managed, without resolving ownership. [source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/Projects/Key-Management/Key-Management-Guidelines>)
- **Time reference and purpose:** Give the date or reporting period and state the inventory’s purpose. An amount without a time reference may be difficult to interpret alongside another record.

When records disagree, preserve the differences and investigate them rather than silently combining figures. A wallet view, provider statement, platform balance, and internal ledger are distinct sources. Their inclusion in one inventory does not make them equivalent evidence. The description should let a reader determine what was counted, which record supplied the amount, how access was controlled, and when the record applied.

This framework is for making an inventory understandable, not for prescribing a custody or accounting system. Valuation, accounting classification, and legal title are separate questions that may require their own evidence and analysis.

## What the term does not tell you

The phrase “digital asset holdings” does not establish current market value, liquidity, security, or expected returns. A recorded quantity is not a valuation, and the general label does not describe whether an asset can be transferred or redeemed under its particular arrangements.

Nor does the phrase determine tax treatment, regulatory classification, accounting treatment, or availability in a particular jurisdiction. Those conclusions cannot be inferred from terminology. They require current, relevant authoritative evidence and, where appropriate, qualified review. Regulatory recommendations or descriptions of provider practices should not be treated as universal requirements: for example, FATF standards describe preventive measures expected of virtual asset service providers under those standards, while exact legal duties and implementation depend on jurisdiction. [source](<https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Virtualassets/Virtual-assets-fatf-standards.html>)

Use “digital asset holdings” as a starting label, then specify the asset category and identifier, control arrangement, recorded quantity, source, and time period. That makes the description more precise while keeping distinct the questions of what is recorded, who controls access, and what the record does—and does not—establish.
