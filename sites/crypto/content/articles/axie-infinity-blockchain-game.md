## What makes Axie Infinity a blockchain game

A blockchain game combines a game with some use of blockchain technology. In this category-level description, the label does not mean that every game action, rule, or screen is necessarily on-chain. It points instead to a connection between the game and blockchain-associated assets or activity. The evidence available for this article does not establish which specific Axie Infinity features or assets are currently recorded on-chain, so those details should not be assumed.

A blockchain is a shared ledger maintained across a distributed system. Under normal operation, a published record cannot simply be changed by one participant. That can make a blockchain record useful as evidence of a transaction or state recorded by the network. It does not, by itself, describe the full game or establish what a player can do with an asset inside it. [^1]

The distinction is practical: playing a game and interacting with a blockchain are not interchangeable activities. A gameplay feature may be governed by the game's own systems and rules, while a blockchain component may concern a recorded asset or transaction. ## How gameplay and digital assets relate

Game access and digital-asset ownership are different concepts. Access concerns whether a person can use the game under its current rules and conditions. An asset record concerns information associated with a digital item or its transfer. The existence of such a record does not establish that the item is required to play, available to every player, or usable in a particular game mode.

A blockchain can maintain a shared record of transactions, but the record's meaning depends on the system that defines and uses it. It may show that a particular transaction or state was recorded; it does not automatically prove that an asset has a particular gameplay function, that a game will continue to support it, or that every item described by the game is represented on-chain. ## What blockchain adds—and what it does not establish

A shared ledger can provide a record of activity that network participants can consult. In that limited sense, it can help distinguish a recorded transaction from a statement made only in a game interface. But a ledger entry is evidence of what the system recorded, not a complete description of the broader product or the player's experience. [^1]

The record alone does not establish an asset's gameplay utility, future value, or legal rights. Those conclusions depend on factors beyond the technical record, including the game's rules and applicable terms. Blockchain representation should therefore not be treated as a guarantee that an item will remain usable, that a game feature will persist, or that a player has a particular legal entitlement.

Gameplay rules and platform policies also remain distinct from blockchain recordkeeping. The ledger can preserve information under the network's rules, while the game determines how its own features operate. The evidence used here does not specify Axie Infinity's current policy or mechanics, so claims about how the game treats any particular record or asset require separate, up-to-date confirmation.
