XRP is not established as a stablecoin by the evidence available for this article. A stablecoin is designed to keep its value stable relative to a reference asset or unit; the permitted evidence does not establish that XRP has that design. This distinction is about intended value stability, not a claim about XRP’s price or future performance.

## Is XRP a stablecoin?

The evidence available here does not identify XRP or establish its relationship to the XRP Ledger. It therefore cannot support a definitive factual description of XRP’s network role. It also does not establish that XRP is designed to maintain a stable value against a reference asset. On this evidence, the careful answer is that XRP cannot be classified as a stablecoin; that is not a claim about its legal status in any jurisdiction.

The key distinction is between an asset’s role in a network and its value design. A token may be associated with a blockchain or used in a transfer without being designed to maintain a stable value. Conversely, the word “stablecoin” refers to a value-stability objective, not simply to the fact that an asset can be transferred or used in a financial workflow.

## What makes an asset a stablecoin?

For this article, a stablecoin means a token designed to maintain a stable value relative to a reference asset or unit. The available evidence describes one typical design in which an issuer receives collateral before minting tokens and burns tokens after redemption, with offchain reserve assets supporting the arrangement. That is a description of a model, not proof that every stablecoin uses it or that a particular token reliably holds its target value. [source](<https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2023/NIST.IR.8408.pdf>)

Another design may attempt to stabilize value through rules and market incentives that expand or contract supply, rather than through a fully matched reserve of the reference asset. Its stability can depend on demand, liquidity, and the credibility of those incentives, and the mechanism can fail reflexively. [source](<https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/dp/2025/english/usea.pdf>)

These examples help explain why classification depends on design intent and supporting mechanism, not merely a token’s name or transferability. They do not establish how any specific token behaves unless evidence about that token is available.

A stable value is an objective, not a guarantee. A token’s design may aim to track a reference value, but the definition alone does not prove that the target will always be maintained. Nor does a token’s use in moving value or settling an obligation by itself make it a stablecoin: a stablecoin transfer can form part of such a workflow, while the transaction role and the token’s value design remain distinct questions. [source](<https://www.bis.org/media-releases/20220713-cpmi-and-iosco-publish-final-guidance-stablecoin-arrangements-confirming-application-principles>)

## How XRP differs in value design

To establish that XRP is a stablecoin, evidence would need to show that it is designed to maintain a stable value relative to a reference asset or unit. The allowed evidence does not supply that information, so it cannot support a claim that XRP has a peg, reserve backing, or another value-stabilization mechanism. It likewise does not establish XRP’s particular price behavior.

That limitation matters: absence of support in this article’s evidence is not proof that a particular price movement occurred, or that XRP will behave in any specific way. It means the classification should not be inferred from a payment or network role. A token may be used in a transfer workflow without that fact demonstrating an objective to maintain a fixed or stable value.

For the same reason, avoid treating “cryptoasset” and “stablecoin” as interchangeable labels. The first can refer broadly to a digital asset; the second makes the more specific claim that the asset is designed around value stability. The evidence here supports describing the distinction, but not making additional claims about XRP’s market history, network operation, or legal treatment.

## Why the distinction matters in crypto terminology

Precise terminology helps readers understand what a statement does—and does not—say. Calling an asset a stablecoin communicates an intended relationship to a reference value. Calling it a token used in a transfer describes a role in a workflow. These descriptions are not equivalent, and one does not establish the other.

When a document or discussion labels XRP a stablecoin, ask what evidence supports the stated value-stability design. When it describes a token as being used for settlement, keep that transaction role separate from the question of whether the token is designed to remain stable in value. Standards guidance for systemically important stablecoin arrangements says their transfer function should specify when a transfer becomes irrevocable and unconditional and have a clear legal basis supporting finality. That guidance concerns transfer finality; it does not, by itself, classify XRP or any other token as a stablecoin. [source](<https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d206.pdf>)

In short, the available evidence does not establish XRP as a stablecoin or establish that it is designed to maintain a stable value against a reference asset. Use “stablecoin” for the value-stability design category, and do not infer that classification solely from a token’s network association or use in a transaction.
