## What “BlockFi crypto lending” refers to

“BlockFi crypto lending” refers here to crypto-lending activity associated with BlockFi as a company. The central distinction is institutional: a centralized business can sit between customers and lending activity, whereas a DeFi protocol can execute specified actions through smart contracts. A smart contract is software on a blockchain that carries out the conditions encoded in its rules; it does not, by itself, establish the terms of a separate company’s customer relationship.

The word *centralized* describes where relevant operations or decisions are organized. It does not answer who legally owned, controlled, or held any particular asset, nor does it specify the customer’s rights. ## How centralized lending differs from DeFi lending

In DeFi, a user interacts with a protocol through an application or wallet, and smart contracts can execute actions according to programmed conditions. A contract’s rule set can govern on-chain transitions, but it does not automatically cover every activity around the protocol. The application, its interface, or other supporting processes may matter too. For this article, the useful distinction is limited: protocol rules govern the actions encoded in the contracts, while a centralized company can mediate decisions through its own processes and customer arrangements.

The difference is not simply “human decisions” versus “code.” A company’s decisions may be shaped by agreements and internal operations; a protocol’s behavior is shaped by its programmed rules and implementation. Neither description reveals every responsibility or dependency in a specific arrangement. The precise roles must be checked against the relevant company documentation or protocol design.
