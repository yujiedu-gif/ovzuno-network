## What the FTX crypto token refers to

FTT is the name used for the token associated with FTX in the question addressed here. It therefore cannot support a detailed account of FTT’s original terms, identify a contract or network, or describe the token as currently functional.

A useful starting distinction is between a token and the asset, service, or right someone may associate with it. In one broad description, tokenization represents financial assets and liabilities on programmable digital ledgers. That description concerns a general technical model; it does not show that a particular token represents a particular asset or liability. [source](<https://www.imf.org/en/publications/imf-notes/issues/2026/04/01/tokenized-finance-574921>)

The same caution applies to a token’s relationship with a company or platform. A token record, an account balance maintained by a service, and a claim against an entity are different kinds of things. The evidence available here does not establish how FTX accounts were recorded, the terms applicable to FTT, or how any particular FTT holder is treated in relation to an FTX entity. The token name alone cannot resolve those questions.

There is also a difference between a technical record and an authoritative ownership record. In issuer-sponsored tokenized-security models described by SEC staff, a crypto network may form part of a master ownership record, or an onchain transfer may trigger an update to an authoritative record maintained offchain. The governing documents, applicable law, and transfer mechanics determine which record is legally effective. This is a specific example concerning tokenized securities, not evidence about FTT or a conclusion about FTX. [source](<https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/corp-fin-statement-tokenized-securities-012826-statement-tokenized-securities>)

## What functions were attributed to FTT

This limitation matters because “utility” can refer to different things. A description might state an intended design, identify a feature that a platform offered at a particular time, or assert that a feature is currently operating. Those statements require different evidence. A dated primary document could support what a design document said; evidence tied to a specific period would be needed to substantiate a historical operational claim; and a present-tense claim needs current evidence that directly addresses present operation. Without such material, a description of intended utility should not be presented as proof of a working feature or guaranteed benefit.

Token arrangements in general can support platform-based intermediation across the end-to-end life cycle of financial assets. That broad possibility does not establish that FTT supported any particular service, represented an asset, or created a right against FTX. It is background about token arrangements, not a finding about this token. Similarly, the fact that programmable digital ledgers can represent financial assets and liabilities does not tell a reader what a particular token’s rules were. A statement about FTT should be connected to evidence that identifies the token and the exact function being claimed. If the source is historical, keep its time period explicit. If it is promotional or describes an intended use, do not silently convert that wording into proof of independent operation or current availability.

## What to verify before relying on FTT information

For a claim about FTT’s design, seek a dated primary record that identifies FTT and states the relevant terms or function. For an assertion that a platform feature operated historically, look for reliable evidence tied to the period in question. For a claim about current operation or availability, require recent evidence that directly addresses that claim. An old description of intended utility cannot establish present functionality.

If a source supplies a contract identifier, network, or token-standard label, verify that the record is specifically associated with the FTT being discussed and that it matches the claim. The evidence authorized here does not provide an FTT contract identifier, confirm its network, or establish current contract behavior. Those details should not be guessed or filled in from unrelated token examples.

Keep operational and legal questions separate. Trading availability, fees, platform functionality, legal or regulatory treatment, tax consequences, security, recovery, and compliance are distinct matters. The evidence available for this explainer does not establish answers to them or provide a jurisdiction-specific assessment. A statement about one of these matters needs evidence that is current, relevant to the specific claim, and appropriate to its scope.

In practical terms, assess each FTT statement by asking what it actually proves: a historical design description, a claim about operation at a particular time, a statement about present status, or an assertion about legal rights. Do not infer the latter categories from the former.
