## Evidence of a crypto-spending product

To establish whether a specific product supports crypto spending, look for current first-party terms, product pages, or help documentation that explicitly describes a spending feature and its payment flow. A general description of crypto services is not enough. The documentation should connect the stated feature to a card purchase or another defined checkout method and explain what happens to the customer’s funds.

Where the materials provide the information, identify the card issuer, payment network, funding source, and any conversion step. Check whether the product documentation says that cryptocurrency is converted, whether a fiat balance is used, or whether another arrangement applies. These are questions to answer from product-specific evidence, not assumptions based on the words “crypto” or “card.” The evidence available here does not answer them for Yellow Card.

Keep general payment-card mechanics separate from evidence about a particular provider. A payment flow described by one company, network, or product cannot be transferred to another as if it were a universal rule. Even a well-documented process elsewhere would not prove that Yellow Card uses it. If the product materials do not explain how a purchase is funded or processed, mark those details as unverified and request written clarification from the provider.

A useful minimum evidence set is documentation that names the product, specifies the spending function, and explains the customer-facing route from funding to purchase. If documentation establishes only a crypto-related service but says nothing about merchant payments, it does not establish crypto spending. If it describes a card but not the role of cryptocurrency, it likewise leaves the crypto-funding question open.

## Review payment terms and operational details

Before deciding whether a documented service fits a use case, read its current terms for fees, conversion methods or rates, limits, and transaction timing. Check whether the materials explain when a rate is set and what happens if a payment is incomplete or otherwise does not proceed as expected. Look for separate explanations of authorization and settlement. Authorization is the stage at which a payment is approved or declined; settlement concerns completion of the payment between the relevant parties. Product terms should explain, where applicable, what happens after a decline and how refunds or reversals are handled. Do not assume that a refund follows the same path, uses the same asset, or has the same timing as the original payment unless the terms say so.
