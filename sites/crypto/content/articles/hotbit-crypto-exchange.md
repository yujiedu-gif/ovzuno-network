## Where Hotbit fits in centralized exchange infrastructure

A centralized exchange is a venue in which a platform operator provides a market-facing trading service. A common model includes an internal order book: an off-chain record of outstanding buy and sell orders. In that model, a customer typically transfers funds and custody to the exchange before trading on the venue’s internal system. Those materials may document what was represented at a particular time, but they do not, by themselves, establish present availability.

When researching a status claim, keep the evidence types distinct:

- **First-party statements** show what an identifiable operator said and when it said it. They are evidence of the statement; they do not automatically independently confirm every underlying event.
- **Independent reporting** can corroborate or qualify a company statement when the publisher, date, sourcing, and relationship to the specific claim are clear.
- **Archived product material** can help establish what a page described at the time it was captured. It does not alone prove that a feature was functioning then or is functioning now.

A material claim is stronger when its source directly addresses that claim, is attributable, and has a clear date. For an operating-status conclusion, a historical notice and a current access claim are not interchangeable: the former may document a past announcement, while the latter requires current evidence about the specific service. Where records are missing, undated, or in conflict, the evidence supports an unresolved conclusion rather than a definitive status assertion.

## How to evaluate claims about the exchange and its infrastructure

Start by identifying exactly what is being claimed. A statement that Hotbit once listed an asset, a statement that an operator announced a change, and a statement that withdrawals are currently available are different claims. Each needs evidence tied to the relevant time and the named service. Check the source’s identity, publication date, whether it refers directly to Hotbit, and whether later attributable material confirms or contradicts it.

Also check whether multiple articles provide genuinely separate support. Several pages repeating the same assertion may all derive from one original announcement or report. Repetition alone does not establish independent corroboration. If a source is an archive, record what it can show—the contents captured at that point—and avoid treating it as proof of later operating conditions.
