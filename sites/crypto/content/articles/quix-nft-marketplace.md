## How marketplace activity relates to an on-chain record

A marketplace interface is an application view through which a user may inspect or initiate an NFT-related action. A blockchain transaction is a separate signed instruction. For Ethereum transactions, the authorization commits the sender to fields that include the destination, value, optional input data, nonce, and fee limits. The transaction’s fields therefore describe an authorized instruction; they are not the same thing as the wording or summary shown in a marketplace screen. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/transactions/>))

## What an NFT sale does—and does not—establish

A token record and rights in an associated work or asset are different questions. In the United States, transfer of an NFT does not necessarily transfer copyright rights in the underlying work or the associated physical or digital asset. Those rights depend on applicable law and valid contracts, licenses, or other governing terms.

Accordingly, a listing or sale should not be treated on its own as proof of what a holder may do with associated content, what access is granted, or whether other rights have transferred. Those questions require the terms that govern the particular token or transaction. The evidence available here does not include Quix-specific terms or licenses, so it cannot establish what rights, if any, accompanied an NFT offered through Quix.

This also sets a limit on what a marketplace description can prove. Readers should look for the applicable license or other governing documentation and avoid treating a marketplace listing as proof of authenticity, exclusivity, copyright transfer, or legal title.
