## How staking works at the protocol level

At a high level, proof of stake uses cryptocurrency committed as stake, with stake influencing consensus participation or weight. The concrete rules belong to each protocol. Validator selection, delegation, custody, rewards, penalties, and withdrawal conditions cannot be assumed to work the same way across networks.

Ethereum provides one example of network-specific mechanics, not a template for every service. Its proof-of-stake finality design combines validator attestations, fork choice, and checkpoint finality under protocol-defined supermajority conditions. Its incentives include rewards for correct participation, missed rewards or penalties for inactivity, and slashing for specified conflicting messages. These statements describe Ethereum protocol behavior only; they do not establish how a platform feature works or what its users receive.

Other networks may use different arrangements. For example, Tezos documentation distinguishes staking from unstaked delegation balances: staked funds are at risk of protocol slashing for specified delegate misbehavior. That distinction is specific to the described Tezos mechanism and should not be generalized to another network or to a platform’s own loss-allocation terms.

A useful way to read any product description is to ask what role it assigns to each participant. The protocol defines network-level rules. A platform may separately describe the service it operates, while the user’s rights, asset control, and access depend on the terms applicable to that service. Without those terms, protocol information alone cannot show who controls assets, how participation is arranged, or what happens when a user seeks access to funds.

## Rewards, risks, and responsibilities

Protocol rewards are not the same as a guaranteed user outcome. Ethereum’s incentive rules illustrate why: correct participation may be rewarded, inactivity may result in missed rewards or penalties, and specified conflicting messages may lead to slashing. Those are Ethereum-specific conditions, not universal proof-of-stake rules or a forecast of what a particular service will pay.

Slashing is a protocol-defined penalty in some staking designs. The Tezos example shows that conditions can be tied to specified delegate misbehavior, while Ethereum’s rules identify specified conflicting messages. Neither example establishes how a Blockchain.com product, if offered, would select validators, allocate protocol outcomes, or handle losses. Verify both the relevant network documentation and the service terms before drawing conclusions about who bears which risk.

That is a protocol-specific distinction, not proof of a general liquidity rule or a platform’s withdrawal policy. A reader must verify the actual protocol and product conditions rather than infer access from the word “staking.”
