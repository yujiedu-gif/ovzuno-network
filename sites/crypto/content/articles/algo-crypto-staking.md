## What people mean by algo crypto staking

In conventional usage, staking refers to committing cryptocurrency as stake that influences consensus participation or weight. Specific networks differ in how participation is assigned and whether users can delegate. For example, Ethereum uses ETH deposits as slashable economic collateral for validator participation, while Solana permits a delegator to assign stake to a validator and retain token control under its stake-account model. These are examples of other networks, not evidence about ALGO.

The word “staking” in a service description may instead refer to a third-party arrangement. A provider could describe a reward program using that term without the available evidence showing that a user’s ALGO is committed to consensus, delegated to a validator, or earning a protocol-defined reward. Those distinctions matter: the label alone does not establish who funds a reward, what activity generates it, or who controls the assets.

## Protocol rewards versus third-party programs

A protocol reward is a payment or incentive defined by the network’s rules. A third-party program is an offer whose terms and mechanism are set by its operator. A program may use ALGO without being a protocol staking feature; its funding could come from an activity or arrangement that the network itself does not define as a staking reward. Without program-specific documentation, the source of any advertised return cannot be established.

Before treating an offer as staking, identify the responsible entity and check its terms for the mechanism, eligibility, custody, fees, lockups, and withdrawal conditions.
