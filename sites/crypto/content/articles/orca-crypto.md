## What “Orca crypto” refers to

“Orca” may be used to refer to an exchange, an application, a protocol, or a token associated with a project. These are not interchangeable. The exchange or application is the user-facing service; a protocol is the rules and software that may support its operation; and a token is a digital asset. A shared name does not establish how these components relate or what a token does.

## How a decentralized exchange works

At a high level, a DEX can enable peer-to-peer digital exchange through distributed record-keeping instead of relying entirely on a conventional central transaction processor. In some designs, smart contracts—software deployed on a blockchain—carry out parts of the trading process. However, the available evidence here does not establish Orca’s contract design, whether it uses liquidity pools, how swaps are routed, or which assets or networks it supports. Treat those as questions for current, authoritative project materials rather than as universal DEX features.

It is useful to compare this model with a centralized exchange without assuming that every venue works the same way. A centralized exchange commonly records orders and executed trades in an internal order book and account system. Customers typically transfer assets into the exchange’s custody before using its internal trading system, although some venues or products use different arrangements. These are characteristics of the centralized-exchange model, not evidence about Orca.

The word “decentralized” should not be read as a claim that every part of a service has no central operator or intermediary. A project’s network execution, application interface, custody arrangements, and supporting services are distinct points to investigate. General evidence that cryptocurrencies can support peer-to-peer exchange does not resolve how any particular application divides those responsibilities.

A valid signature does not, by itself, establish the signer’s legal identity or that the transaction is wise. Users should understand what they are authorizing and check the transaction details through the relevant wallet or application materials.
