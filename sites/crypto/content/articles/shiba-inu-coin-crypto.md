## What token ownership does—and does not—mean

A token record and a set of rights are not automatically the same thing. A token’s name or association with a project does not by itself establish ownership of a company or project, a claim against an issuer, a redemption right, governance authority, or access to a service. Each proposed right needs supporting technical or contractual documentation; legal conclusions may require evidence beyond technical documentation.

Third-party tokenized instruments illustrate why the distinction matters. A third-party tokenized security may represent a custodial entitlement or a synthetic instrument rather than direct ownership of the referenced security. That is a general example, not a description of SHIB. It shows why a reader should ask what an instrument actually represents instead of assuming that a token label proves ownership of something else.

Even a confirmed technical connection between a token and an application would not, on its own, establish the legal meaning of holding the token.
