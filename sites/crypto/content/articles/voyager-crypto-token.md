## What the Voyager crypto token refers to

A digital token can be understood as a digital ledger record about an asset and its owner, with programmable rules governing use or transfer. That definition describes a technical record; it does not by itself determine legal title, enforceability, or the quality of the represented asset. Nor does it identify a particular token or show that a platform currently supports it. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/tokenisation-context-money-and-other-assets-concepts-and-implications-central-banks>)

“Voyager crypto token” is commonly used as a search term for VGX. But the allowed evidence for this article does not establish that identification from primary token records. It does not verify an authoritative ticker, network, contract address, or migration history. Those details should not be inferred from a name appearing in an older article, account screen, or other description.

Keep the token distinct from Voyager Digital, its platform, and associated services. A token record, an organization, and a service are different things. Evidence that establishes one does not automatically establish the current operation, support, or availability of the others. This article cannot confirm those present-day conditions.

The distinction is important because a digital record may relate to a particular asset or arrangement without itself establishing the legal or practical relationship. Tokenization can combine a ledger record about an asset and its owner with programmable rules, but the record alone does not settle what rights a holder has or how those rights work. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/tokenisation-context-money-and-other-assets-concepts-and-implications-central-banks>)
