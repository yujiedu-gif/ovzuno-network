## What the EverGrow crypto website is meant to provide

A project website can tell readers how a project presents itself: its stated purpose, descriptions of an associated token or service, and links to further material. Those are descriptions of what the project says, not independent confirmation that the statements are accurate or that a linked service works as described. The available evidence does not include verified EverGrow page content, so claims about its purpose, token, applications, documentation, or services should not be inferred.

A useful first step is to separate three kinds of information: an attributed project statement, a detail supported by an independently identifiable record, and an unresolved question. For example, write “the page describes the project as…” when reporting its own language. Reserve “independently verified” for a specific detail backed by evidence that can be checked separately and that actually supports that detail.

Some project websites describe an application as a smart contract paired with a frontend interface. That is one possible application structure, not proof of any particular project’s implementation or of who operates its supporting services. Hosting, key management, indexing, and other supporting services can remain centralized dependencies even when a smart contract runs on a decentralized network.
