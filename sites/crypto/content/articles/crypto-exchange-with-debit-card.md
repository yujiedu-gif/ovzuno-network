## What the exchange-and-card connection means

An exchange account and a card account are not automatically the same thing. A centralized exchange customer typically transfers assets into the exchange’s custody before using its internal trading system, although some venues use different custody, brokerage, or settlement arrangements. ## A practical checklist before relying on the card

Use the product’s current documentation to answer these operational questions:

- **Account connection:** Is the card linked to the exchange account, or does it use a separate card account or funding arrangement?
- **Funding source:** Which specific balance pays for a purchase, and which assets are supported?
- **Conversion:** Does conversion occur, when is the amount determined, and how are any rates, fees, or spreads described?
- **Transaction stages:** How does the provider distinguish authorization, pending activity, and settlement? When are balances and account history updated?
- **Exceptions:** What do the terms say about refunds, reversals, and disputed transactions, including their effect on balances?
- **Eligibility and limits:** Is the card currently available for the relevant location and account, and what limits or conditions apply?
- **Access and recovery:** What documented security controls, account-recovery steps, and card-replacement procedures apply?

The practical decision is whether the documented funding path, conversion point, settlement process, and exception rules match the way you need to manage the account. Exchange custody or trading functions do not answer those card-specific questions. Verify each point in the current card terms and account materials before relying on the connection.
