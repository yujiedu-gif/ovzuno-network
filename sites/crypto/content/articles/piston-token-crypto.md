## How to verify a Piston token’s identity

A sound identification process connects a project’s statement to records on the network it names. Each link answers a different question: what the project claims, where the relevant record should be found, and whether that record corresponds to the address in the disclosure.

Use these checks in order:

1. **Locate a project-controlled disclosure.** Look for documentation that explicitly identifies the token and states its network and exact contract address. The evidence available for this article does not establish such a disclosure for Piston, so no candidate address can be treated as confirmed here.
2. **Confirm the network named in the disclosure.** Network context is part of the identification. A record examined on a different blockchain does not demonstrate a match to the disclosed address and network.
3. **Inspect the address and records on that network.** Once a project supplies a specific address, an explorer can be used to inspect that address and relevant transaction records. Explorer references commonly concern transactions by hash, blocks by hash or height, and addresses; those lookup categories help navigate records but do not certify the project’s claim. [^1]
4. **Compare, rather than assume.** Check whether the address in the project disclosure is the address being inspected and whether the records support the particular statement being made. A matching token name, symbol, logo, or explorer label alone does not establish project affiliation.
5. **Keep discrepancies open.** If project materials give conflicting addresses, omit the network, or cannot be attributed to the project, do not choose one candidate as genuine without further authoritative evidence.
