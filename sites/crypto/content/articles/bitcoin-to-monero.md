Bitcoin-to-Monero conversion means exchanging value associated with Bitcoin (BTC) for Monero (XMR) across separate networks. It is not a native Bitcoin transfer that becomes a Monero transaction: the networks use distinct records and rules, and the permitted evidence here does not establish how any particular conversion process works. It also does not substantiate Monero’s transaction design or privacy properties, so those details require separate, current sources.

## What “bitcoin to monero” means

A cryptocurrency, in the technical definition used here, is a digital asset, credit, or unit that can be cryptographically transferred between users of a blockchain network. That definition describes transfers within a network; it does not mean an asset can be sent directly through a different network’s transaction system. [source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/Cryptocurrency>)

The useful distinction is between a **protocol transfer** and a **conversion**. A protocol transfer moves an asset according to the rules of the network that records it. A conversion exchanges value associated with one asset for value associated with another. Because Bitcoin and Monero are separate networks, a conversion cannot be understood simply as changing the destination address of a Bitcoin transaction.

Bitcoin supports peer-to-peer digital exchange through distributed record-keeping rather than a conventional central transaction processor. That general description does not imply that the Bitcoin protocol itself provides a conversion into Monero. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2018/cryptocurrencies-looking-beyond-hype>)

Cross-network coordination is a separate technical category. For example, bridges for stablecoins coordinate value between blockchains that otherwise maintain separate token records. This example illustrates that separate networks may require coordination; it does not show that BTC-to-XMR conversion uses a bridge, nor does it establish the design of any conversion service. [source](<https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2023/NIST.IR.8408.pdf>)

At a high level, a process that changes BTC exposure into XMR exposure must account for an exchange between assets. Whether it does so through a service, a specific technical arrangement, or another mechanism is not established by the allowed evidence. The label “conversion” alone does not identify the parties involved, who controls assets, or which records settle the exchange.

## How Bitcoin and Monero transactions differ

Bitcoin transactions use digital signatures to authorize transfers through a chain of signed ownership records. A signature is part of the protocol’s authorization model; it does not, on its own, identify the person or organization associated with the signing key. [source](<https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf>)

## What changes when value moves between networks
