An NFT, or **non-fungible token**, is a distinct token recorded on a blockchain. “Non-fungible” means the token is individually distinguishable rather than interchangeable with another unit on a one-for-one basis. The token is the blockchain record; any file, content, use, or rights associated with it are separate things to identify.

## NFT meaning simplified: the short definition

In simple terms, an NFT is a distinct digital token whose ownership is recorded under a blockchain contract’s rules. On Ethereum, an NFT contract records ownership by associating a unique token identifier with an Ethereum address. The record identifies the token and the address recorded as its owner; it does not necessarily mean that the token is the image, file, or other subject someone sees alongside it. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>))

The phrase “blockchain-recorded” describes where the token record appears. It does not mean every detail connected to the token is stored there, or that a record resolves every question about what the token represents. To understand a particular NFT, keep the token itself distinct from references, content, and rights that may be connected to it.

The token’s identity is also specific to its contract and identifier on the relevant chain. A name, symbol, or picture alone is not enough to establish which token is meant. The useful starting point is the record that distinguishes the token, rather than a visual label supplied by an application.

## What makes an NFT different from a fungible token?

Fungibility is about interchangeability. If units are fungible in a given system, one unit can generally stand in for another equivalent unit without treating each as a separately identified item. Non-fungibility emphasizes the opposite feature: an individual token can be distinguished from other tokens by its identity.

A simple conceptual comparison is a balance of interchangeable units versus a list of separately identified entries. In the first case, a system can represent how many units an address holds. In the second, it matters which particular token is recorded. This contrast explains the word “non-fungible”; it does not imply that every NFT corresponds to a unique physical object, carries a special use, or has a particular value.

“Distinct” is therefore a statement about identification, not a promise about importance, authenticity, or scarcity in the broader world. A token identifier distinguishes a record within its contract. It does not, by itself, verify the truth of every claim made about associated content or establish what someone is entitled to do with that content.

## What an NFT represents—and what it does not automatically include

An NFT may be associated with a digital or physical subject, or with some defined use, but the association is not the same as the subject itself. Minting creates a token record and initial ownership under the smart contract’s rules; that act alone does not create, authenticate, store, or transfer rights in the referenced work or asset. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>))

Some NFT contracts expose a token URI, a reference to metadata associated with a particular token. That metadata may describe the token or point to media. The reference and the content it points to are not automatically the same thing as the token record. Metadata and referenced media may be onchain, content-addressed, or dependent on mutable offchain hosting. The token standard alone does not guarantee that referenced material is available, permanent, accurate, or licensed. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)) ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>))

This distinction matters when an application displays an image or other material next to a token. The display can make the token and its associated content appear like a single object, but they are different parts of the arrangement: one is the blockchain token record, and the other is content or metadata that may be referenced separately. The particular storage and reference design depends on the implementation; there is no basis here for treating all NFTs as using one universal storage method.

Rights are a further, separate question. A blockchain record of token ownership does not, by itself, establish copyright ownership, grant a license, or transfer rights in an associated work. Any rights that accompany a particular token depend on the applicable terms and law. The technical meaning of NFT should therefore not be read as a complete statement of what its holder may reproduce, distribute, access, or otherwise do.

A token may also report other information without guaranteeing a particular outcome. For example, ERC-2981 standardizes retrieval of a royalty recipient and amount for a stated sale price. Payment remains voluntary and requires marketplace or participant implementation. That royalty signal does not, by itself, define the token’s complete rights or require every participant to make a payment. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2981>))

## A simple way to remember the NFT definition

Keep three questions separate when reading an NFT description:

1. **Which token is it?** Identify the blockchain contract and the token identifier, rather than relying only on a name or image.
2. **What is associated with it?** Check whether the record references metadata or other content, and distinguish that reference from the token itself.
3. **What rights or uses are stated?** Look separately at the applicable terms. Do not assume that owning the token automatically supplies rights in associated content.

In short, an NFT is a distinct blockchain-recorded token. “Non-fungible” describes its individual identity rather than one-for-one interchangeability. What the token represents, what content it references, and what rights or uses accompany it are related but separate questions. That mental model captures the basic meaning without assuming a particular content-storage design or a universal set of rights.
