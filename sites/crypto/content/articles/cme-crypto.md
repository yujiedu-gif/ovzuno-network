## What “CME crypto” refers to

In this context, “CME crypto” means CME in connection with crypto-linked derivatives markets. A derivative is a contract whose obligations and exposure are determined by its terms and reference. ## How crypto derivatives fit into market infrastructure

Market infrastructure refers to the roles and arrangements that connect participants with trading systems and handle related functions. A conceptual map can include participants and access interfaces connected to trading systems, alongside custody, settlement, data, and external-service relationships. Those relationships vary by arrangement, so this map is not a claim about CME’s specific architecture.

Several functions should be kept distinct. A marketplace provides a setting for trading; brokers or other access intermediaries may connect participants to a market-facing platform; clearing arrangements address contract obligations; and custody concerns control or safekeeping of assets. Settlement and custody are not interchangeable terms. Settlement concerns completion of obligations under a transaction or contract. Custody concerns the safekeeping or control of assets. ## CME crypto products versus spot exchange trading

A spot exchange facilitates transactions in digital assets themselves. In a centralized exchange model, the venue commonly maintains off-chain records of outstanding buy and sell orders in a limit order book. A customer’s trading activity may therefore be recorded in the venue’s internal system, while custody arrangements determine who controls the relevant assets. The details vary by venue and account arrangement.

A derivatives contract is different because the traded object is a contractual position. Its terms define the relevant obligations and settlement mechanics. It can provide exposure connected to a crypto asset without being identical to a spot purchase or an on-chain transfer of that asset. Whether the contract requires delivery, cash settlement, or another outcome is contract-specific and cannot be determined from the word “derivative” alone.

This is a functional distinction, not a venue comparison or a judgment about which product is preferable. A spot exchange’s internal order-book and custody model should not be projected onto a derivatives marketplace. Conversely, calling a product a derivative does not establish its settlement path, required account structure, or the parties responsible for carrying out the relevant functions.

The reader’s useful question is not simply whether a product is “crypto.” Ask instead: Does the transaction involve trading the asset itself or a contract linked to it? Which entity provides access? Which entity handles the contractual obligations? Does the arrangement involve custody or transfer of the underlying asset? Contract specifications and intermediary disclosures are needed to answer those questions for a specific product.
