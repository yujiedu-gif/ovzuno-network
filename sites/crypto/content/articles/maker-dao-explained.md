## What MakerDAO governance is responsible for

MakerDAO governance is a protocol-specific process, so broad descriptions of decentralized autonomous organization (DAO) governance cannot establish its exact responsibilities. DAO governance is a general label for different ways a group may make decisions. Possible mechanisms include proposals, voting, delegation, token- or share-based membership, reputation, and multisignature execution. Those possibilities do not show which mechanisms MakerDAO uses, or which kinds of decisions fall within its authority. [^1]

The authorized evidence here does not identify MakerDAO’s current governance scope or list the protocol parameters and policies it can change. Naming specific areas as confirmed responsibilities would therefore go beyond what is established. Readers should look for current protocol documentation describing governance authority and compare it with governance records that show what decisions were actually submitted and made.

Keep governance decisions distinct from protocol behavior and implementation. A proposal is a suggested action; a vote records a decision under whatever procedure applies; implementation means that a change or action was carried out. The available evidence does not specify whether MakerDAO decisions execute automatically, need an additional operational step, or follow different paths for different decisions. A vote alone does not establish that a change took effect, and the existence of a governance process does not establish that governance is fully decentralized.

## How Maker DAO proposals move through governance

A useful process map separates proposal development, formal submission, voting, and implementation. The authorized evidence does not document MakerDAO’s current sequence or explain the requirements for moving between these stages. It also does not establish voting mechanisms, thresholds, deadlines, or execution steps. Those details must be verified against current protocol documentation and records before being described as current procedure.

When reviewing a proposed process, ask what evidence supports each stage:

1. **Proposal development:** Does a current source explain how an idea is prepared or discussed? Discussion should not be described as formal submission unless the source establishes that transition.
2. **Formal proposal:** What record shows that a proposal entered the official process, and what requirements does the documented procedure specify?
3. **Voting:** What record establishes that a vote occurred and what outcome was recorded? The evidence here does not support claims about MakerDAO’s voting method, eligibility, threshold, or timing.
4. **Implementation:** What separate record, if any, shows that the decision was carried out? A result and an implementation are distinct claims and need evidence for each.
