## What “Starling crypto payments” can mean

A bank transfer and a cryptocurrency payment are different kinds of activity. In a possible purchase flow, a person transfers fiat money from a bank account to a separate service, which may then arrange for cryptocurrency to be delivered. The transfer funds a purchase; it is not itself a cryptocurrency payment to a recipient or an onchain transaction. Whether any particular service accepts a transfer from a Starling account must be confirmed with the relevant providers.

A cryptocurrency payment, by contrast, involves sending a digital asset to a recipient. If that transfer occurs onchain, it is a transaction handled under the applicable network’s rules. A bank transfer does not become an onchain transaction merely because it is part of a process that also involves cryptocurrency.

A third-party service may connect these steps, but the service’s role does not establish Starling’s role. The wording could refer to a bank account used to fund a purchase, an intermediary that offers a crypto-related service, or cryptocurrency sent to a recipient. These possibilities should not be collapsed into a claim that Starling provides a crypto payment feature.

The wording may also appear in an advertisement, search result, or social post. Treat those as claims to verify, not as authoritative confirmation. First identify the precise activity being described and which organization is said to support it.

## Where a bank transfer ends and a crypto transaction begins

A bank transfer moves fiat between accounts. A crypto transaction, when one takes place, is a distinct instruction processed under the relevant cryptocurrency network’s rules. A service can connect the steps operationally, but they are not the same transaction. The use of a bank account to fund a crypto-related service does not establish that the bank initiated, received, or settled an onchain transfer.

This distinction also helps when checking records. A bank record may document a fiat movement. A crypto provider or network record may document a separate crypto movement. Evidence of one step does not, by itself, establish the outcome of the other. For example, proof that money left a bank account would not alone show that cryptocurrency reached a recipient; that would require information about the separate purchase or transfer, if one occurred.
