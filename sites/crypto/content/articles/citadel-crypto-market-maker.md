## How crypto market making fits exchange infrastructure

A short infrastructure distinction helps clarify why naming a market role is not the same as naming an exchange. Market making is an activity carried out in relation to a market. An exchange, by contrast, is a venue or system that can record orders and trades. A centralized crypto exchange commonly records customer orders and executed trades in an internal order book and account system.

These descriptions are not interchangeable. A firm’s participation in a market would not, by itself, show that it operates the venue; and the existence of an exchange order book does not identify which firms participate in it. A search phrase that places “Citadel” beside “market maker” establishes neither role without source evidence.

The distinction also prevents an overbroad inference from business models. Some exchange operators combine multiple services, but that general fact cannot be used to infer the activities of an unrelated or unidentified entity. In this article, the exchange detail is context only: it explains the difference between a market role and venue infrastructure, not how market makers generally operate or how exchange liquidity works.
