## What an NFT ownership record can establish

A blockchain ownership record concerns the token and the holder reported by that record. It should not be expanded into a claim about rights or access that the record does not itself describe. In particular, token control and rights to associated content are separate questions.

A token record does not by itself establish copyright ownership in an associated work, even where it describes ownership of a particular copy. Similarly, transferring an NFT does not necessarily transfer the associated physical or digital asset or copyright rights in the underlying work. Rights depend on applicable law and valid contracts, licenses, or other governing terms.

Words such as “owns the digital asset” can therefore be ambiguous. They may describe control of a token, but that wording alone does not establish copyright, permission to reproduce a work, exclusive use, or continued access through a platform. Rights and permissions may depend on applicable law, contractual terms, product transparency, and external systems beyond the token record.

A rights-management or license reference can be associated with a token as a separate specification. But a technical standard or token label does not, by itself, determine whether such rights are legally enforceable or how broad they are. Readers should look for the actual terms governing the relevant content or feature rather than treating the token record as a substitute for them.
