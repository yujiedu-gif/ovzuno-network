## How crypto-payment infrastructure fits together

A payment service may involve several different activities: starting a payment, observing or processing a transaction, assessing whether it meets specified conditions, arranging settlement, and providing records that a merchant can use. These are useful categories for interpreting a service description. They should not be taken as a claim that every provider performs each task, or that any particular task is part of a CoinsPaid service.

The parties responsible for those activities can differ. A provider might perform a step itself, coordinate with another party, or require the merchant to handle it. A business reviewing a service should therefore ask who initiates or constructs a transaction, who authorizes or submits it where applicable, who monitors its status, and who provides records for reconciliation. The answer should be documented for the exact service under consideration.

Payment status needs particular care. A notice that a transaction exists does not necessarily establish that the merchant’s own conditions for treating an order as paid have been met. As one implementation example—not a CoinsPaid workflow—CDP signing interfaces can return signed transactions that an application broadcasts separately. This illustrates that authorization and broadcast can be distinct actions in some systems. It does not establish how a particular payment provider handles either action or what a merchant should count as settlement.

For business operations, ask what each reported status means, which party determines it, and what event permits the merchant to fulfill an order or record a payment. Also ask how records connect to an order or invoice. A provider’s status label should be understood from its current documentation, not assumed to have a universal meaning.

## Claims to verify before relying on a provider

Review claims against current written materials and the business’s intended use and location. A broad overview or marketing description may not answer the operational questions that matter. Check the details directly with the relevant provider and, where appropriate, with independent authoritative sources.

- **Entity and responsibility:** Identify the legal entity that would contract with your business and the entity responsible for each offered function. Ask whether other counterparties are involved and what responsibilities they take on. Verify the answer in the applicable terms rather than relying only on a brand name.
- **Service scope:** Determine which payment stages the service covers and which remain the merchant’s or another party’s responsibility. Ask for a description of transaction handling, settlement, reporting, and the merchant’s operating duties. Do not assume a provider carries out every activity commonly associated with payment infrastructure.
- **Assets, networks, and availability:** Confirm the exact asset and network combinations available to the intended business, and check that the recipient or service accepts the specified combination. Payment instructions are not interchangeable across all assets and networks; compatibility and acceptance depend on the asset, network, destination, recipient requirements, and service. Verify the exact instructions before authorization.
- **Fees, settlement, and limits:** Locate current documentation for fees, settlement choices, service limits, and conditions. Confirm that it applies to the precise entity, service, location, and proposed use. The evidence available for this article does not establish any CoinsPaid fees, settlement terms, or limits.
- **Security and incident handling:** Request written information about the controls relevant to the proposed service and how incidents are handled. Separate a provider’s own descriptions from independent validation, and do not treat a general security statement as proof of performance. The evidence here does not establish CoinsPaid security controls or incident practices.
- **Access and recovery:** Ask how accounts are accessed and what documented backup and recovery procedures apply. Identify who is responsible for each step and what options exist if access is lost. The available evidence does not establish any CoinsPaid account-recovery process.
- **Records and support:** Ask what transaction and reconciliation records are available, how errors or disputes are handled, and which team or party is responsible for operational support. Confirm how those responsibilities are documented and how the merchant can escalate an unresolved issue.
- **Jurisdiction and compliance:** Check any claims about licensing, regulatory status, service availability, identity checks, transaction monitoring, or other compliance practices against current authoritative sources for the relevant jurisdiction. A general description cannot establish a legal or compliance conclusion for a particular business.

For any point that could affect payment acceptance, reconciliation, or operations, request a written answer connected to the exact service and contracting entity. Keep a record of what is confirmed and what remains unanswered. An unresolved detail is not confirmation.

## Questions to ask during business due diligence

A structured review can help turn a broad description into questions that are answerable and tied to a specific service. Ask the provider, and record the answers and supporting documents:

1. Which legal entity contracts with the business, and which entity is responsible for each service function?
2. Are other counterparties or subcontractors involved? If so, what does each one do, and where are the responsibilities set out?
3. Does any party hold, control, or otherwise handle assets as part of the proposed service? If so, which party, at what stage, and under what documented terms?
4. Who constructs, authorizes, submits, monitors, and reports transactions, where those functions apply?
5. What payment or transaction states does the service report? What does each state mean, and what evidence should the merchant use when reconciling an order?
6. What is the documented process for delayed, incomplete, erroneous, or disputed payments, and for operational incidents?
7. What account-access, backup, and recovery procedures apply, and who is responsible for each part?
8. Which assets and networks are supported for this business and location? Where are the current fees, settlement conditions, limits, and availability stated?
9. What identity checks, transaction monitoring, privacy practices, and data-retention terms apply to this service and location, where relevant?
10. Which current written materials substantiate important claims, and which questions require confirmation from an independent or qualified source?

The purpose is not to assume that a provider performs every function, but to make responsibilities and conditions clear. If an answer is verbal, broad, or inconsistent with the written terms, request clarification. Keep separate notes for facts verified in documents, statements awaiting confirmation, and questions that remain open. This makes it easier to avoid treating an incomplete description as established capability.
