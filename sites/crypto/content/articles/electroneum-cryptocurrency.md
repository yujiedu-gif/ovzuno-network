## Why jurisdiction and activity matter

Start by describing what is happening and who is involved. An individual holding an asset is not the same factual case as a person transferring it, accepting it as payment, or operating a service involving it. A business’s activities may raise questions different from those facing an individual. These distinctions help frame a local inquiry; they do not determine the legal or tax result.

Record the locations that may be relevant, such as where an individual resides, where a business operates, and where a service is offered or a transaction takes place. Which locations matter depends on the applicable rules and facts. A conclusion reached for one jurisdiction should not be treated as an answer for another.

Dates matter as well. A source may have an effective date, address a specific period, or have been superseded. Check the current version of the relevant regulator’s and tax authority’s materials, and confirm that they apply to the activity and dates in question. The available evidence here does not supply a complete, current jurisdiction-specific legal or tax analysis for Electroneum.

The same discipline applies to broad labels. Calling an activity “crypto use” or “a crypto business” may hide important differences in what the person actually does. For a useful assessment, write down the steps in the activity, the parties involved, and the locations before searching for rules.

## A practical verification workflow

1. **Define the people and locations.** Note the individual’s residence, business location, and any other locations tied to the activity. Do not assume all are legally relevant; use them to identify what needs checking.
2. **Describe the activity and dates.** Specify whether the question concerns holding, transferring, accepting payment, operating a service, or another activity. Include the dates under review.
3. **Find current official materials.** Consult the relevant regulator for classification and potential operator obligations, and the relevant tax authority for tax treatment, reporting, and recordkeeping. Confirm that each source addresses the jurisdiction and activity at issue.
4. **Separate unresolved questions.** Write down which points remain open—for example, classification, authorization, reporting, or cross-border treatment. Do not fill gaps by borrowing a rule from another jurisdiction.
5. **Seek qualified advice where needed.** For material, business-related, cross-border, or ambiguous circumstances, consult a qualified legal or tax professional in the relevant jurisdiction. Provide the facts, dates, and official materials reviewed.
