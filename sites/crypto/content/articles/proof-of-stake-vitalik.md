## From design ideas to validator consensus

At a general level, proof of stake is a consensus model in which cryptocurrency is committed as stake, and stake influences a participant’s consensus weight. Selection, delegation, custody, rewards, penalties, and withdrawal rules are protocol-specific. This definition describes a broad design category; it does not establish the details of a particular person’s proposal or every rule of Ethereum. (Source: NIST proof-of-stake glossary.)

For Ethereum specifically, the relevant documented consensus design combines validator attestations, a fork-choice rule, and checkpoint finality under protocol-defined supermajority conditions. In this context, attestations are validator messages contributing to the protocol’s assessment of blocks and checkpoints; finality refers to the protocol’s treatment of history once the applicable conditions are met. These are protocol-level features, not claims about who first proposed them. (Source: Ethereum proof-of-stake documentation.)

## How to tell a proposal from an implemented Ethereum rule

When evaluating a claim that an idea became an Ethereum rule, check the evidence in sequence:

1. **Identify the source and date.** Determine whether it is an individual’s post, a research paper, a formal proposal, protocol documentation, or software release record. A date helps place the statement in the context of an evolving design.
2. **Check authorship and status.** Confirm who wrote the source and whether it presents a suggestion, an explanation, or a specified change. Do not treat a proposal as adopted merely because it is technically detailed or widely discussed.
3. **Look for the change pathway.** Ethereum’s governance description identifies proposal, review, implementation, coordination, and stakeholder adoption as parts of protocol change. Evidence for an early stage does not demonstrate completion of later stages.
4. **Verify the rule in protocol documentation.** Use documentation for the particular Ethereum consensus behavior being described. A general article about proof of stake cannot establish a particular implementation detail.
5. **Keep attribution separate from status.** Even if a feature appears in current documentation, that fact alone does not establish who originated it. Conversely, an attributable proposal does not establish that it is currently deployed.

If the source record does not connect these steps, state the uncertainty. This method avoids turning an explanatory essay into a protocol specification or presenting a historical proposal as a current network rule.
