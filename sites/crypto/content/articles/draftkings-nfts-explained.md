A DraftKings-branded digital collectible should be understood as a possible combination of a distinct blockchain token and the content or experience associated with it—not as automatic proof that the token holder owns copyright or has permanent access to a platform feature. The evidence available for this explanation does not establish the specific token design, current product status, or rights attached to any particular DraftKings collectible. Those details must be checked in the applicable official documentation and terms.

## What “DraftKings NFTs” refers to

The phrase “DraftKings NFTs” points to NFTs associated with DraftKings-branded digital collectibles. But a brand name alone does not establish how a particular collectible is implemented, what it contains, or whether it is currently offered. This explanation therefore does not assume a specific blockchain, token standard, contract, or service arrangement.

An NFT, or non-fungible token, is a distinct token recorded by a smart contract. In Ethereum’s high-level model, an NFT contract records ownership by associating a unique token identifier with an address. That record can identify who controls the token under the contract’s rules; it does not, by itself, describe every right connected to the item the token represents. [source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)

This distinction matters because “the NFT” can refer loosely to several different things: the token record, a collectible image or other media, and any experience or benefit described by a platform. These components should not be treated as interchangeable. Without documentation for a particular collectible, it is not possible to say where its media is stored, how its token is structured, or what associated features are included.

## How an NFT record differs from a collectible

A token is a record governed by a contract; the collectible is the content or subject that people associate with that record. Minting an NFT creates a token record and assigns initial ownership under the smart contract’s rules. Minting alone does not create, authenticate, store, or transfer rights in the referenced work or asset. [source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)

In practice, a token may be associated with a media file or description, but that association does not make the token and media the same thing. Nor does the token record alone establish that the content will remain available, that a platform will continue displaying it, or that the token holder has permission to reuse it. These are separate questions requiring evidence about the specific implementation and applicable terms.

A standardized token feature should also be read narrowly. For example, ERC-2981 standardizes a way to retrieve royalty information—a recipient and amount for a stated sale price. The standard describes that information; royalty payment remains voluntary and depends on marketplace or participant implementation. It is not proof of a guaranteed payment or of rights in the underlying content. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2981>)

## What ownership may—and may not—mean

“Ownership” can refer to control of the token according to its contract, while copyright, a license, permission to reproduce or distribute content, and access to a platform feature are different matters. An NFT ownership record does not automatically convey copyright, physical title, or every offchain right. The rights that apply depend on additional legal terms and systems. [source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)

Likewise, a token’s existence does not establish that a particular benefit is attached to it, how long that benefit lasts, or whether it can be transferred with the token. Those claims require applicable official terms and product documentation. A reader should not infer guaranteed access, permanence, resale value, or commercial-use permission from the fact that a collectible is called an NFT.

For a particular collectible, read the terms that apply to the token and its associated content. Look for what rights are expressly granted, any restrictions on use or transfer, and the conditions for any stated platform access or other feature. If the terms do not establish a right or benefit, the token record alone is not evidence that it exists.

## What to verify before relying on a claim

Before relying on a product-specific description, look for official materials that identify the collectible and explain its token record, referenced content, and applicable rights. Check whether claims about access, transfer, or other features are stated in current terms and what conditions those terms impose. If documentation does not identify a technical detail, avoid assuming it from the brand or the word “NFT.”

Availability, geographic restrictions, fees, and service status are time-sensitive product questions. This explanation does not establish any of them; verify them through current authoritative materials rather than treating a general description as confirmation. Similarly, regulatory or tax conclusions require evidence specific to the relevant jurisdiction and circumstances, and are outside the scope of this definition.

Treat claims about security, custody, recovery, or compliance as unverified unless supported by documentation relevant to the specific service and jurisdiction. The key interpretive boundary is simple: a token record can identify token ownership under its contract, but the content, intellectual-property rights, platform access, and other benefits require separate evidence. [source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)
