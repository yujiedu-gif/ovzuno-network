## How a crypto-funded card purchase may be processed

A purchase may include an authorization stage, a funding decision, a possible conversion from digital assets to fiat, and a settlement process. These stages are a way to frame the questions to investigate; they are not confirmation of the KuCoin card’s transaction path.

In one program model described by Visa, digital assets may be converted to local fiat at the point of sale, with merchants receiving standard payments through Visa’s existing settlement process. That is a description of Visa’s programs, not a universal rule for crypto-linked cards and not evidence that a KuCoin card uses that model. Other products may differ in conversion timing and in the merchant-side payment path.

For a specific card, find the official explanation of what happens from purchase request through settlement. In particular, check:

- **Funding source:** Which account, asset, or balance is used, and whether the cardholder must load funds in advance.
- **Conversion timing:** Whether any crypto-to-fiat conversion occurs before loading, during purchase authorization, or at another point.
- **Settlement currency and debiting:** Which currency is used in the transaction and when the cardholder’s balance is reduced.
- **Amount changes:** What the terms say about a final purchase amount that differs from the initial authorization.

These are verification points, not assumed features. If the official materials do not describe a step clearly, treat the product’s behavior at that step as unknown. Avoid interpreting “paying with crypto” to mean that a merchant necessarily receives crypto directly; the payment path depends on the particular program.
