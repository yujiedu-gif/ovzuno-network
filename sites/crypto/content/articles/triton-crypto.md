## How to verify a Triton staking or validator claim

Look for primary documentation published by the identified organization or project. It should say what the service does and name the supported network or networks. For a staking or validator claim, the documentation should explicitly describe the relevant function rather than relying on general terms such as “blockchain,” “infrastructure,” or “rewards.”

Check which role the documentation actually establishes. Does it describe operating validator infrastructure, letting users delegate stake, supplying node software, or providing a different service? Those descriptions are not interchangeable. A service could mention a network without claiming to operate validators on it; conversely, a software project could describe node tools without offering a customer-facing staking service.

Use third-party listings, social posts, and search snippets as leads to the primary source, not as confirmation. The evidence available for this article does not include Triton-specific technical documentation or attributable service records. It therefore cannot confirm any Triton staking, delegation, validator, or node-service function.

## What a confirmed service would need to disclose

A general claim of participation in blockchain infrastructure does not establish that a particular network is supported or that a service is currently available.

For example, Solana’s protocol documentation says a validator can charge commission deducted as a percentage of rewards earned by delegated stake; that is a Solana-specific fact, not evidence about Triton or a universal pricing rule ([source](<https://solana.com/docs/references/staking>)). No Triton fee, reward, or yield terms are established here, so none should be inferred or stated.
