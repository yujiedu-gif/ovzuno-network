Proof of stake means a blockchain consensus approach in which cryptocurrency committed as stake influences participation or weight in reaching agreement about the network’s history. The exact role stake plays—and what participants must do—depends on each protocol. The phrase describes a consensus model, not a universal method for staking tokens or a promise of rewards or security.

## What “proof of stake means” in plain language

A blockchain network needs rules for deciding which updates to accept as part of its shared history. Consensus is the network process for reaching agreement under those rules. In a proof-of-stake model, participants commit cryptocurrency as stake, and the amount or status of that stake influences consensus participation or weight. This is the general definition; it does not specify one universal procedure for proposing or checking updates. [source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/proof_of_stake_consensus_model>)

The key idea is that stake has a role in the protocol’s consensus process. A network defines how participants qualify and what influence their stake carries. Those details are protocol-specific, so the label “proof of stake” alone is not enough to predict how a particular network operates.

## Consensus mechanism versus staking tokens

“Consensus mechanism” refers to the rules and process a blockchain uses to coordinate agreement. “Staking” refers more broadly to committing or delegating cryptocurrency under a network’s rules. These ideas are related, but they are not interchangeable: staking may be one way to participate in a proof-of-stake system, while the consensus mechanism is the wider protocol design. [source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/proof_of_stake_consensus_model>)

The relationship varies by network. Protocols can set different rules for participation, selection, delegation, custody, withdrawals, rewards, and penalties. For example, Ethereum’s proof-of-stake design uses ETH deposits as slashable economic collateral for validator participation; that is a network-specific design, not a definition that applies to every proof-of-stake blockchain. [source](<https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/>)

## What the definition does—and does not—tell you

The definition tells you that stake influences consensus participation or weight. It does not, by itself, tell you who can participate, how participants are selected, what operational tasks they perform, or whether users can delegate assets. Those questions require the documentation for the specific protocol.

Nor does the phrase guarantee a particular reward, security level, degree of decentralization, or protection from loss. For example, Ethereum’s protocol includes rewards for correct participation, missed rewards or penalties for inactivity, and slashing for specified conflicting messages. Those are Ethereum-specific rules, not universal consequences of the proof-of-stake label. [source](<https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/consensus-mechanisms/pos/rewards-and-penalties/>)

Keep the term separate from any practical decision to stake. A consensus definition does not explain a service’s terms, custody arrangements, fees, or risks. Those details must be assessed in the relevant network and service context; this definition makes no claim about them.

## A short example of the concept

Imagine a hypothetical network whose rules use committed stake to determine which eligible participants can take part in consensus, and how much weight their participation carries. The example illustrates only the core relationship: stake affects consensus participation under protocol rules. It does not assume a particular selection process, task, reward, or penalty.

Actual proof-of-stake systems can differ in how they define eligibility and use stake. The useful plain-language takeaway is therefore: proof of stake is a consensus approach where committed cryptocurrency influences participation or weight, while the protocol determines the specific mechanics.
