## What to verify before relying on network details

For a dependable technical picture, check current official documentation and look for explicit statements about:

- **Network identity and status:** Which network is being described, and whether the material identifies its current operating status and version.
- **Consensus and security roles:** Whether the design uses validators, another security model, or a combination; what each role is responsible for; and how the protocol selects or authorizes participants.
- **Staking or delegation:** Whether either exists in the documented design, what the terms mean for that network, and whether a described process is protocol-level or offered by a third party.
- **Token roles:** Whether an asset is used for a protocol function, and which official technical source defines that function.
- **Operational conditions:** Any stated eligibility, fees, availability, yields, security controls, or recovery procedures. Treat these as time-sensitive and do not infer them from general staking descriptions.

Record the document’s date and version when relying on a detail that can change. Compare a statement about protocol rules with the source that defines those rules, rather than treating interface text, estimates, or promotional material as equivalent technical evidence. If official documentation does not answer a question, leave it unresolved instead of filling the gap with a description of another blockchain.
