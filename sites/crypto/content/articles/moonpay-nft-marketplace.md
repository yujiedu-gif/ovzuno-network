## What an NFT purchase establishes about ownership

A blockchain record can indicate that a token is associated with an address under the rules of the relevant contract. That record concerns the token; it does not automatically establish ownership of the associated image, file, or other content. Transferring an NFT does not necessarily transfer copyright or rights in the underlying physical or digital asset.

A token record alone also does not establish that a holder has a license, exclusive rights, or permission to reproduce or otherwise use linked content. Those permissions need to be assessed through applicable listing terms and rights documentation. A buyer should identify what work or asset is covered, who grants the permission, and what uses the terms describe. The evidence available here does not include MoonPay marketplace terms or terms for any particular listing, so it cannot establish what rights a buyer would receive through MoonPay.

The address in a record is not, by itself, a verified identity. A valid signature can support attribution of a transaction to the private key corresponding to an address, but it does not identify the human, organization, device, or service controlling that key. Accordingly, an address alone does not prove that a named person owns the token, nor that the address is linked to a particular marketplace account.

It is useful to keep three questions separate: what token the record identifies, which address the record associates with it, and what separate terms grant regarding linked content. A marketplace listing or interface may present information about these subjects, but without the listing and rights documentation, the token record cannot answer the content-rights question. The same limitation applies whether a user encounters a token through a marketplace or another service.
