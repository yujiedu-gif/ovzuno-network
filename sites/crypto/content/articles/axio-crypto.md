## How to assess staking and validator claims

If a source is presented as evidence about Axio, first check whether it is attributable to the entity and whether it names the specific network or service. Then look for a technical description that explains who may participate, what validators do, and whether the rules belong to the protocol or to a separate provider. Without those links, a staking label should not be read as proof of a particular system.

A useful review keeps three kinds of claims separate:

- **Protocol mechanism:** What the network’s own technical documentation says about participation, validator duties, and protocol-level penalties.
- **Service terms:** What a wallet, exchange, custodian, or other provider says about its separate staking or delegation offering.
- **User-facing claims:** What a page advertises, which may not establish either protocol behavior or provider arrangements.

This separation prevents a service description from being mistaken for a network specification. It also avoids assuming that a feature documented for one blockchain applies to another. For instance, Solana documentation says that a Solana validator can charge commission as a percentage of rewards earned by delegated stake. That is a Solana-specific statement, not evidence about Axio or a universal rule for staking services. [source](<https://solana.com/docs/references/staking>)

Where a project makes claims about bridge-based verification or cryptographic proofs, those mechanisms also need to be tied to the relevant system. A bridge’s security depends materially on who or what verifies the source-chain event, and labels such as “trusted” or “trustless” do not remove associated contract, governance, chain, or operational risks. [source](<https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/bridges>) A validity proof, likewise, is a mechanism that a base-layer verifier can accept for a proposed offchain state transition; that description alone does not establish the safety of an unrelated project or service. [source](<https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/scaling/zk-rollups/>)

Do not infer operational security, recovery arrangements, availability, eligibility, fees, or returns from the word “staking.” Each requires current, attributable evidence about the particular network or service. The evidence available here does not supply those Axio-specific details, so they cannot be stated as established facts.
