## How to assess cloud mining claims

Start by asking what specific evidence would support each operational statement. For a claim that a service has mining hardware, seek a traceable connection to equipment and the responsible operator. For claims about pools or current activity, seek records that can be independently checked and tied to the relevant period and contract. The allowed evidence provides no such records for Minelab, so no conclusion about its mining activity follows.

Mining software can transform block-template information into block-header work that hashing hardware searches and reports. This explains why an operational claim should connect work construction to hardware and its output, rather than rely only on a displayed hashrate. It does not establish that a customer can independently observe each step, or that a particular service provides this evidence.

Examine each record for what it actually demonstrates. A record might identify a device model, a pool account, a reported hashrate, or a transaction. One such record does not necessarily establish the full chain from a customer contract to active equipment and then to a customer payout. Ask who produced it, when it applies, what system it covers, and whether it can be checked independently. If a link is missing, state precisely what the record supports and what remains unknown.

Do not treat screenshots, testimonials, or projected returns as independent proof simply because they appear detailed. The allowed evidence does not substantiate such material for Minelab. It also does not explain how the platform defines hashrate, contract duration, interruptions, or the relationship between mining operations and payouts. Those are questions for current documentation, not facts to assume.

A useful evidence note can record the claim, the source, its date and scope, the independent check performed, and the remaining gap. For example, a specification for a named device may document that model’s features; unless a separate record connects it to Minelab, it does not establish that the platform possesses or operates it. This approach avoids turning a narrow document into a broad conclusion about the service.
