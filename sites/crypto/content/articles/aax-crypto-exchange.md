Operational status is time-sensitive and service-specific. To evaluate a status claim, start with the source’s date and scope. Identify the named entity and the specific service, then check whether the statement concerns a past event or present availability. An archived page can document what it displayed at an earlier time, but by itself it cannot establish that the service remains available. Search snippets and user reports can identify leads for further checking, but they do not independently verify the status of an exchange or a particular service.

Where applicable, seek corroboration in authoritative company communications and relevant official records. Corroboration should match the exact claim: a record about one entity, region, or product does not automatically establish the status of another. If no current, authoritative evidence resolves the question, say that status is unverified. Do not infer the status of deposits, withdrawals, or recovery from a general website reference or a historical product description.

A concise review record can help prevent scope drift:

- Name the entity and service the source actually identifies.
- Record when the source was published and the period it addresses.
- Classify the evidence as an announcement, a technical specification, a historical record, or evidence of present availability.
- Note whether current, authoritative corroboration exists for the specific status claim.

These steps do not themselves establish AAX’s status. They make clear what evidence would be needed and where the available record leaves uncertainty.
