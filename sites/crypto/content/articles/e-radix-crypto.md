The phrase alone does not resolve these possibilities.

A blockchain is generally defined as a tamper-evident and tamper-resistant digital ledger implemented in a distributed fashion. ## Radix network and crypto asset terminology

A network or protocol and a crypto asset are related concepts, but they are not interchangeable. A network is the system described by its rules and participating components. A crypto asset, in a general technical definition, is a digital asset, credit, or unit that can be cryptographically transferred between users of a blockchain network. That definition does not identify a project-specific asset or establish its name, role, or relationship to a particular network. [source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8202/final>)

To distinguish the terms in a Radix-related source, check three separate points:

- **Network or protocol:** What exact name does the source use for the system? Does it identify the network, protocol, or another component?
- **Asset:** Does the source give an exact asset name or ticker, and does it clearly connect that asset to the named network?
- **Role:** Does authoritative documentation explicitly state what the asset does, and does it describe the asset as native to a specific network?

The evidence available here does not confirm Radix’s official network name, the official name of an associated asset, or that asset’s role. It cannot support naming a particular asset as Radix’s native asset. Do not treat a project name, network name, asset name, or ticker as equivalent unless a reliable source explicitly connects them.

“Native asset” should be used with care. A source should identify the asset and the network to which it applies; a familiar ticker or a third-party label is not enough to establish that relationship. Nor should readers infer that an asset is issued directly on a network simply because a page associates the two names. If documentation describes a representation that moves between chains, that is a separate mechanism to verify.

For general context, bridged assets are not physically moved from one ledger to another. Designs can coordinate value across chains through mechanisms such as minting, burning, locking, releasing, or swapping, and the exact accounting and redemption path depends on the bridge design. [source](<https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/scaling/sidechains/>) This general description does not establish that any specific Radix-related asset is bridged, nor does it establish how a particular transfer works. Such a statement would require evidence about that asset and its actual transfer arrangement.
