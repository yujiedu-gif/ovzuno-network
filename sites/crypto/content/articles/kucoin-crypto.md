## How orders, records, and blockchain transfers differ

An order record, an executed trade record, and an on-chain transaction are different kinds of records. A centralized exchange commonly records customer orders and executed trades in an internal order book and account system. Those records should not be treated as public blockchain data merely because the activity concerns cryptoassets. The available evidence does not establish which of KuCoin’s operational records, if any, are published on a blockchain.

A deposit or withdrawal may involve a blockchain transfer, but that is a separate process from placing or matching an order in a venue’s internal system. If a blockchain transaction is used, the network’s record concerns that transaction; it does not by itself reveal every internal account entry or trading event connected with it. Conversely, an internal trade entry is not evidence that a separate blockchain transfer occurred.

The exchange and the network also have different roles. An exchange’s trading and account systems concern activity recorded within its service arrangement. A blockchain network maintains its own transaction records under the network’s rules. The exact link between the two depends on the service, asset, and transfer arrangement.
