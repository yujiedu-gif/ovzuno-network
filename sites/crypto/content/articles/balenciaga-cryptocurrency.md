## How to verify a Balenciaga crypto-payment claim

Begin with primary material: an official Balenciaga statement, official checkout information, or an official customer-service response. Capture the publication or confirmation date, and note whether the source describes an earlier pilot, a limited offer, or an option said to be available at the time of the statement. A repost or an article repeating an unattributed claim is not equivalent to confirmation from the brand.

Then inspect the scope. Look for the country or region, the sales channel, and any stated time limits. A claim tied to one location or one checkout channel cannot be extended to other countries, stores, or the brand as a whole unless separate evidence supports that broader scope. Similarly, a historical announcement does not establish present availability.

If possible, corroborate the statement against the relevant official checkout flow or support information. The evidence should indicate that cryptocurrency is actually presented as a payment choice for the stated purchase context. A crypto-related announcement, by itself, does not establish that a customer can use crypto to complete an order.

Keep a record of what the source says and what it leaves unanswered. For example, a statement might identify an option but not specify the assets, network, locations, or conditions. Those omissions should remain open questions; they should not be filled in from another provider’s general documentation or from assumptions about how crypto payments usually work. If the source cannot confirm the payment function, date, and scope, describe the claim as unverified.
