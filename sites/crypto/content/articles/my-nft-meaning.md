“My NFT” usually means a person is referring to a non-fungible token they hold or control. The phrase can describe the token record, but it does not by itself establish ownership of a linked file, copyright, a license, or permission to use associated content.

## What “my NFT” means in everyday use

“My NFT” is an informal phrase, not a distinct technical category. In a wallet or marketplace context, someone may use it to refer to a token displayed in connection with their account. More generally, the speaker may intend to say that they control the token, or may be making a broader claim about the digital item or rights associated with it. The phrase alone does not tell a reader which meaning is intended.

A useful first step is to separate the token from what it may represent or reference. A digital token can be a ledger record about an asset and its owner, with programmable rules governing use or transfer. But a technical record does not, by itself, determine legal title, enforceability, or the quality of the represented asset. “My NFT” therefore identifies a relationship someone claims to have with a token; it is not a complete description of every related asset or right.

## What an NFT record can show

An NFT is a token record managed under a particular system’s rules. In the Ethereum model, an NFT contract can record ownership by associating a unique token identifier with an Ethereum address. That record indicates the address the contract recognizes as holding that token. It does not, just from the address alone, identify the person or organization behind it.

Token creation, often called minting, creates a token record and initial ownership under the smart contract’s rules. Minting does not by itself create, authenticate, store, or transfer rights in the referenced work or asset. The token record and the thing associated with it are distinct parts of the arrangement.

An NFT may also be associated with metadata or a reference to content. The existence of a reference does not settle what the holder may do with the content, who created it, or whether it will remain available. Different NFT arrangements can rely on external systems and terms, including those governing content, identity, or storage. Those dependencies are separate from the fact that a token record exists.

## What “my NFT” does not automatically mean

Holding or controlling a token does not necessarily transfer ownership of the associated physical or digital asset, or copyright rights in an underlying work. A token record is not a substitute for evidence about the terms governing the associated content. Rights depend on applicable law and valid contracts, licenses, or other governing terms.

This distinction matters because “ownership” can refer to different things. It may mean the blockchain record recognizes an address as holding a token; it may refer to control of a file or physical object; or it may refer to legal rights in a work. These meanings should not be treated as interchangeable. A token can be involved in a rights-management or licensing workflow, but the token alone does not show that such a workflow exists or define its permissions.

Nor does a token’s association with a digital item guarantee that the item is stored in the token record or remains accessible. The token and the external content or services connected to it may have different dependencies. Without the relevant documentation, a reader cannot infer from the phrase “my NFT” alone what the connection guarantees or what uses are permitted.

## A simple example of the distinction

Suppose someone sees a token associated with an image in a wallet and says, “That’s my NFT.” The statement may mean that the token record recognizes an address connected to that person. It does not, on its own, answer who owns the image, whether the person may reproduce it, or what other rights apply.

To interpret the claim, ask what evidence it points to. The token record can indicate the token and the address recognized under the relevant system. The documented terms for the associated work or asset are needed to understand any additional rights or permissions. If those terms are not available, the careful interpretation is limited: the person is referring to a token they say is theirs, not necessarily claiming every right in the content associated with it.
