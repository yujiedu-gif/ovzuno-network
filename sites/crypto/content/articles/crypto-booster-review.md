## Verify the payment flow and supported assets

Ask for a step-by-step description of a payment, from customer initiation through confirmation and the business’s receipt or settlement of funds. The description should distinguish the customer’s wallet, any application or processor, the blockchain network, and the merchant’s own systems. It should also define each status the product displays and explain which status, if any, corresponds to the merchant’s own fulfillment conditions. A general payment-processing principle is that a broadcast transaction alone should not fulfill an invoice; the merchant’s configured settlement conditions matter. This does not establish how Crypto Booster handles transactions or statuses.

Request an explicit, current list of supported assets and networks. Confirm the exact token representation and destination details required for each payment path; a familiar asset name or symbol does not establish compatibility. Provider documentation for stablecoin deposits, for example, can be specific to a chain and token representation, and unsupported or bridged variants may not be credited or recoverable. That is a reason to verify exact combinations with a prospective provider, not evidence that Crypto Booster supports any of them.

If conversion, custody, or settlement involves another service, ask who performs each step and what record confirms completion. If the product depends on a separate wallet or processor, identify that dependency and ask how payment status is reconciled between systems. A business should request the complete flow, including what happens when a payment is delayed, incomplete, or sent using an unsupported asset or network. Without product-specific documentation, Crypto Booster’s flow and supported options remain unverified.

## Assess business operations and integration requirements

Request actual integration documentation and prerequisites before estimating implementation effort. Find out whether the documented workflow uses a hosted checkout, an API, a wallet connection, manual payment instructions, or another method; do not assume any of these is available. Ask who creates payment requests, monitors transaction status, handles exceptions, and reconciles payments with order or accounting records. Record which responsibilities fall to the provider, any other service, and the business itself.

Settlement timing, transaction fees, service availability, support coverage, refund handling, and treatment of partial, late, or overpaid transactions all require direct evidence. No current Crypto Booster terms on these matters are established here. Request the applicable written terms and ask whether they vary by asset, network, payment method, or operating region. ## Review security, recovery, and compliance evidence

Request documentation identifying the custody model and explaining how keys and other sensitive credentials are generated, stored, used, recovered, and, when necessary, revoked or destroyed. Key management covers the lifecycle of keying material, but naming a process is not proof that a specific implementation is secure. Ask who can authorize a transaction, what happens if access credentials are lost, and which party is responsible for recovery. The evidence available for this review does not establish Crypto Booster’s answers.

Look for specific security documentation rather than broad assurances. Ask whether the provider describes incident reporting, response responsibilities, and customer notification. If a product cites a bug bounty, treat it as one element rather than a complete security case: a bug bounty is a defined reporting and reward process intended to encourage responsible disclosure, and it complements rather than replaces secure design, testing, independent audit, monitoring, and incident response. No security program or bounty for Crypto Booster is verified here.

Ask the provider to state which compliance responsibilities it claims to handle and which remain with the business, and to identify the jurisdictions and product terms covered by those statements. ## Use a decision checklist before proceeding

Before assessing fit, write down each business requirement, the evidence needed to address it, and who must provide that evidence. A practical checklist includes:

- **Product identity and purpose:** Obtain current documentation identifying Crypto Booster and describing its role in crypto payments.
- **Payment flow:** Request the customer-to-merchant sequence, status definitions, confirmation conditions, and exception handling.
- **Assets and networks:** Confirm exact supported assets and network representations, and ask how unsupported payments are handled.
- **Integration and responsibilities:** Obtain technical prerequisites and identify the tasks assigned to the provider, other services, and the business.
- **Commercial and operational terms:** Request written terms for fees, settlement, availability, support, refunds, and reconciliation.
- **Security and recovery:** Obtain the custody and key-management description, incident procedures, and account or asset recovery terms.
- **Compliance scope:** Ask for documented jurisdictional scope and an allocation of responsibilities; seek qualified advice where needed instead of treating a provider statement as a legal determination.

For each answer, record the document or written response, its date, the specific claim it supports, and any conditions or limitations. Separate “documented,” “partially documented,” and “unresolved” rather than turning a missing answer into an assumption. If a statement depends on a third party, request that service’s relevant terms or documentation as well. This makes it easier to see whether a capability is actually supported by evidence or merely described in general terms.

A business can assess workflow fit only after its material requirements are supported by sufficiently specific evidence. For Crypto Booster, the evidence available for this review is not enough to reach that product-fit judgment. The appropriate next step is to request the missing product documentation and clarify unresolved operational and risk questions—not to infer features, assurances, or business suitability from the product name alone.
