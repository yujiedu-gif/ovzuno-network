## What “YieldNodes NFT Has Been Claimed” Can Indicate

The wording could refer to a recorded action, an account-specific status, or something else. Without reliable evidence about the particular page or communication, its intended meaning cannot be established. A status shown by an interface is not the same thing as independent evidence of an on-chain event.

An NFT is a token record, and its meaning depends on the contract and other information associated with it. On Ethereum, an NFT contract can record ownership by associating a unique token identifier with an address. That record concerns the token and address; it does not, on its own, identify the person or organization behind the address. Nor does token ownership automatically transfer copyright or ownership of an associated physical or digital work. **[source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>) [source](<https://www.copyright.gov/policy/nft-study/Joint-USPTO-USCO-Report-on-NFTs-and-Intellectual-Property.pdf>)**

Minting creates a token record and an initial owner under the smart contract’s rules. It does not, by itself, authenticate, store, or transfer rights in a referenced work or asset. **[source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)** Therefore, the word “claimed” should not be read as proof of a particular platform promise, entitlement, or wallet control.

Rights and token records must be considered separately. NFT technology may support workflows for managing, transferring, or licensing intellectual-property rights when valid legal terms and authorized rights holders are present. That possibility does not mean the token alone establishes those rights or verifies who was authorized to create it. **[source](<https://www.copyright.gov/policy/nft-study/Joint-USPTO-USCO-Report-on-NFTs-and-Intellectual-Property.pdf>)** For this message, the available evidence does not establish what YieldNodes means by the wording or what a specific token conveys.
