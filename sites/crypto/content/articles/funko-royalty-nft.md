To understand a specific Funko Royalty NFT, separate its token record from the associated collectible and check the applicable project terms.

## What is a Funko Royalty NFT?

An NFT, or non-fungible token, is a distinct token recorded under a smart contract. In Ethereum’s high-level NFT model, a contract associates a unique token identifier with an address to record token ownership. That record describes the token relationship under the contract’s rules; it does not, by itself, explain every right or benefit connected to the token. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>))

The words “Funko Royalty NFT” point to a specific branded subject, but the available evidence here does not include authoritative Funko or project documentation defining its features. The name alone cannot establish whether a token represents a digital collectible, access, a license, or another benefit—or whether “Royalty” describes a feature at all. Those are product-specific questions, not conclusions that can be drawn from general NFT mechanics.

A useful starting point is therefore modest: identify the token and the official materials that describe it. Until those materials establish the connection, do not treat the token as proof of a particular digital or physical collectible or as evidence of a particular right.

## Separate the token, collectible, and rights

Three related but distinct questions help prevent confusion:

- **What does the blockchain record?** It can record an address as holding a token under the applicable contract’s rules.
- **What collectible or content is associated with it?** That connection must be described by the collection’s own records or project materials.
- **What rights does the holder have?** Those depend on applicable legal terms, such as a license or other governing agreement, rather than token possession alone.

The distinction matters because the token and the thing it references are not automatically the same asset or right. In the United States, a joint U.S. Copyright Office and USPTO report explains that transferring an NFT does not necessarily transfer copyright rights in the underlying work or the associated physical or digital asset. Rights depend on applicable law and valid contracts, licenses, or other governing terms. This is a general distinction, not a determination about a specific Funko token. ([source](<https://www.copyright.gov/policy/nft-study/Joint-USPTO-USCO-Report-on-NFTs-and-Intellectual-Property.pdf>))

NFT technology may support intellectual-property management, transfer, or licensing workflows when valid terms and authorized rights holders are present. That potential use does not grant every NFT holder rights in the referenced work. For a particular collectible, the terms need to say what permission, if any, is granted and who is authorized to grant it. ([source](<https://www.copyright.gov/policy/nft-study/Joint-USPTO-USCO-Report-on-NFTs-and-Intellectual-Property.pdf>))

The word “royalty” can also invite an unsupported inference. One technical example, ERC-2981, standardizes retrieval of royalty recipient and amount information for a stated sale price. But the standard only describes royalty information: payment remains voluntary and requires implementation by a marketplace or participant. This illustrates why a label or technical signal should not be mistaken for a guaranteed payment. It is not evidence that a Funko Royalty NFT uses ERC-2981, promises royalties to its holder, or has any particular financial feature. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2981>))

## How the blockchain record relates to ownership

A blockchain record can support a narrow statement about the token: a particular address is recorded as its holder under the contract’s rules. An address is not necessarily a named person or organization. For Ethereum externally owned accounts, the corresponding private key controls the address; a valid signature supports attribution of key control for a transaction, but does not by itself identify the human, organization, device, or service controlling that key.

This means that “the address holds the token” and “a particular person owns a specific collectible or has a particular license” are not interchangeable claims. The first concerns the token record. The second requires additional evidence about identity, the referenced item, and the applicable rights. An on-chain transfer can show a token’s movement under the contract’s rules; it does not settle the scope of a separate license or prove that an off-chain item changed hands.

If a project claims that a token is linked to a physical collectible, that link also needs an identifiable process. ERC-4519 is one proposed technical interface for linking NFTs to physical assets or IoT devices through asset addresses and owner or user authentication states. Its security depends on the physical device, key protection, manufacturer or issuer, firmware, and the off-chain-to-on-chain binding process. This is an example of a technical approach, not evidence that Funko uses it or that any particular collectible has been authenticated this way. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4519>))

## What to verify in official project terms

To assess what a particular Funko Royalty NFT represents, look for official materials that answer these questions:

1. **Who issued the token, and which token is described?** Confirm the issuing entity and the token or collection identification using official project materials. A name or image alone does not establish the precise token or its associated terms.
2. **What content or collectible is linked to it?** Look for an explicit description of the digital content or any physical item and the stated relationship between that item and the token. Do not assume that a reference means ownership or possession of the referenced object.
3. **What rights are expressly granted?** Read the operative language about content use, licensing, access, or other benefits. Distinguish an explicit grant from a promotional description, a token name, or an assumption based on the word “Royalty.”
4. **What happens when the token is transferred?** Check whether the terms say any rights or benefits follow the token, and whether they impose conditions or limits. The token’s ability to transfer does not itself answer what happens to rights described in a separate agreement.
5. **Do the terms apply to this token and remain current?** Verify that the documentation covers the specific token and check whether the project identifies a current version. Without applicable terms, the evidence here cannot determine which rights, if any, attach to a particular holder.

These checks keep two kinds of evidence in their proper roles: blockchain or collection records can help identify the token and its recorded holder, while the applicable project terms are needed to establish any stated content use, license, or benefit. One should not be treated as a substitute for the other.
