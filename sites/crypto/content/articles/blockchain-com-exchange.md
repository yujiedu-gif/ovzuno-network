## How an exchange differs from a blockchain network

A blockchain network maintains records according to its own protocol. An exchange service, by contrast, provides an interface or arrangement through which users may access crypto-related functions. A service may interact with a blockchain network, but the service and the network remain distinct concepts.

One useful distinction is where activity is recorded. In the centralized-exchange model, the venue commonly records customer orders and executed trades in an internal order book and account system. The model described by the Bank for International Settlements also typically involves transferring assets into the exchange’s custody before using its internal trading system. These are general descriptions, not claims about the exact design of a named platform. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publ/work1227.pdf>)

A decentralized exchange can work differently. In a public permissionless decentralized-exchange model, users can retain control of assets while trades execute or settle through smart contracts on a blockchain. However, decentralized exchange designs differ and may still depend on interfaces, relayers, administrators, or off-chain order components. This example shows why “exchange” does not specify a single technical arrangement. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publ/work1227.pdf>)

Accordingly, the word “exchange” does not tell a reader whether a particular service records activity internally, submits transactions to a network, or combines multiple arrangements. Those details depend on the service and its documented design. Nor should the general mechanics of one exchange model be assumed to apply to every provider.
