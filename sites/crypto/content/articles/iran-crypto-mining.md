## What Iran crypto mining refers to

The term describes mining activity conducted in Iran; it does not, by itself, determine whether any operator or facility is authorized. Nor does evidence that equipment is operating establish that its activity complies with applicable requirements. A technical account of mining and a legal assessment of an operation answer different questions.

A cryptocurrency system can be described at a high level through three components: protocol rules, a transaction ledger, and participants who update, store, and read that ledger. This model helps locate mining within a broader system, but does not establish how Iranian rules treat a specific mining site or its associated businesses. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2018/cryptocurrencies-looking-beyond-hype>)

This article addresses jurisdiction-specific operational questions, not how proof-of-work works, how to select hardware or a pool, or whether an operation may be profitable. The available allowed evidence does not answer the Iran-specific legal and cost questions. That limitation matters: missing evidence should be recorded as unresolved, not replaced with assumptions based on another country or a reported example.

## How a mining operation is organized

A facility assessment starts by identifying the parties and dependencies involved. Depending on the arrangement, those may include the equipment owner or operator, site owner, electricity supplier, connectivity provider, and any pool or hosting service. The available evidence does not establish which of these services are available, permitted, or reliably accessible in Iran. Listing them is a way to map what must be checked, not a claim that a given arrangement exists there.

For each component, record who controls it and what evidence supports the arrangement. For example, a site’s electricity contract may identify a supplier and customer, while a hosting agreement may describe the services a provider claims to offer. Those documents should be checked for scope and date. They do not, without further evidence, prove that the mining activity is authorized or that the service will remain available.

Permission to participate in a network is also distinct from permission to operate a business or facility in a jurisdiction. Blockchain networks can use permissionless participation, permissioned participation, or arrangements that combine controls; the permission model describes network access, not Iranian regulatory approval. [source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8202/final>)

Keep the record specific to the site and arrangement. A statement about one operator, locality, service, or period should not be generalized to all mining activity in Iran. If a key party or contractual relationship is unknown, mark it as an information gap rather than assuming the facility is self-operated or independently supplied.

## Verify current licensing and regulatory requirements

The allowed evidence does not establish Iran’s current mining-license or registration requirements, the authorities responsible for them, or the activities currently permitted. It therefore supports neither a conclusion that a particular operation is authorized nor a categorical conclusion that mining is prohibited.

For a decision involving an actual facility, seek current Iranian primary or otherwise authoritative sources that identify the activity covered, the authority issuing the requirement, the publication or effective date, and the relevant geographic and operator scope. Check whether a source addresses facility operation itself or a distinct part of an arrangement, such as equipment ownership, electricity use, hosting, or pool participation. Avoid treating a rule about one activity as a rule about all of them.

Separate formal requirements from descriptions of practice. A report that an operation has run, or that enforcement occurred, is not necessarily the text of a current rule. For each source, note whether it is a regulation, official guidance, a public notice, or a report about events. If the source does not identify its effective date or does not cover the activity in question, its limits should be stated plainly.

If authoritative sources conflict, record the conflict and do not infer a definitive answer from whichever account is easiest to find. The evidence available here cannot resolve such a conflict or determine any individual operator’s status. This discussion is an evidence-checking framework, not legal advice.

## Use a dated verification checklist

Maintain a separate entry for each material question rather than treating “legal” or “operational” as a single status. A useful record includes:

- **Question:** licensing, registration, regulatory oversight, electricity access, tariff, restrictions, taxes, reporting, fees, service availability, security, recovery, or compliance.
- **Source:** issuing authority, provider, or other origin, with a link or document reference where available.
- **Date and scope:** publication or effective date, date checked, location, activity, and operator type addressed.
- **Evidence status:** confirmed within the source’s stated scope, conflicting, outdated, or unresolved.
- **Next step:** the current authoritative source or qualified professional needed to clarify the issue.

Keep each conclusion no broader than its evidence. A source about one operator or activity does not establish a countrywide rule; a provider’s statement does not, by itself, establish regulatory permission; and a published power condition does not prove availability at a specific site. Recheck material claims before relying on them, particularly when the source is dated or its scope is unclear.

If evidence is missing or conflicting, preserve that uncertainty in the record. This checklist can help readers distinguish known facts from questions requiring further verification, but it cannot establish current Iranian requirements where the available evidence does not do so. For decisions about a specific operation, use current authoritative material and qualified advice suited to the parties and jurisdiction involved.
