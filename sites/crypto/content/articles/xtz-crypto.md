## What XTZ means in the Tezos network

XTZ refers to the asset associated with Tezos. It is useful to keep three things separate: the asset, the network protocol, and the people or services involved in operating infrastructure. A token name does not describe who runs a baker, what a service offers, or which operational terms apply.

## xtz crypto and validator participation

A validator is a general term for a participant that carries out protocol duties. In Tezos documentation, the relevant participation language refers to delegates and baking rights. The documented description says that consensus participation rights are selected from delegates according to protocol-defined baking power and pseudorandom selection. “Baking” is therefore a Tezos-specific term for a role in consensus participation, not another name for the XTZ asset.

This description identifies a relationship between delegates and participation rights, but it does not specify every current requirement for becoming a baker or receiving those rights. It should not be read as a universal eligibility rule or as a guide to the current protocol configuration. The Tezos documentation cited for this point is an alpha document and notes that it can change before activation; readers should verify the active protocol version before relying on operational specifics. More generally, proof of stake refers to a consensus model in which cryptocurrency is committed as stake and influences a participant’s consensus weight. That general definition helps explain why an asset can matter to participation, but it does not establish Tezos-specific amounts, eligibility conditions, delegation mechanics, or reward terms. Those rules are specific to the protocol and, where relevant, to a service arrangement. ## Bakers, delegators, and their different roles

A baker is the infrastructure participant associated with baking rights; a delegator is someone relating their participation to a baker through delegation. These labels describe different roles. Holding or delegating an asset should not be confused with operating the infrastructure that performs protocol duties.

The available Tezos evidence supports the connection between delegates and baking-right selection, but it does not fully document how a particular delegation arrangement works. In particular, it does not establish whether a given service holds assets, what authority a participant retains, how delegation can be changed, or how recovery works. Avoid assuming that delegation either transfers custody or leaves custody unchanged without checking the relevant protocol documentation and any service terms.

This distinction matters when reading descriptions of XTZ participation. A protocol’s consensus rules describe how the network assigns or recognizes participation rights. A wallet or service may separately present a process for setting up delegation. The protocol-level description alone does not verify the steps, responsibilities, or custody model of that wallet or service.
