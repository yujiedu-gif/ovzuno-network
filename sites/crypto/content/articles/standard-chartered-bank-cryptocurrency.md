## What institutional treasury teams need to verify

Once a specific activity is identified, request current written documentation from the named provider or bank entity. Confirm that it applies to the organization’s own use case rather than inferring availability from a general reference to cryptocurrency.

Start with eligibility: which customer types and legal entities may participate, and are there geographic or onboarding conditions? Then establish the actual service scope. A document describing one function does not prove access to another. If the service concerns an asset, ask which asset and representation are covered rather than assuming that every asset described by a shared category or symbol is supported.

Check the operational terms that matter to the proposed use. Ask what transaction functions are available, which parties perform them, what operational dependencies or exceptions are described, and whether limits, cutoffs, or processing conditions apply. If a fee or limit is not documented, request confirmation instead of assuming it. Likewise, do not infer a yield or return feature from asset support or a bank-related announcement; the supplied evidence does not establish any such Standard Chartered feature or its terms.

Finally, confirm status directly. Ask whether the service is available to the organization now, or whether the reference is historical, prospective, or limited to a project or partnership. Keep the dated source and written response with the team’s assessment, noting the entity, customer type, and jurisdiction to which each applies. This is a verification process, not a claim that Standard Chartered offers any particular function.
