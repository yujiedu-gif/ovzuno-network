For a business assessing an Indian crypto exchange, treat it as part of a wider market-infrastructure arrangement: identify the trading and asset-handling functions it performs, map dependencies on other providers, and verify India-specific legal, regulatory, tax, and availability claims using current authoritative evidence. The evidence available for this article does not establish the current status of any particular Indian exchange or the requirements that apply to it, so those conclusions must remain open until independently verified.

## What an Indian crypto exchange does in the market

An exchange can provide a venue where customers trade digital assets. In a centralized model, a venue may offer near-real-time trading, liquid order books, and advanced order functionality after assets have been deposited. These are possible functions, not guarantees about every operator, asset, or market condition. The available evidence does not establish which services a particular exchange serving Indian markets provides. ([source](<https://www.bis.org/publ/work1227.pdf>))

A business review should distinguish the exchange’s role from the broader arrangement around it. Ask which entity operates the trading venue, which entity holds or controls assets, and whether other providers handle payment or liquidity functions. Functions such as trading, custody, and other cryptoasset activities may be combined or separated across different firms and services; organization varies. That general structural distinction does not establish how a specific operator in India divides responsibilities.

Think of the arrangement as a functional map, not a universal technical design. Participants may connect to trading systems through access interfaces, while custody, settlement, data, and external-service relationships vary by arrangement. This map helps frame questions, but it does not reveal a specific operator’s architecture without evidence.

## Core systems behind exchange operations

Examine the service as distinct functions rather than as a single label. For trading, establish what the operator itself provides and what depends on external parties. For asset handling, seek documentation explaining custody arrangements and how assets move into, through, or out of the service. For fiat interfaces, identify the relevant payment provider and the boundary between its role and the exchange’s role. These are due-diligence questions; the allowed evidence does not confirm the architecture of any named operator.

Execution models can differ. In public-blockchain automated-market-maker trading, an individual swap can be affected by public transaction visibility, execution ordering, pool depth, and price impact before block inclusion. This describes one type of onchain execution environment, not every exchange’s internal trading process. If a service routes activity to external venues or pools, ask what documentation shows that routing occurs and which organization is responsible for each step. ([source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/trading-defi-era-automated-market-maker>))

An interface is another function to inspect. For a decentralized-exchange interface, relevant information includes token identity, allowances, quoted outputs, slippage, price impact, network fees, and transaction state. This is guidance for that interface context; it does not prove the safety of underlying contracts or assets, and it should not be read as a description of every centralized exchange interface. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/design-and-ux/dex-design-best-practice/>))

Keep claims about availability, supported assets, geographic access, fees, and service levels tied to current operator-specific documentation. ## How to assess exchange infrastructure

Begin by mapping the service boundary. Record the named operator and each provider that appears to perform custody, payment processing, or liquidity-related work. For each function, ask who is responsible, what contractual or technical evidence supports that description, and what happens if a provider or connection is unavailable. Do not treat a provider relationship as proof that the exchange itself operates that function.

Next, request verifiable material on security controls, incident handling, recovery processes, and operational continuity. The evidence available for this article does not establish that any particular exchange has specific controls, has tested them, or can recover within a given period. A marketing statement is not a substitute for documentation identifying the control, its owner, its scope, and any relevant limitation.

Then distinguish current capabilities from plans and third-party services. Ask whether public descriptions identify what is live, which components another organization supplies, and what evidence substantiates each claim. Where evidence is absent, record the point as unresolved rather than treating it as an established capability.

A useful evidence record links each assertion to a document and its scope. For example, a statement about asset handling should identify the relevant entity and arrangement; a statement about service availability should identify the operator’s current terms. The record should also show which questions remain unanswered. This is a review method, not evidence that a particular provider meets a standard.

## Questions to resolve before relying on an exchange

Use a written review to close evidence gaps:

- **Identity and scope:** Which entity operates the service, what functions does it claim to perform, and which markets or assets are currently supported? Obtain current documentation rather than relying on generic descriptions.
- **Responsibilities:** Who handles trading, custody, asset movement, payment processing, and any external liquidity function? Identify third parties and the boundaries between their roles and the operator’s.
- **Security and continuity:** What verifiable evidence describes security controls, incident response, recovery processes, and operational continuity? Record what remains undocumented.
- **Terms and availability:** Where are current service terms, fees, asset support, and geographic availability documented? Treat each as time-sensitive and operator-specific.
- **India-specific claims:** What current authoritative evidence supports claims about applicable rules, registration, reporting, or compliance? Keep tax and legal conclusions out of the infrastructure assessment unless separately verified using qualified, current sources.

The result should be a map of functions, providers, evidence, and unresolved questions—not a ranking or an assumption of suitability. If a claim lacks current, verifiable support, label it unconfirmed.
