In cryptocurrency, **NFT** means **non-fungible token**: a distinct token represented through a blockchain system. A blockchain record concerns the token and its recorded activity; it does not, by itself, establish that a related file or work belongs to the token holder or that the holder has particular rights in it.

## What does NFT mean in cryptocurrency?

“Non-fungible” means distinct rather than interchangeable on a one-to-one basis. Units of a fungible token are generally treated as equivalent to one another; an NFT, by contrast, has a distinct identity within the system that records it. The term describes the token’s identity, not the value or significance of anything associated with it.

A blockchain is a shared record maintained according to the rules of its network. Some blockchain systems also support smart contracts: programs whose code and persistent state are kept at an on-chain address. In those systems, contract rules can maintain token-related records. This is a general explanation, not a claim that every blockchain or NFT uses the same design.

The key distinction is between the token and what someone says it represents. An NFT is the distinct token record; a digital item or rights claim connected to that token is a separate matter to examine.

## What the NFT and its blockchain record identify

A blockchain record can show information about a token as represented under that system’s rules. In systems that use smart contracts, contract state and functions can be associated with an on-chain address. The record’s meaning therefore depends on the particular system and the information that system records; “NFT” alone does not specify every implementation detail.

A related file or description may be separate from the token record. For example, a token may be discussed alongside an image, document, or other digital material. The existence of a token does not mean the file itself is contained in the token, nor does the term alone tell a reader where that file is stored or how it can be accessed. Those details require information about the specific implementation and its associated documentation.

It is useful to keep three questions distinct: What token is being identified? What information does the blockchain record about that token? What separate item or claim is linked to it? An answer to the first or second question does not automatically answer the third.

## A token is not automatically the file or the rights

A digital file and a token associated with it are not the same thing. The token may be connected to a file or description, but the connection does not make the file identical to the token. Nor does the token’s existence, by itself, establish that a linked file is authentic, that a particular person created it, or that the file will remain accessible.

The same distinction applies to rights. A blockchain token record does not by itself establish copyright ownership, a license, or another legal right in a linked work or item. Specific rights or asset connections require supporting implementation details and applicable documentation. A rights-management or license reference may be associated with a token, but a technical label or standard does not, on its own, determine whether rights are legally enforceable or define their scope.

This distinction is important when the record identifies a token holder. The recorded holder and the owner of a related work, file, or real-world item are different claims. To understand any claimed permission or right, a reader would need to consider the relevant documentation and applicable terms separately. This general definition does not decide the legal status of a particular token or arrangement.

## How NFTs differ from interchangeable crypto tokens

Fungibility concerns whether units are interchangeable; it is separate from whether a token is useful, valuable, authentic, or legally meaningful. A fungible token is designed around equivalent units. An NFT is distinguished by its individual token identity. That conceptual contrast explains the name, but it does not establish what the token represents.

Different token systems can also organize token types and quantities differently. As a result, “NFT” should not be read as a promise that every token system has identical behavior or that every token necessarily corresponds to one unique digital item. The term describes a category at a high level; details depend on the particular system and its implementation.

Distinct identity alone does not prove authenticity or exclusive rights. It tells a reader that the token is treated as distinct in its system, not that every statement associated with it is correct.

## What the definition does not tell you

The definition of NFT does not establish who created a related item, whether that item is genuine, what use is permitted, or what legal rights apply. It also does not establish market value, security, or other properties of a particular token. Those are separate questions that require evidence appropriate to the claim.

For a careful reading, separate the token record from any linked file and from any claimed rights. Ask what the blockchain record actually says, what documentation explains the connection to an item, and what terms support a claimed permission or right. Do not treat the NFT label as a substitute for those details.

In short, an NFT is a distinct token represented through a blockchain system. Its record concerns the token and its activity under that system’s rules. The record alone does not make a related file the token, verify claims about that file, or establish copyright, a license, or other rights.
