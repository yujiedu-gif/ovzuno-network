An NFT, or non-fungible token, is a blockchain-based token distinguished from other tokens by its identity rather than treated as an interchangeable unit. Holding one establishes control of a token record under the relevant system; it does not, by itself, establish rights to associated media, prove authenticity, or guarantee access to that media.

## What are NFT tokens?

A token is a digital record implemented through a system’s rules. “Non-fungible” means that a token is distinguished from another rather than designed to be interchangeable one-for-one as an identical unit. The term describes how tokens are identified, not what they are worth or what purpose they serve.

An NFT is therefore not necessarily the image, audio, document, or other item people associate with it. The token record and related content are separate things. This distinction matters because a record can identify a token without establishing all the claims someone makes about the content connected to it.

The label “NFT” also does not tell you everything about the token’s project or implementation. A token label or interface does not specify the complete project-specific rights, service conditions, current availability, or legal treatment associated with that token. Those questions require project documentation and terms, and legal status is not determined by the label alone.

## How an NFT’s blockchain record works

A token record is maintained within a particular technical system. For a record to be meaningful, the reader needs to know which system it belongs to and how that system identifies the token. Names, symbols, or a displayed image alone are not enough to establish every fact about a token or its associated content.

Some NFT implementations refer to metadata or other content separately from the token record. In that arrangement, the token and the referenced material are related but not identical: the record represents the token, while a separate source may supply descriptive information or media. The exact arrangement depends on the implementation, so it should not be assumed that every NFT stores or retrieves content in the same way.

That separation creates an important limit. The existence of a token record does not, by itself, show that referenced material is available, accurate, or permanent. Nor does it establish that a service will continue to provide access. To interpret a particular token, identify its technical context and consult the project’s documentation about what the record refers to and where related material is maintained. Do not infer details that the record or documentation does not establish.

## What holding an NFT does—and does not—establish

Holding an NFT means having control of the token within the system that records it. That is narrower than owning every item or right associated with it. A token record alone does not establish copyright, a license to use associated media, title to a physical object, or any other particular right. The relevant terms and applicable law matter; legal outcomes should not be generalized across jurisdictions.

Likewise, possession of a token does not by itself prove that the person who created or issued it had authority over the associated work, that the work is authentic, or that a holder has access to a related service. Those matters require evidence beyond the token record. A record can document token activity without verifying every off-chain statement made about the token or its content.

Keep the distinction clear when reading descriptions of “ownership”: the token record concerns the token, while media, rights, identity, and access may depend on separate systems or terms. The evidence available here supports that distinction, but does not establish the rights or access conditions for any particular token.

## How NFTs differ from other crypto tokens

The key difference is interchangeability. Fungible tokens are designed so units can be treated as equivalent units of the same token. NFTs are distinguished from one another instead. This describes a difference in how the tokens are identified; it does not mean that all NFTs have the same function or that a particular NFT carries a specific right or feature.

A token standard can define common operations, but it does not necessarily determine a project’s complete rights, services, availability, or legal treatment. Those details depend on the implementation and related arrangements. The comparison is therefore about the token’s distinct identity, not a guarantee about its content or significance.

## What to verify before interpreting an NFT record

Start by identifying the technical system and the specific token record under discussion. Then check the project documentation and terms for what the token is said to represent, whether related media or metadata is handled separately, and what rights or services are actually described. If the available documentation does not answer a question, the record alone should not be treated as an answer.

Keep claims about authenticity, creator identity, copyright, licensing, access, availability, security, recovery, or legal treatment separate from the fact that a token record exists. Each requires evidence appropriate to that claim. This approach makes it possible to describe what holding an NFT establishes—control of a distinct token record—without treating that record as proof of every associated claim.
