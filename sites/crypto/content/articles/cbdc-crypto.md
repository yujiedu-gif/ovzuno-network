CBDC means “central bank digital currency”: a digital liability of a central bank that is widely available to the public. In crypto discussions, the term identifies the issuer and the kind of money involved; it does not, by itself, specify a blockchain, payment method, or particular design. [claim_fed_cbdc_liability]

## What does CBDC mean in crypto?

A central bank digital currency, or CBDC, is a digital form of central-bank money. The Federal Reserve’s general definition describes a CBDC as a digital liability of a central bank that is widely available to the public. “Liability” here identifies the central bank as the entity responsible for the money represented; it does not describe a private company’s token or the technical platform used to transfer it. [claim_fed_cbdc_liability]

The word “crypto” often appears alongside CBDC because both subjects involve digital money and may involve cryptographic technology. But being digital or discussed in the crypto sector does not make an instrument a cryptocurrency. The relevant distinction begins with what the instrument represents and who issues it.

The definition above is a general one, not a complete blueprint for every design. It also should not be read as a statement about whether a particular country has issued, approved, or plans to issue a CBDC. Those are separate questions that require current information from the relevant public authority.

## How a CBDC differs from cryptocurrency

A cryptocurrency, in a technical definition derived from NIST, is a digital asset, credit, or unit that can be cryptographically transferred between users of a blockchain network. That definition describes a network-based digital asset; it does not say that every cryptocurrency has the same issuer, governance, or issuance policy. [claim_nist_crypto_definition]

A CBDC is distinguished by its relationship to a central bank: it is defined as that central bank’s digital liability and, in the Federal Reserve’s general description, is widely available to the public. Cryptocurrency, by contrast, is a broader technical category of transferable digital assets. Its governance and issuance arrangements may differ from one system to another. [claim_fed_cbdc_liability] [claim_nist_crypto_definition]

This is not a simple contrast between “centralized” and “decentralized.” The definitions do not establish that every cryptocurrency is decentralized, nor that every CBDC uses a centrally operated technical architecture. They identify different attributes: the CBDC definition centers on a central-bank liability, while the cryptocurrency definition centers on cryptographic transfer within a blockchain network. Avoid inferring governance, access, or control from either label alone.

Nor does the word “CBDC” establish that the system uses a blockchain. A central-bank liability is a monetary and issuer distinction; blockchain is a possible technology choice. Similarly, the term “cryptocurrency” does not establish that the asset is issued by a central bank.

## CBDC, digital payments, and the role of ledgers

A digital payment is a way to transfer money, while a CBDC is a category of money. A payment system can move existing forms of money without creating a new central-bank liability. Therefore, seeing a digital transfer or a payment application does not establish that the money being transferred is a CBDC.

A ledger is a record of transactions or balances. Blockchain and distributed-ledger systems are possible ways to maintain records, but the CBDC definition does not specify a ledger design. The same caution applies to other technical features: the label alone does not tell you how transactions are authorized, recorded, or settled.

Stablecoins are another category that may appear in the same discussion. A typical fiat-reserve-backed stablecoin uses offchain reserve assets and an issuer’s minting and redemption process: minting follows receipt of collateral, while tokens are burned after redemption. The exact reserves, rights, eligibility, fees, and timing depend on the arrangement. [claim_fiat_issue_redeem]

That makes the high-level distinction useful but not exhaustive. A CBDC is central-bank money under the stated definition; a reserve-backed stablecoin is a privately arranged token model with an issuer and reserve assets. The evidence for the stablecoin model does not establish the details of any particular token, nor should every instrument called a stablecoin be assumed to have identical backing or redemption terms. [claim_fed_cbdc_liability] [claim_fiat_issue_redeem]

## What the term does not tell you

The definition alone does not tell you whether a CBDC is designed for retail use by the public or for wholesale use among institutions. It also does not establish whether a particular system uses accounts, tokens, a distributed ledger, or another technical structure. Those are implementation choices, not conclusions that follow from the word CBDC.

The label also does not determine privacy features, programmability, settlement arrangements, legal status, or availability. The Federal Reserve’s general definition identifies a digital central-bank liability widely available to the public, but that definition is not evidence that a specific CBDC exists or is available to use. [claim_fed_cbdc_liability]

When evaluating a named project, consult current primary materials from the relevant central bank or public authority for its definition, design, and status. Keep those project-specific facts separate from the general meaning of CBDC. The term is a starting point for identifying the issuer and monetary relationship—not a complete description of a payment product or its operating rules.
