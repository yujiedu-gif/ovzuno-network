The phrase “Marqeta crypto” is ambiguous. It may refer to questions about using card infrastructure in a crypto-related program, but that phrase alone does not establish that Marqeta provides cryptocurrency custody, a wallet, direct crypto acceptance, or blockchain settlement. A business should verify the specific product and agreements before treating a card-related capability as crypto-payment infrastructure.

## What people may mean by “marqeta crypto”

The phrase could be asking whether a card program can interact with a crypto account or service, or whether a company can use card infrastructure as part of a broader payments flow. Those are questions about a possible connection between card payments and crypto; they do not identify a particular product, transaction path, or provider responsibility.

A crypto-linked card transaction and a direct cryptocurrency payment are different descriptions. In a card transaction, the merchant-facing payment process may be associated with a crypto account, but that does not establish that the merchant receives a digital asset. Direct crypto acceptance would require evidence that the merchant accepts a digital asset through a crypto payment path. Likewise, a reference to crypto does not establish that settlement occurs on a blockchain.

Do not infer one from the search phrase, a general description, or another party’s label. Current official product materials and applicable agreements would need to identify the service, the participants, and the supported use case.

## How card infrastructure differs from crypto payment rails

Card issuing and processing refer to functions in card-payment flows. Crypto-related functions are separate questions: Who controls crypto assets? Is there a wallet? Does someone authorize a blockchain transaction with a digital signature? Does a blockchain record the settlement? Does the merchant accept a digital asset, or receive another form of payment?

These distinctions matter because a label such as “crypto card” does not answer them. For example, signing a cryptocurrency transaction is a distinct authorization mechanism: digital signatures authorize transfers, but signatures alone do not establish who operates a service, what an agreement promises, or whether a merchant receives crypto. A claim about card infrastructure therefore cannot stand in for evidence about wallet custody or on-chain payment functions.

A blockchain-based route can work differently from card processing. In one specific example, an automated-market-maker decentralized exchange uses smart contracts and liquidity pools, with transactions settled when included in a blockchain block. That example illustrates a blockchain transaction path; it is not evidence that Marqeta or a particular card program uses one.

The practical test is to follow the payment to the merchant. If a card transaction is connected to a crypto account, ask what asset the merchant actually receives and which entity settles with the merchant. Without documentation of that path, do not describe the transaction as direct crypto acceptance or blockchain settlement.

## Where a crypto-linked card flow may involve separate providers

A conceptual flow could involve several different parties: a card platform, an issuer or program manager, a card network, a crypto exchange or wallet provider, and a merchant or acquirer. This is a list of possible roles to investigate—not a confirmed Marqeta arrangement. The actual participants and their duties must come from documentation for the specific program.

Trace each function separately rather than treating “the provider” as a single actor:

- **Funds and custody:** Identify which entity holds or controls the customer’s funds or crypto assets. Do not assume that the card platform is the custodian.
- **Conversion:** If crypto is converted to fiat, find evidence naming the entity that performs the conversion and identify the asset delivered to the next party. A conversion step is not the same thing as card authorization or merchant settlement.
- **Authorization:** Determine which party makes the authorization decision and what the relevant product materials say about that role. Do not infer the answer from a crypto connection alone.
- **Merchant settlement:** Confirm which entity settles with the merchant and in what asset or form. A card transaction associated with crypto does not establish that settlement is on-chain.
- **Wallet and blockchain actions:** If a wallet, transaction signature, or blockchain submission is claimed, look for explicit documentation identifying the responsible party and the function performed.

Responsibilities depend on the product design and the relevant contracts. A diagram can organize questions, but it cannot verify that a provider performs a role or that a particular integration exists. Keep the conceptual flow separate from confirmed product facts.

## What to verify in Marqeta documentation and agreements

Begin with current official product documentation and agreements for the intended program. Look for explicit descriptions of supported use cases, named counterparties, geographic scope, and limitations. Record what each document actually confirms, rather than treating a general card capability or a third party’s description as proof of crypto functionality.

Next, map the flow from the customer’s funds to the merchant. For every step, write down the responsible legal entity, the asset involved, and what event triggers the next step. Ask who controls the funds, who provides any wallet, and who signs or submits any blockchain transaction. If conversion is involved, identify the provider and distinguish conversion from card authorization and settlement.

Review the applicable terms for availability, fees, compliance responsibilities, security controls, and operational limitations. Do not assume these details: they require current product documentation or provider terms, and this article does not establish Marqeta-specific terms for them. Product details can change, so verify them with the relevant provider and in the applicable agreements before relying on them.

For any claim of direct crypto acceptance, wallet functionality, custody, or on-chain settlement, require explicit supporting documentation. Ask whether the claim describes a Marqeta capability, a separate provider’s service, or a combination of roles. If the materials do not answer a question, mark it as unverified rather than filling in the gap with assumptions.

## A practical way to describe the capability accurately

Use wording no broader than the evidence supports. If materials confirm only a role in a card program, describe that role as card infrastructure used in a crypto-related program. Name other providers and functions only when documentation supports those details. In an internal assessment, separate confirmed capabilities, third-party functions, and unanswered questions.

Before making an operational decision or describing the flow externally, verify the details with the relevant provider and review the current agreements. The central distinction is straightforward: a card transaction connected to crypto is not, by itself, evidence of merchant-facing crypto acceptance, custody, wallet functionality, or blockchain settlement.
