## What “staking” means in a crypto network

In a proof-of-stake system, staking means committing cryptocurrency as part of the network’s consensus process. The stake influences consensus participation or weight, although selection, custody, withdrawal, reward, and penalty rules differ by protocol. The term alone therefore does not establish how a particular network works or what a participant must do.

For example, Solana’s documented delegation model lets a delegator assign stake to a validator, increasing that validator’s consensus weight. The delegator retains token control under the stake-account model. This illustrates one network-specific arrangement; it is not evidence about Helium or a universal rule for staking.

Staking is distinct from other ways a token might be used. A network could require tokens for a function unrelated to consensus, or participation could involve providing a service. Those mechanisms should not be called proof-of-stake security unless the protocol documentation says the assets affect consensus participation or weight. Likewise, rewards associated with network activity are not automatically staking yields: the mechanism and eligibility rules matter.
