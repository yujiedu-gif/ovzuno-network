## Build a decision record without assuming suitability

A useful decision record separates what current documents establish from claims that remain unverified. It should be specific enough for another reviewer to understand the scope and reproduce the checks.

1. **Define the assessment:** Record the business use, organization or user type, relevant jurisdiction, and functions under review. Avoid treating a broad brand-level inquiry as though it answered questions about a specific legal entity or service.
2. **Log the evidence:** For each material statement, record the source, the date reviewed, any publication or effective date shown, and the exact point the document supports. Note whether the evidence is official, jurisdiction-specific, and applicable to the intended account or use.
3. **Separate facts from open questions:** Mark each item as documented, not documented, or requiring confirmation. Keep unresolved availability, integration conditions, operational dependencies, security controls, custody, recovery, regulatory status, and commercial terms visible rather than converting them into assumptions.
4. **Assign review responsibility:** Route technical questions to the relevant technical reviewers and security, legal, or compliance questions to the appropriate internal reviewers. The record should identify who owns each unresolved item and what evidence is needed to close it.
5. **Recheck time-sensitive details:** Verify the current terms and availability again before relying on them. An earlier document or a general description does not prove that a service remains available or that its conditions have not changed.
