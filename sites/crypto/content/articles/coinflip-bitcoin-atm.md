A CoinFlip Bitcoin ATM refers to a bitcoin ATM associated with the CoinFlip operator. More generally, a bitcoin ATM is a physical kiosk through which customers can carry out certain cryptocurrency-related transactions. Using one typically means following the machine’s instructions, supplying any requested information, specifying a transaction, and responding to payment and wallet prompts. The exact process and terms depend on the particular machine and location; this general definition does not establish CoinFlip’s current features or service conditions.

## What a CoinFlip Bitcoin ATM refers to

The phrase combines an operator name with a type of service. “CoinFlip” identifies the operator associated with the ATM, while “bitcoin ATM” describes the kiosk category. Keeping those elements separate helps avoid assuming that all machines bearing a brand name have identical features, transaction options, or requirements.

A bitcoin ATM is a physical interface for carrying out a transaction. A wallet, by contrast, is a tool used to manage keys and blockchain addresses. The machine may ask a customer to provide or scan an address as part of a transaction, but the kiosk and the wallet have different roles. The precise arrangement depends on the transaction flow and the user’s setup.

The words “Bitcoin ATM” also do not, by themselves, establish which transaction directions the machine supports. U.S. financial-crime guidance describes convertible virtual currency kiosks as allowing customers to exchange fiat currency for virtual currency and, at supported two-way kiosks, virtual currency for fiat currency. That description identifies a possible kiosk model; it does not confirm that every machine supports both directions or that a particular CoinFlip machine offers either option at a given location.

## How a bitcoin ATM transaction generally works

A customer generally starts by reading the machine’s instructions and selecting from the transaction options it presents. The process may then ask for transaction details, information about the customer, a payment, and a wallet address or other way to direct the transaction. The exact sequence, checks, and available steps are not universal.

One documented Bitcoin ATM purchase flow in Australia illustrates how a particular operator’s process can work: selecting an asset and amount, accepting disclosures, completing phone and identity checks, scanning a wallet QR code, inserting cash, confirming the transaction, and receiving the crypto. This is an example from one operator and jurisdiction, not a description of CoinFlip’s current process or a rule for every kiosk. Operators and locations can use different checks, screens, limits, supported assets, and delivery methods.

At the wallet end, a Bitcoin transaction can spend a specific prior transaction output, identified by its transaction ID and output index, while creating new outputs that can later be spent. This is part of Bitcoin’s transaction model; it does not establish how a kiosk collects payment, requests customer information, or delivers assets. Those are service-specific details to check in the machine’s instructions and the operator’s current disclosures.

If a machine asks for a wallet address, take care to follow its displayed instructions and verify the address and transaction details before confirming. The address is a destination in the transaction flow, not proof of who controls it. The general steps above explain what the kiosk may ask a customer to do; they do not establish the applicable transaction status, timing, or support process for any particular service.

## How it differs from an exchange or wallet

A bitcoin ATM is a physical kiosk. An exchange is a service through which customers may exchange or trade assets, while a wallet is used to manage access to crypto assets. A transaction can involve more than one of these components, but the terms are not interchangeable. In particular, a machine that prompts for a wallet address is not necessarily itself the wallet, and the transaction flow does not establish that every customer needs the same wallet setup.

The distinction also helps identify who is responsible for which part of the process. The kiosk presents its transaction instructions; wallet software or a wallet provider may manage keys and addresses; and an exchange, if part of a particular arrangement, has a separate role. Do not infer what one component does, or what assistance it provides, from a general description of another.

## What to verify before using a machine

Before starting, look for current information specific to the machine’s location. Confirm whether it is available and which transaction directions, supported assets, payment methods, fees, and limits apply. A broad description of bitcoin ATMs cannot establish those operator- and location-specific terms.

Read the machine’s disclosures and check whether identity verification or other requirements apply where it is located. In the United States, CVC kiosk operators that facilitate transmission between an exchanger and a customer wallet or operate as an exchanger are generally considered money services businesses under federal Bank Secrecy Act treatment, with applicable registration, recordkeeping, reporting, and transaction-monitoring obligations. This is a defined U.S. scope, not a statement of requirements in every jurisdiction or a determination about a particular machine’s legal status. Local rules and the facts of a service arrangement require separate verification.

Before confirming a transaction, review the wallet instructions, destination address, and other details shown by the machine. Check the operator’s current support information and its stated process for handling problems rather than assuming funds can be recovered. The available information here does not establish CoinFlip’s current locations, fees, limits, supported payment methods, transaction options, or recovery procedures; those details require current, location-specific verification.
