## How to check company, product, and operational claims

Use a source-by-source check rather than relying on a single promotional description:

1. **Identify the entity.** Look for a legal name and records that connect it to the “Bytex Crypto” name. A website, application, or social-media account alone may not establish who operates the service.
2. **Identify the product.** Find current first-party documentation that names the product and describes its functions. Keep the product’s own claims distinct from independently confirmed facts.
3. **Attribute each function.** Determine which entity is said to provide trading, custody, market data, or connectivity. A feature offered by a partner should not automatically be attributed to the named service.
4. **Check consistency and currency.** Compare entity, product, and contact details across records. Confirm that the documentation applies to the same service and is current enough for the claim being assessed.
5. **Record gaps and conflicts.** If sources disagree, do not resolve the disagreement by inference. If ownership, a function, or operating status cannot be confirmed, label it unverified.

This process helps prevent an inference error: treating one technical component as proof of a broader business role. A signing tool, an indexed data endpoint, or another isolated capability may be part of a larger service environment, but it does not alone show that its provider is an exchange, broker, custodian, or market operator. Establish each role separately using evidence attributable to the identified entity.

When comparing sources, keep track of what each one can establish. A product page may describe a feature; a company record may identify an organization; and an official regulator record may address a specified status or activity. These records answer different questions. One kind of evidence should not be treated as a substitute for another, and a statement about a company should not automatically be applied to every product associated with it.
