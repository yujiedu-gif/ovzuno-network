An NFT means a **non-fungible token**: a distinct token represented and tracked on a blockchain. “Non-fungible” means it is not interchangeable with another unit on a one-for-one basis as if the two were identical. The token is a blockchain record; it is not necessarily the digital item people associate with it, and the record alone does not establish copyright or ownership of that item.

## What does NFT mean?

A token is a digital record managed under a blockchain system’s rules. “Non-fungible” describes a token treated as individually distinguishable rather than as one interchangeable unit among many. An NFT is therefore a particular token, not simply a label for any image, video, or document that appears online.

On Ethereum, an NFT contract records ownership by associating a unique token identifier with an Ethereum address. The token’s identifier is unique within its contract; it is not necessarily a universal identifier for the associated content or for the same-looking token on every system. This helps explain why the token and the item connected with it should be described separately. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)) ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>))

The word “ownership” can cause confusion here. In this context, the blockchain record indicates which address the contract recognizes as holding the token under its rules. That statement is about the token record. It does not automatically mean that the address holder owns every related file or has every right someone might associate with that file. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>))

## The NFT token is not necessarily the digital item

An NFT may be associated with digital content, but association does not make the token and the content the same thing. A token can have metadata—a set of descriptive information—that includes a URI, or link, referring to information such as a name, description, or image. Depending on the implementation, the metadata and referenced media may be on the blockchain, stored using content-addressed systems, or hosted off-chain in a way that can change. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>))

This distinction has practical consequences. A blockchain record can remain a record of a token while the referenced content is handled separately. The token’s existence does not, by itself, guarantee that a linked file will remain available, stay unchanged, or be licensed in a particular way. Those properties depend on the content arrangement and other relevant systems, not merely on the token label. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>))

The same separation applies to rights. A token record does not automatically create or transfer copyright in a referenced work. An NFT may be used in a rights-management or licensing workflow, but any rights associated with a token must be specified through relevant terms and assessed under applicable law. The token itself is not a substitute for those terms. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>))

A technical standard can describe how information is represented without deciding what a holder may legally do. For example, ERC-2981 provides a way for a contract to return a royalty recipient and amount for a stated sale price. That royalty information is a technical signal; payment is voluntary and depends on marketplace or participant implementation. The example shows why a standard’s defined behavior should not be confused with a guaranteed real-world outcome. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2981>))

## What an NFT record can show

A token’s blockchain record can identify the token under its contract and show token ownership as represented by that contract’s rules. It can also provide a history of recorded token activity. On Ethereum, the token identifier and contract address together distinguish an ERC-721 token on a chain; names, symbols, and media alone are not reliable global identifiers. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>))

What the record does not show automatically is equally important. A token record is not, on its own, proof that a related file is authentic, that the person who created the token had authority over the work, or that the token holder has copyright or other enforceable rights. Those claims concern the associated item and governing terms, not just the token’s place in the blockchain record. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)) ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>))

Metadata adds another boundary. A URI can point to metadata for a token, but the presence of that link should not be treated as a guarantee about the linked material. The way the content is stored or maintained may affect what remains accessible or accurate over time. Checking the token record and understanding how its referenced information is provided are separate verification tasks. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>))

In short, the blockchain record can establish facts about the token as represented in that system. To understand a related digital item, a reader would also need to consider what the metadata refers to and what applicable terms say. The record alone should not be stretched into a broader claim about a file or a legal right. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)) ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)) ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>))

## A simple example of an NFT

Imagine a hypothetical digital collectible associated with an NFT. The token has an identifier in a contract, and the blockchain record associates that token with an address. Separate metadata might describe the collectible or point to an image file. Any permission to reproduce, display, or otherwise use that image would be a further matter described by relevant terms; it does not follow just from the token record. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)) ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)) ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>))

This example has three distinct parts: the token, the media that may be referenced, and any rights or permissions connected with that media. The example explains the terminology only; it is not a claim about a particular product, file, or transaction. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>))

## Questions the definition does not settle

Knowing that something is an NFT does not answer every question a person might have about it. The definition alone does not establish ownership of an associated physical or digital item, copyright, authenticity, continued access to a file, or the scope of any license. The meaning of a specific arrangement depends on its technical implementation and relevant terms, as well as applicable law. This explanation does not determine those questions for any particular token. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)) ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>))

The simplest useful distinction is this: **an NFT is a distinct blockchain token; the media it may refer to and any rights connected with that media are separate matters.** The blockchain record can describe the token and its recorded ownership, but it should not be read as proof of every claim about a related digital item. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)) ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>))
