Representative examples of NFT tokens include digital collectibles, game items, tickets, memberships, and records associated with certificates or claims. Each illustrates a way a distinct token can identify or refer to something; none, by itself, guarantees the associated media, rights, access, or underlying facts.

## What makes an NFT example relevant?

An NFT is a non-fungible token: a token treated as distinct rather than interchangeable with another token. In the ERC-721 standard, a token is uniquely identified on an Ethereum chain by the combination of its smart-contract address and token ID. A familiar name, symbol, or image is not a reliable global identifier. This is why an example should be understood through the specific token record, not just its label or appearance. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)

A token may expose a URI, or uniform resource identifier, that points to metadata for that token. Metadata is descriptive information associated with the token and may refer to media such as an image. The ERC-721 standard does not guarantee that the URI or referenced content will remain available, permanent, accurate, or licensed. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)

The examples below describe possible uses, not endorsements of particular tokens or collections. They also do not establish value, authenticity, ownership of related property, or guaranteed access. Those questions require information beyond the token’s technical identity.

## Example NFT tokens in digital collectibles

A digital collectible NFT can identify a distinct item, such as an image or another digital work, within a collection. Under ERC-721, the contract address and token ID distinguish the token from others. The associated image, however, may be referred to through metadata rather than contained in the token’s identity. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>) [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)

That difference matters when interpreting what a collector holds. The token record can identify a particular token, while the file or other content it refers to may be stored or served separately. A token identifier does not itself make the referenced file permanent or establish that the person holding the token has copyright or other rights in the work. The standard’s metadata interface does not guarantee availability, accuracy, permanence, or licensing. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)

Not every NFT-like example uses a one-token-per-item structure. ERC-1155 allows one contract to manage multiple token types, including fungible, non-fungible, and semi-fungible configurations. A token ID can have a quantity greater than one, so whether a particular token type is non-fungible depends on its implementation and supply. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1155>)

These standards describe technical interfaces and token configurations. They do not say what a collectible means to its creator or holder, whether the associated work is authentic, or what rights accompany it. Those details must be established separately.

## NFTs in games, tickets, and memberships

A game-related NFT might identify an in-game item or asset. For example, a game could associate a token with a particular item in its software. The token record alone does not explain how the game recognizes that token, what the player can do with it, or whether the game’s functionality will continue. Those details depend on the game’s design and terms, and any referenced media may have its own availability limitations.

A ticket NFT might be used as a digital record associated with an admission credential. The token can identify a record, but that does not by itself establish that an issuer will accept it at an event or guarantee entry. The issuer’s systems and conditions determine whether the token is recognized and what access it represents. A reader evaluating this example should distinguish the on-chain record from the admission process that gives the record practical effect.

A membership NFT might function as an identifier for a service, community, or other gated experience. In this example, an application or service would need to recognize the token and apply the relevant access conditions. The token’s existence does not, on its own, guarantee that access will be available, permanent, or unchanged. The specific service and its terms remain important.

These categories are illustrative, not universal functions of NFTs. Two tokens using the same standard may serve different purposes, and a token’s visible image or description does not prove that a service will provide the associated experience. The practical question is what the issuer or application has committed to do and how it recognizes the token.

## Other uses: certificates and recorded claims

An NFT may be associated with a certificate, provenance record, or other claim. In this kind of example, a token can serve as a digital record linked to an asset or statement. Tokenization can create a digital representation of a tangible or intangible asset using distributed ledger technology. That description does not, by itself, establish the accuracy of the representation or the rights it conveys. [source](<https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/corp-fin-statement-tokenized-securities-012826-statement-tokenized-securities>)

A recorded connection is not the same as independent verification. The token’s presence alone does not establish who issued a certificate, whether the issuer had authority, or whether the underlying facts are true. A record that links a token to an item can indicate the intended association, but evidence about the item and the issuer remains a separate matter.

Similarly, a token linked to a claim does not by itself settle the claim’s legal effect, transferability, or redemption conditions. Those may depend on the applicable terms and circumstances. The token can make a record identifiable; it cannot replace the separate evidence needed to understand what the issuer promises or what the linked asset actually is.

## What an NFT token does not establish by itself

An NFT alone does not establish ownership of copyright, physical property, or exclusive control of referenced content. Its technical identity is separate from the rights, if any, associated with a work or asset. The relevant terms and circumstances determine what those rights are; the token record should not be treated as a substitute for them.

Persistence and access also require separate consideration. Metadata can refer to content, but an ERC-721 token URI does not guarantee that the metadata or referenced media will remain available, accurate, permanent, or licensed. A service that uses a token to identify access must separately recognize it and apply its own conditions. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)

A practical way to read an NFT example is to separate three questions. First, what token is being referenced? Where the ERC-721 model applies, that means identifying the contract address and token ID. Second, what does its metadata actually point to, and are there limitations on the referenced content? Third, what does an issuer, game, event, or service say about associated rights or access? The token record can help answer the first question, but the others call for separate evidence.

This approach applies across collectibles, games, tickets, memberships, and records linked to claims. It avoids treating a technical identifier as proof of everything connected to it. Security, recovery, fees, availability, compliance, tax, and legal treatment are separate, context-specific questions; the examples here do not establish their answers.
