## What “CS50 blockchain” refers to

In an educational context, the phrase may be shorthand for CS50-related teaching or material about blockchain. That is a possible interpretation, not confirmation that a specific course or resource exists under that name. The exact reference matters: a course, an individual lesson, and a third-party description of educational material are different things.

A blockchain, by contrast, is a technical system rather than a teaching title. NIST describes a blockchain as a tamper-evident and tamper-resistant digital ledger implemented in a distributed fashion. This definition distinguishes the subject from the material that might teach it; it does not verify that CS50 offers a particular course or lesson. [source](<https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2018/nist.ir.8202.pdf>)

The phrase alone also does not identify a blockchain network, protocol, token, wallet, or payment product. Educational material could discuss a blockchain without naming or operating one. Mentioning a technical system in a course would not make the course itself infrastructure or a service.

## What blockchain topics educational material may cover

The evidence here does not establish the topics covered by any particular CS50 course or lesson, so it would be inaccurate to attribute a curriculum. As general context only—not as a description of CS50—technical material may use terms such as “distributed ledger.” One high-level account of transaction processing describes a transaction becoming part of a shared ledger after cryptographic authorization, validation under network rules, grouping into a block, and a consensus decision. This is a general lifecycle description, not a confirmed lesson topic or a universal implementation rule. [source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8202/final>)

Specific network designs also differ. Bitcoin’s design selects the valid chain representing the greatest cumulative proof of work when competing histories exist. That is a Bitcoin-specific description, not evidence that every blockchain uses the same rule.
