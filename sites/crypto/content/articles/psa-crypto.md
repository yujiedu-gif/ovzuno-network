## What “PSA crypto” refers to in Singapore

In this context, “PSA” is shorthand for the Payment Services Act, while “PSA crypto” is a search phrase. The phrase itself does not name a particular regulated service, show that a business is covered, or establish an exemption. ## How the Payment Services Act can relate to crypto activities

A cautious scope inquiry starts with the service a business actually performs, rather than the name of a token, protocol, or product. A cryptocurrency system, for example, can include protocol rules, a transaction ledger, and participants who update, store, and read that ledger. Implementations differ in permission model, consensus, governance, and ledger access. These features describe system architecture; they do not by themselves answer whether a business activity falls within a Singapore statutory category. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2018/cryptocurrencies-looking-beyond-hype>)

The same distinction matters when a business handles, transfers, or facilitates transactions. The evidence here does not provide the Payment Services Act’s current definitions of relevant services or establish how the Act treats receiving, holding, transferring, exchanging, or arranging crypto transactions. It would be unwarranted to say that every crypto business is covered. It would be equally unwarranted to assume that a business is outside the Act merely because it uses software, a token, or a system described as decentralized.

A business may combine several functions. A centralized exchange operator, for instance, may combine third-party trading, proprietary trading, lending, token issuance, and custody, although not every operator performs every function. This is an example of possible business roles, not a statement about Singapore licensing or the classification of any particular service. Each function and the actual arrangement require separate review. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/aer-2022/future-monetary-system>)

That example illustrates why an activity-based inquiry should avoid treating a business as a single undifferentiated service. A customer-facing service, an operational role, and the underlying technology may be different parts of an arrangement. The evidence here does not establish how Singapore law classifies those parts, so the example should guide fact collection rather than substitute for checking the statute.

## Facts a business should map before assessing scope

Before comparing a business model with legal language, record how the service works in practice. This is a fact-gathering framework, not a licensing checklist or legal determination. A useful description separates the roles and actions involved:

- What service is offered, to whom, and which entity performs each part?
- Who initiates, receives, or benefits from a transaction, and what does any intermediary do?
- Does the business receive, hold, transfer, exchange, or otherwise control assets or value, where applicable?
- Which actions are performed by the business, an affiliate, a technology provider, or another service provider?
- Where do relevant activities take place, and which parties are involved?
- What does the customer-facing product promise, and what does it actually do operationally?

Describe functions separately from labels such as “platform,” “protocol,” “wallet,” or “exchange.” A product label can summarize a service, but it does not establish the legal character of the activity. Likewise, understanding what a holder can claim against an issuer or another party may matter when describing an arrangement; the relationship between a token and any backing asset should not be assumed from the token’s technical record alone. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/wholesale-digital-tokens>)

Where a feature’s operation affects the factual description, document who can initiate or authorize actions, what assets or value the business handles, and which entity carries out each step. Do not assume that a software component’s presence resolves who provides a service. Nor should a business treat a general label such as “decentralized” as a conclusion about the activities performed by identifiable entities. This framework helps make the facts clear; it does not supply the missing legal classification.
