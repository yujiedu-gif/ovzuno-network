## Crypto infrastructure versus a crypto asset

Crypto infrastructure, in this context, means tools or services used to build or operate applications involving crypto networks. A cryptocurrency, in the technical sense used here, is a digital asset, credit, or unit that can be cryptographically transferred between users of a blockchain network. Infrastructure and assets are therefore different categories: one refers to tools or services; the other to a transferable digital unit.

That distinction does not tell you what any specific organization offers. A platform name, by itself, is not proof that the organization operates a network, issues an asset, provides a wallet or custody service, or offers any other particular function. Each description requires evidence about the specific service or asset, rather than an inference from the brand name or the word “crypto.”

The distinction matters because a software or service reference can appear in the same general context as a token without being that token. Conversely, mentioning a crypto asset does not establish which tools, providers, or networks support it. Treat the asset and the infrastructure used around it as separate subjects unless reliable documentation connects them in a specific way.
