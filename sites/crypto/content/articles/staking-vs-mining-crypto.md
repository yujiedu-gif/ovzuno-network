# Staking vs Mining Crypto: How the Models Differ

Staking and mining describe participation in different consensus models, not interchangeable ways to earn crypto. In proof of stake (PoS), cryptocurrency is committed as stake, and stake influences consensus participation or weight. In proof of work (PoW), participants use computation to solve a problem that is costly to solve but straightforward for other nodes to verify. The protocol determines how participation works; the broad labels do not specify a universal eligibility rule, reward, penalty, or resource requirement.

The comparison below separates general definitions from an Ethereum-specific PoS example. It focuses on the network role, the evidence needed to assess resources and custody, and the risks that can be stated from the available protocol claims. It does not establish current terms for any particular network beyond the Ethereum details identified as such.

## Staking and mining serve different consensus models

PoS uses cryptocurrency committed as stake, with stake influencing consensus participation or weight. Selection, delegation, custody, withdrawal, reward, and penalty rules are protocol-specific. The general definition therefore does not establish whether a participant must operate a validator, can delegate, faces a minimum, or can withdraw at a particular time.

PoW uses a computational problem that is costly to solve but straightforward for other nodes to verify. Algorithms, difficulty rules, and hardware differ by network. That definition describes the work’s role in the consensus model; it does not specify a particular machine, operating cost, or compensation arrangement.

These are not exhaustive categories for all crypto networks. A comparison should begin by identifying the network and its consensus mechanism, then consult that network’s rules for eligibility, assigned duties, and participation conditions. Do not infer a specific operating model from the words “staking” or “mining” alone.

## How rewards and penalties differ

A universal yield comparison cannot be drawn from “staking” and “mining.” The available general claims do not supply rates or a common basis for comparing compensation across networks, configurations, or periods.

For Ethereum, validator incentives include rewards for correct participation, missed rewards or penalties for inactivity, and slashing for specified conflicting messages. These are Ethereum proof-of-stake rules. They do not establish the reward or penalty rules for other PoS protocols, and no current rate is asserted here. The specified basis for slashing matters: this claim concerns conflicting messages, not every operational mistake or outage.

For PoW, the accepted definition establishes the computational mechanism but does not state a particular network’s block rewards, transaction-fee rules, pool terms, or expected operator return. Those details must be verified in current documentation for the network and, where relevant, the specific arrangement. Do not treat a general description of mining as evidence of compensation.

When comparing economic exposure, separate protocol rules from service terms. On Ethereum, the stated incentives include both rewards and possible missed rewards, penalties, or slashing under the conditions described above. For another network or a third-party arrangement, verify the applicable rules rather than assuming Ethereum’s treatment carries over. Historical results or estimates, if used in a separate analysis, should not be treated as guarantees of future outcomes.

## Risks to assess before choosing a model

**Protocol rules and penalties.** The PoS definition makes selection, delegation, custody, withdrawal, rewards, and penalties protocol-specific. Ethereum’s stated rules include missed rewards or penalties for inactivity and slashing for specified conflicting messages. Confirm the exact conditions in the chosen protocol’s documentation; do not assume those Ethereum rules apply elsewhere.

**Resource and implementation questions.** The general PoW claim establishes computation and says that algorithms, difficulty rules, and hardware differ by network. It does not establish a universal hardware configuration or operating budget. The general PoS claim establishes stake’s role, but not a universal validator setup. Gather network- and setup-specific evidence before treating any resource estimate as applicable.

**Custody and third parties.** Custody rules are protocol-specific under the general PoS definition. In Ethereum, third-party staking adds custody, smart-contract, operator, fee, and legal risks beyond base-protocol staking. For other networks or arrangements, the available claims do not establish comparable terms. Review the actual documentation rather than assuming that delegation, pooling, hosting, or a service has a particular effect on control or liability.

**Withdrawal and liquidity.** The general claims do not specify lockups, withdrawal delays, or unbonding periods. Check the selected protocol’s current rules and any relevant arrangement terms before relying on an exit timeline. Do not infer liquidity conditions from the words “staking” or “mining.”

## A network-specific comparison checklist

Before committing tokens or infrastructure, identify the evidence needed for the exact network and arrangement:

1. **Consensus and eligibility:** Confirm the network’s consensus mechanism and who is eligible to participate. Verify any minimums or other conditions in authoritative, current protocol documentation.
2. **Role and duties:** Determine what validators or miners are required to do and how the protocol assigns participation or work. For Ethereum, the stated validator duties are checking and attesting to valid blocks and proposing a block when selected. Do not apply that example to another PoS network without evidence.
3. **Rewards and penalties:** Find the current reward calculation and all relevant penalty conditions. For Ethereum, distinguish rewards for correct participation, missed rewards or penalties for inactivity, and slashing for specified conflicting messages. Verify other protocols independently.
4. **Withdrawal and custody:** Check the network’s current withdrawal, lockup, or unbonding rules. For a third-party Ethereum staking arrangement, review the stated custody, smart-contract, operator, fee, and legal risks. For other arrangements, establish control and terms from their own documentation.
5. **Mining inputs:** Verify the chosen network’s algorithm, difficulty rules, hardware compatibility, and current arrangement terms. Obtain dated sources for any proposed energy or cost figures; these are not established by the general PoW definition.
6. **Staking implementation:** Verify the actual participation method, including delegation or third-party terms where relevant. Confirm any infrastructure, key-management, software, or connectivity requirements from network-specific technical documentation rather than assuming them universally.
7. **Comparison assumptions:** Keep protocol-defined rules separate from service terms and estimates. State the network, configuration, time period, and assumptions for any comparison, and do not present historical rewards or estimates as guaranteed outcomes.

Staking places committed cryptocurrency at the center of the general PoS definition; mining places computational work at the center of the general PoW definition. Beyond those distinctions, resource needs, rewards, penalties, custody, and exit conditions require network- and arrangement-specific evidence. A sound comparison begins with that documentation, not with assumptions about either model.
