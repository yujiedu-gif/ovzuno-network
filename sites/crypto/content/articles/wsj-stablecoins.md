## How stablecoin settlement works

A stablecoin aims to maintain a stable value relative to a specified asset, currency, or basket. “Stable” describes that objective; it does not, by itself, establish that the token’s market value or redemption value will always match its reference. ([source](<https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2023/NIST.IR.8408.pdf>))

Settlement is the process by which a payment obligation is completed. At a basic level, a token transfer records a change in token holdings on a ledger. But that record is only one part of a payment arrangement. Whether the transfer completes a buyer’s obligation, can be exchanged for the reference asset, or settles obligations between institutions depends on the surrounding arrangements—not just on the token moving between addresses.

Some fiat-reserve-backed stablecoin models use minting after an issuer receives collateral and burning when a holder redeems, with offchain reserve assets supporting the process. The actual reserve composition, custody, legal rights, eligibility, minimums, fees, and redemption timing vary by arrangement. ([source](<https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2023/NIST.IR.8408.pdf>)) The model therefore does not justify assuming that every token can be redeemed by every holder, on demand, on identical terms.

A token’s presence on a ledger is also distinct from a holder’s ability to redeem it. Issuance and redemption near a reference value can create an arbitrage path between a primary market and secondary-market prices. But fees, minimums, eligibility, delays, market liquidity, banking access, or suspended redemption can weaken or block that path. ([source](<https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/dp/2025/english/usea.pdf>))

## What settlement may change—and what it does not

Programmable digital ledgers can represent financial assets and liabilities, enabling platform-based workflows across parts of an asset’s life cycle. But programmability does not remove the need for legal certainty, governance, operational resilience, or reliable settlement assets. ([source](<https://www.imf.org/en/publications/imf-notes/issues/2026/04/01/tokenized-finance-574921>)) A token transfer may be one step in a workflow; it does not by itself resolve every obligation or dependency around the payment.

For example, a transfer recorded on one blockchain does not establish that a recipient can use the token on another blockchain or redeem it through the same arrangement. Stablecoins represented on different chains may rely on different issuers, contracts, custody, or bridge mechanisms. ([source](<https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2023/NIST.IR.8408.pdf>)) Those differences matter when someone describes a payment as broadly available, interoperable, or settled end to end.

A settlement claim should therefore specify what event it calls “settlement.” It might refer to a ledger record, receipt by a merchant, redemption by a holder, or completion of obligations between institutions. These are not interchangeable descriptions. Nor does a token transfer alone demonstrate universal acceptance, immediate redemption, or lower total cost. Those conclusions require evidence about the particular arrangement and the institutions and services around it.

## What to verify before relying on a settlement claim

Start by identifying the token and the claim being made. Ask which reference asset the token aims to track, which issuer or governing arrangement is involved, and which ledger and token representation are being discussed. If a claim concerns use across chains, check whether the representations rely on distinct contracts, custody, or bridge mechanisms rather than assuming they are identical. ([source](<https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2023/NIST.IR.8408.pdf>))

Next, examine the operational details that the claim leaves implicit:

- **Transfer and settlement:** What event is described as completion—a ledger transfer, redemption, or settlement of an obligation between institutions?
- **Redemption:** Who can redeem, under what conditions, and what fees, minimums, delays, or eligibility rules apply? These details are arrangement-specific, and access to redemption may be limited or interrupted. ([source](<https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2023/NIST.IR.8408.pdf>)) ([source](<https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/dp/2025/english/usea.pdf>))
- **Rights and backing:** What claim does a holder have, and how do reserve assets relate to that claim? These questions are central to understanding an asset-backed token arrangement, but their answers depend on its structure and applicable law. ([source](<https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P170723-3.pdf>))
- **Availability and recovery:** Which token representations and ledgers are supported? What documented process applies if a transfer, redemption, or access pathway fails? Do not treat a token’s stated reference value as evidence that loss or recovery outcomes are guaranteed.

For regulation, tax, and compliance, look for current authoritative information that applies to the relevant jurisdiction and arrangement. The evidence summarized here does not establish a jurisdiction-specific legal status, tax treatment, or universal set of compliance obligations. Avoid turning a general description of a stablecoin into a legal conclusion about a particular issuer, holder, or payment.

## Separate the reported claim from the system evidence

When evaluating an article about stablecoin settlement, first confirm the publication date and the scope of the article’s claim. Then trace operational or quantitative statements to documentation about the specific token arrangement and process, where available. An issuer’s description, a proposal, or an estimate should not be presented as a demonstrated system property without evidence supporting that interpretation.

The central distinction is simple: “WSJ stablecoins” is ambiguous unless tied to a particular report, while stablecoin settlement describes a real set of arrangements whose behavior depends on the token, ledger, redemption terms, and surrounding institutions. A careful reading keeps those questions separate and asks what evidence supports each step—from transfer record to completed payment.
