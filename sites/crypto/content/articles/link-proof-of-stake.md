## What “link proof of stake” can mean

The phrase can blend two different ideas: LINK, the token named in the search, and “proof of stake,” a term for a blockchain consensus model. Context helps resolve the ambiguity. A reference to LINK or Chainlink points toward token staking in a particular program; a discussion of selecting participants to help establish blockchain history points toward consensus staking.

“Staking” is not enough on its own to identify which mechanism is meant. In proof of stake, cryptocurrency is committed as stake and influences a participant’s consensus weight. The rules for selection, delegation, custody, rewards, penalties, and withdrawal depend on the specific protocol. These are network-level design questions, not implications of a token’s name.

## LINK staking versus proof-of-stake consensus

Token staking means committing or otherwise participating with a token under a specific program’s rules. Proof-of-stake consensus is a blockchain mechanism in which stake influences participation or weight in consensus. The same word—staking—can therefore describe activities with different purposes and responsibilities.

A token’s staking program does not, by itself, establish that the network associated with the token uses proof-of-stake consensus. To determine what a particular program does, look for its stated function and the system it affects. Does the documentation describe participation in a token program, or does it describe protocol rules for agreeing on blocks and recording transactions? Do not infer one from the other.

Protocol-specific rules also matter. As an example of a particular network’s design, Ethereum validator participation uses ETH as slashable economic collateral. That example illustrates that consensus staking can be tied to a network’s validator role, but it does not establish that another token’s staking program has the same role or mechanics.
