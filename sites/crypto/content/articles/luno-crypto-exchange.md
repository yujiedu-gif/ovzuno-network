## How to describe an exchange’s place in market infrastructure

Market infrastructure is the set of functions and arrangements involved in access to a market, trading, custody, settlement, and related services. A trading venue is one part of that picture. Custody concerns holding or controlling assets; settlement concerns completing obligations arising from a transaction. These functions may be combined or separated across different firms and services.

A common centralized-exchange model offers a limited illustration: a customer typically transfers funds and custody to the exchange before trading on its internal order book. That is a general model, not a verified description of Luno’s arrangements. Custody and legal ownership arrangements vary by platform and jurisdiction. ([source](<https://www.bis.org/publ/work1227.pdf>))

A broader conceptual map can include participants and access interfaces connected to trading systems, with custody, settlement, data, and external-service relationships varying by arrangement. This is a way to organize questions, not a universal technical design or a claim about Luno’s architecture.

For a Luno-specific assessment, keep the questions separate: What trading service is documented? Which entity provides it? Who holds or controls assets? How are transaction obligations settled? A statement about trading alone does not answer the other questions.
