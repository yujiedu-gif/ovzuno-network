## How to verify current operating and access claims

Treat each statement about present operations as a separate verification task. Website access, account access, deposits, withdrawals, trading, and availability of a particular asset are distinct claims. Evidence for one does not establish the others, and the evidence supplied here does not verify any of these conditions for Hotbit.

Start by locating a dated statement that identifies its author or issuing entity and specifies the service or user group covered. Determine whether it is an official statement, independent reporting, or a public record, and do not blur these source types. Record when the statement was published and whether it describes a past event, a temporary condition, or an ongoing service. A summary that drops those qualifications can make narrow evidence appear broader or more current than it is.

For material claims, look for independent, reputable reporting or relevant public records that address the same question. Compare the dates and the exact facts each source supports. Multiple pages repeating an unattributed statement do not, by repetition alone, establish that it is current or independently corroborated. If sources conflict, report the conflict and the limits of what they establish rather than selecting the more reassuring statement.

A useful way to organize the check is to write down the precise claim and the evidence needed for it:

- **Website or platform access:** What specific page or function is said to be accessible, and when was that documented? Access to a page is not evidence that other functions work.
- **Deposits:** Is there current, attributable evidence that the relevant deposit function is available? A historical instruction does not answer that question today.
- **Withdrawals:** Is there separate evidence for withdrawals, including the service and user scope? Do not infer this from evidence about deposits or from a general platform announcement.
- **Accounts:** Does the source establish that a specified account function works now, or only describe an earlier service or policy?
- **Assets:** Is the statement about a specific asset and a specific service, with a date? A past mention does not show that the asset is currently available to users.

When current, authoritative evidence is not available, the accurate description is that the condition is unverified. This avoids turning uncertainty into either a positive claim that a service works or a negative claim that it does not. The distinction is especially important when readers might rely on an operational statement to make decisions involving an account or funds.
