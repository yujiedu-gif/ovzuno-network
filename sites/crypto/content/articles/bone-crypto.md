## What BONE crypto refers to

“BONE” is a name used to refer to an asset, but a name alone does not identify a particular token contract. This article’s allowed evidence does not confirm a BONE contract address, its network, or an official source that defines its current status. Those facts should remain unresolved here rather than being supplied from an unverified listing or description.

A useful starting point is to separate three things that can be conflated: the asset label, the token’s technical implementation, and the project’s description of its intended role. The label is how an asset may be named in an interface or discussion. The implementation is the specific contract or other network record. The stated role is what project documentation says the asset is for. Confirming one does not automatically confirm the others.

Token interfaces illustrate why that distinction matters. ERC-20, for example, defines a common interface for fungible tokens, including total supply, balances, transfers, allowances, approvals, and transfer and approval events. But interface conformity does not establish a token’s issuance policy, backing, market value, compliance status, or implementation safety. A familiar interface is therefore not proof that an asset is official or that every claim about it is accurate. [^erc20]

When identifying BONE, check for primary documentation that explicitly provides the relevant network and contract address. Compare both details with the asset information in the source you are evaluating. If a source gives only a name or ticker, it has not supplied enough information to distinguish a specific contract from another asset using a similar label.

## What to verify before relying on BONE information

Use a claim-by-claim review rather than treating a ticker or broad ecosystem description as sufficient evidence:

- **Identity:** Look for official documentation that specifies the network and contract address. Compare those details with the asset record or interface you are reviewing.
- **Source and timing:** Identify who published a role description and whether it refers to a current, historical, or planned function. Do not convert an undated or aspirational description into a statement of present availability.
- **Specific function:** Check whether the source expressly documents governance, protocol use, or access. Do not infer one function from another or from the asset’s name.
- **Platform connection:** Verify claims about ShibaSwap and Shibarium separately. A relationship to one does not prove a relationship to the other or a requirement to hold BONE.
- **Operational claims:** Seek separate, current evidence for availability, fees, security, and recovery. A stated ecosystem role does not establish those conditions.
