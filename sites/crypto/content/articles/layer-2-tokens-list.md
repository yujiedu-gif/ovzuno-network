## What qualifies for a layer 2 tokens list?

A layer 2 is a scaling system that operates in relation to an underlying blockchain. The label does not mean every system works in the same way. For Ethereum Layer 2 systems, some security may derive from Ethereum Layer 1, but the exact guarantee depends on the system’s proof, data availability, settlement, governance, and exit mechanisms. This does not establish that every layer 2 has a separate token or shares the same design. [^1]

For inclusion in a list, require authoritative network materials that explicitly connect a token to the network and describe the claimed role. Governance or protocol utility are possible categories, not assumptions: the specific network’s documentation must support the label. A token’s presence in an application, wallet, or network does not alone show that it belongs to the network or operates its protocol.

A usable catalog also states its scope and review date. Token roles and associated documentation can change, so a reader needs dated evidence to assess whether a claim is current. No current, network-specific token records are authorized for this article. Therefore, it cannot provide a date-stamped catalog of named examples or claim to be representative or exhaustive. That is an evidence limitation, not a conclusion that layer 2 networks do not have tokens.

## How to verify whether a token belongs to a layer 2 network

Begin with the network’s own current protocol and governance materials. Look for an explicit statement of what the token does and whether the role belongs to the network, a protocol component, or an application. A ticker, project name, or third-party token listing is not enough to establish that relationship.

Then confirm the token identity on the network named in the claim. A name alone may not uniquely identify a token. Check the contract address against authoritative project or network records, and make sure those records refer to the same network and representation. Explorer references can commonly identify transactions by hash, blocks by hash or height, and addresses. Those lookup capabilities help locate records; they do not prove that the asset has a network-level role. [^2]

Next, separate three questions: Is the asset described as part of the network’s protocol or governance? Is it issued by an application operating on the network? Or is it simply transferable or supported there? These are distinct relationships, and evidence for one does not establish another.

If authoritative sources conflict, omit the stronger claim and describe the uncertainty. A careful entry might say that a token contract is identifiable but the network-level function is unconfirmed. Record the review date so readers can distinguish a verified statement from an undated or outdated label. Since no named network-token pairing is substantiated in the evidence available here, this article cannot demonstrate that process with a specific token example.

## Network token, governance token, and assets on the network

“Network-associated token” is a useful editorial category, not a technical or legal status that follows automatically from a token’s name. A *governance token* is one whose network materials explicitly connect it to governance. That label alone does not establish what decisions holders can make or how a decision takes effect. A *protocol utility token* likewise requires evidence specifying its protocol function. No named layer 2 token’s role is verified here.

An asset can be used on a network without being that network’s own token. An application may issue or use an asset there; that fact does not show that the network assigns it a governance or protocol role. In a different context, a third-party tokenized security may represent a custodial entitlement or synthetic instrument rather than direct ownership of the referenced security. This is not evidence about layer 2 tokens; it illustrates why a token label does not, by itself, establish the rights or relationship being claimed. [^3]

A network may have no separately documented token, while applications on it may have tokens of their own. Conversely, a token may be associated with network governance or operation only if the relevant materials establish that relationship. Do not infer control, value, security, or access rights from a ticker or a broad category label. Verify the exact claim against the relevant records.
