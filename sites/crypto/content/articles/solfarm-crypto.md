## Why solfarm crypto is not proof-of-work mining

Proof of work is a consensus model in which a participant seeking to publish a block solves a computational problem that is hard to solve but relatively straightforward for other nodes to verify. The process consumes computational effort, time, and energy. It is a network-level method for block publication, not a general term for every activity involving digital assets. [source](<https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2018/nist.ir.8202.pdf>)

A mining pool illustrates one feature of this process: a pool can measure contributed work using “shares” that meet a pool target but usually do not meet the harder network target. A share is therefore not necessarily a block accepted by the network. This example is specific to pooled mining; it does not describe yield farming or establish how any Solfarm-related product worked. [source](<https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/mining.html>)

Yield farming, as used in the question, concerns using assets within protocols or strategies that may involve incentives. It does not, simply by being called “farming,” mean that users perform proof-of-work calculations, propose blocks, or participate in a mining pool. The permitted evidence here does not document Solfarm’s particular strategies, incentives, or asset handling, so those details should not be inferred from its name.

The practical distinction is about function. Proof-of-work mining refers to computational work within a network’s block-production and consensus process. Yield farming refers to an application- or protocol-level activity involving assets. These are different categories, even if both may appear in discussions of cryptocurrency. ## Yield farming, consensus, and network participation

Consensus is the network process for coordinating which state or history participants accept. Proof of stake is one consensus model: cryptocurrency is committed as stake, and stake influences a participant’s consensus weight. The specific rules for selection, delegation, custody, rewards, penalties, and withdrawals vary by protocol. The general definition does not establish the mechanics of a named application. [source](<https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/proof_of_stake_consensus_model>)

Solana’s delegation model is a protocol-specific example of network participation. A delegator can assign stake to a validator, increasing that validator’s consensus weight while the delegator retains token control under the stake-account model.
