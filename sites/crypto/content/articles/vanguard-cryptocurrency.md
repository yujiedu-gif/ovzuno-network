## What “Vanguard cryptocurrency” can refer to

The phrase can mean at least two different things: buying or holding cryptocurrency directly, or accessing an investment product connected to cryptocurrency. Those arrangements should not be treated as interchangeable. A product label or a connection to crypto-related activity does not establish that a customer owns cryptocurrency, can transfer it, or has a direct claim to an underlying asset. The product’s official terms must establish what is held and what rights the customer receives.

For a question about direct access, look for explicit language stating whether the relevant account can buy, hold, or transfer the cryptocurrency in question. For a related investment product, identify its holdings, stated objective, and the mechanism that creates any exposure. Do not infer direct ownership from exposure alone.

The distinction is about structure and rights, not a judgment about which arrangement is preferable. For example, an asset-backed token is a digital record associated with an asset, but the record alone does not determine legal title or enforceability. A holder’s claim and the relationship between the token and backing assets require clarity. This is a general consideration, not a description of a Vanguard product or a claim that Vanguard offers an asset-backed token.

## Direct ownership versus related investment products

For a product described as cryptocurrency-related, read its current prospectus or equivalent official disclosure. Identify what the product holds, its stated investment objective, and how its structure is intended to produce exposure. Then review the fees, liquidity terms, and risks described in those materials. These are questions to answer from product-specific documents; the available evidence does not identify or describe a current Vanguard product, so it cannot supply those details.

Keep exposure separate from ownership rights. A customer’s economic exposure to an asset does not, by itself, establish that the customer holds that asset directly or can transfer it. Look for explicit disclosure of the asset held and the customer’s rights, including any limits or conditions. If the documents do not make those points clear, do not fill the gaps with assumptions based on a product name.

Where a product or arrangement involves tokens and backing assets, examine what or whom the holder has a claim against and how those backing assets relate to the claim. For global stablecoin arrangements, policy recommendations call for transparent information about matters including governance, reserve composition and custody, legal claims, redemption rights, operations, and risk management. Those recommendations are disclosure considerations only. They do not show that a Vanguard product is a stablecoin, follows the recommendations, or provides a particular claim or redemption right.

## A decision checklist before taking action

Before opening an account or placing an order, check the following in current official materials:

- **Availability:** Does Vanguard explicitly describe the specific transaction or product as available for the account in question?
- **Date and scope:** Is the statement current, and does it apply to the account type and circumstances being considered?
- **Structure:** Does the product hold cryptocurrency directly, hold another asset, or provide exposure through a different arrangement?
- **Rights:** What does the governing disclosure say the customer owns or has a claim to, and what limits apply?
- **Costs and risks:** What fees, liquidity terms, and risks are disclosed for the specific product?
- **Operations:** What do the documents say about custody, control, and recovery? Do not assume protections that are not stated.
- **Other considerations:** Which official U.S. disclosures apply, and do personal or business circumstances call for qualified tax or legal advice?

If a material point is unclear, request clarification from Vanguard through an official channel and review the governing documents before acting. This checklist does not confirm current Vanguard availability; it identifies the evidence needed to assess access, restrictions, structure, and risks without confusing direct cryptocurrency ownership with a related investment product.
