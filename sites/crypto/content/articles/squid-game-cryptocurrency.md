“Squid game cryptocurrency” does not identify one specific crypto asset. The phrase may refer to the Netflix series, a crypto token or project using a related name, or multiple assets with similar branding. To identify a particular token, readers need its blockchain network and contract address, then attributable project materials that point to the same identifiers. Without those details, the specific asset and any claimed connection to the series remain unconfirmed.

## What “squid game cryptocurrency” can refer to

The phrase is not a unique asset identifier. It combines a recognizable entertainment name with a broad term, but a familiar name or ticker does not tell readers which on-chain token is meant. Names and symbols should be treated as clues to investigate, not as proof that two references describe the same asset.

For example, ERC-20 defines a common interface for fungible tokens, including functions and events related to supply, balances, transfers, allowances, and approvals. That interface does not establish a token’s backing, issuance policy, market value, compliance, or code safety. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-20>) Knowing that a token follows a standard therefore does not resolve who created it, what its name implies, or whether it has any relationship to a television series.

A reference might also concern a project, a website, or another service using similar branding rather than a particular token contract. A decentralized application can combine a smart contract with a frontend interface, while supporting services may introduce their own dependencies. [source](<https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/dapps/>) A website’s name or appearance alone does not identify the contract it interacts with or establish an affiliation with an entertainment property.

The practical starting point is to separate three questions: What exact asset or project is being named? Which network and contract does the reference identify? What attributable evidence supports any claim about who made it or what it is connected to? A name by itself answers none of these reliably.

## How to identify a specific Squid Game-named token

First, copy the exact name and symbol from the reference under review. Keep their capitalization and spelling as shown, but do not treat either as conclusive. Then seek the blockchain network and contract address. These details provide a more precise basis for matching a reference to an on-chain token than branding alone.

The network is essential context. Asset support can be specific to a chain and token representation; a provider may support particular combinations rather than every token that shares a symbol. [source](<https://developers.circle.com/circle-mint/supported-chains-and-currencies>) Accordingly, an address or ticker without its network may be incomplete identification. Do not silently assume that a token on one chain is the same asset as one on another chain merely because their names match.

Next, compare the network and address in the sources available to you. Project documentation, project communications, and any public record cited by those materials should be checked for consistency. If one source names a contract but omits the network, or different sources provide different addresses, record the discrepancy as unresolved. Do not pick the identifier that looks most plausible and present it as verified.

The appropriate identifier can also depend on the token format. For an ERC-721 non-fungible token, the identifying pair is the smart-contract address and token ID on an Ethereum chain; a name, symbol, or media link is not a reliable global identifier. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>) This is not a reason to apply an NFT identification method to every token. Establish what kind of asset the source describes before using a standard’s identifiers.

A useful verification note can be brief and factual: quote the name and symbol as presented, record the stated network and contract address, and note which attributable materials repeat those details. If the source provides no network or address, say that the reference does not identify a specific token rather than supplying missing information from memory or inference.

## What public records can and cannot establish

A network record can support claims about activity represented in that network’s data, but it does not automatically establish the identity of a person, organization, or brand behind an address. As one example of the limits of transaction interpretation, a Bitcoin transaction input spends a specific prior output identified by transaction ID and output index; additional evidence is needed to attribute that activity to a person, project, or brand. [source](<https://developer.bitcoin.org/devguide/transactions.html>) The same caution applies when readers encounter a token address or transaction: the record is not, by itself, proof of the real-world story attached to it.

Project-published materials can show what a project says about its purpose, creators, or claimed relationships. When a project states that it is connected to a show or rights holder, that is evidence of the project’s assertion—not independent proof that the relationship exists. The materials should be described accurately as claims unless attributable evidence supports a stronger statement. If no such evidence is available, the affiliation remains unverified.

Keep the evidence categories distinct. A blockchain record concerns activity or contract data on a specified network. Project materials describe what the project represents about itself. Independent reporting, if available and attributable, may provide separate evidence about people or relationships. These sources can address different questions; one does not automatically confirm the others.

A project website can help identify what the project says, but matching its branding to an on-chain asset still requires checking the network and contract details. If the website’s address differs from a record’s address, or if the record cannot be tied to the project’s own documentation, the match has not been established. A missing or conflicting identifier is a finding to report, not a gap to fill with an assumption.

## Warning signs when names and claims do not match

Treat a familiar name or ticker without a network and contract address as an incomplete reference. Likewise, take care when project materials, other cited records, and the identifier being discussed do not point to the same network and address. Similar logos, wording, or symbols do not resolve such a mismatch.

Claims of a connection to the Netflix series, a rights holder, or another organization need attributable support. Branding alone does not establish authorization, endorsement, or affiliation. If the source supplies no verifiable basis for the claim, repeat it only as an unverified claim—or leave it out.

Do not infer current availability, security, or recovery prospects from a name, website, token standard, or record of on-chain activity. Those questions need evidence specific to the asset, network, service, and relevant circumstances. The identification steps here do not test a contract or establish that a project is safe or unsafe.

The same boundary applies to compliance. A token’s name or recorded activity does not, by itself, settle its regulatory or legal treatment. No jurisdiction-specific conclusion follows from identifying a name, network, or address; such a conclusion requires evidence specific to the relevant jurisdiction and situation.

## Questions this identification does not answer

Identifying a token or project does not establish its price, value, suitability, or future performance. This article does not assess whether anyone should buy, sell, trade, or use an asset. Its purpose is limited to resolving what a reference identifies and distinguishing that evidence from an asserted connection to a show.

Identification also does not establish fees, yields, security, or whether funds can be recovered. Those questions depend on the specific asset, network, service, and circumstances, and require current, attributable evidence. If the available materials do not supply it, the answer is unknown—not a guess based on branding or a token label.

Finally, identification alone does not determine jurisdictional availability, legal status, tax treatment, or compliance obligations. Those matters require evidence appropriate to the relevant place and circumstances. When a source provides only a name, or identifiers and claims conflict, the defensible conclusion is that the specific asset or relationship has not been verified.
