## What payment tasks can a crypto app support?

A payment app is best understood by the task it performs, not by its label. A send feature may let a user initiate a transfer to a recipient; a receive feature may provide details for a transfer; a checkout feature may help arrange payment to a merchant. These are different workflows, and an app’s support for one does not establish that it supports the others.

The app can be an interface within a larger technology stack. An application may connect users with blockchain nodes and smart contracts while also relying on wallets, data-indexing services, storage, and other off-chain services. Those components can be operated by different parties, and a particular app may use only some of them. The interface alone does not establish who holds assets, processes a payment, or resolves a failed transaction. [source](<https://ethereum.org/developers/docs/ethereum-stack/>)

Keep payment functions distinct from trading or investment features. A trading screen may let a user exchange assets, but that does not establish that the app can send a payment to a merchant, or explain the merchant’s receipt and settlement process. Assess the exact feature and the particular payment task rather than assuming that an app’s broader crypto functions apply to checkout.

## Follow the payment flow from sender to recipient

For any proposed payment, trace the steps in order: who initiates it, what asset and destination are selected, which service handles the transfer, what the recipient must do, and what event counts as completion. Ask where custody or conversion may occur, and whether a separate processor or service is involved. The available evidence does not establish a universal payment sequence for apps in the Philippines, so do not treat any one provider’s flow as a general rule.

A documented purchase flow from one operator in Australia illustrates how many stages a particular service can include: selecting an asset and amount, accepting disclosures, completing phone and identity checks, scanning a wallet QR code, inserting cash, confirming the transaction, and receiving crypto. This is an operator- and jurisdiction-specific example, not a description of every app or a claim about services available in the Philippines. [source](<https://coinflip.tech/en-AU/bitcoin-atm>)

At each step, identify the responsible party and the condition for proceeding. For example, clarify whether the app merely presents a payment instruction or whether another service handles conversion or asset custody. Check what the recipient needs to confirm receipt, whether the interface distinguishes a pending payment from a completed one, and what happens if the expected destination or asset is unsupported. Do not assume that an app’s displayed status, by itself, establishes the recipient’s settlement conditions.

## Compare fees, limits, and payment conditions
