## Evidence that connects Spectra to staking or validators

A credible connection requires primary technical material that names the identified network and explains the role at issue. Look for protocol or network documentation that describes validators, their responsibilities, and the consensus process. Then determine whether “staking” is described as a protocol mechanism, a third-party service, or not described at all.

These are different kinds of claims. A protocol document may describe network participation, while a service’s materials may describe a product offered to users. One cannot substitute for the other. If a page only uses terms such as “validator” or “stake” without explaining what those participants do or how the described network uses them, it does not establish the technical connection on its own.

Promotional language should not fill gaps in technical documentation. A claim about rewards, security, or validator operation needs evidence for that particular feature and network. If the primary materials do not describe the feature, the accurate conclusion is that it has not been verified from those materials—not that the feature is impossible or that every claim about it is false.

## How to verify a claimed network or token

Use a short identity-check sequence before interpreting staking claims:

1. **Find the project’s primary reference.** Identify the official documentation or statement that names the project and explains what it is.
2. **Record the network identifier.** Check that the staking or validator document refers to the same network, rather than relying on a similar name.
3. **Match any token address.** Where a token is involved, use an address explicitly associated with the project in its own documentation. Do not assume that a shared name or symbol identifies the same asset.
4. **Cross-check the reference.** Compare the project’s identifiers with reputable records where available. A matching entry may help resolve identity, but a listing alone does not prove legitimacy, technical design, or service quality.
5. **Check dates and scope.** Record when the documentation was published or updated and whether it describes the active network, a proposal, a test environment, or a third-party product.

Keep the conclusion no broader than the evidence. A document about one network or product does not automatically establish facts about every asset, application, or service using a similar name.
