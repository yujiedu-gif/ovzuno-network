It cannot confirm that Dune runs an NFT marketplace, offers a particular form of marketplace analytics, or has a formal relationship with a trading venue. ## Marketplace versus analytics platform

An NFT marketplace generally provides functions related to listing, discovery, or exchange. An analytics platform presents or enables examination of data. The categories can be related without being the same: a dashboard about activity on a marketplace is not, by itself, a place to list or trade an NFT. A service could combine functions, but that should be demonstrated by its own documentation rather than inferred from a label.

A practical distinction is to follow the documented user task. If the product describes dashboards, queries, charts, or indexed data, those descriptions point to an information or analysis function. If it documents listing or exchange functionality, that is evidence of a marketplace role. If the data page sends users to another service to transact, identify the destination separately. A link does not by itself prove that the analytics product operates or controls the venue.

This is also why data access should not be mistaken for transaction execution. Descriptive content about a market does not prove that the page itself offers a venue. Conversely, a marketplace may include information displays, but those displays do not establish the scope or accuracy of every analytics feature. Confirm each function separately.

## How to verify Dune’s current role

Use an evidence check tied to the exact product rather than relying on the search phrase:

1. **Locate current official product documentation.** Find the service’s own description of its functions. Look for direct statements about analytics, data queries, listing, or exchange. A general mention of NFTs or marketplace data does not prove marketplace functionality.
2. **Confirm the product identity and operator.** Check that the material belongs to the intended Dune service or to the exact similarly named product. Similar names are not enough to establish common ownership or affiliation.
3. **Inspect the cited feature, not just its title.** A dashboard title can refer to a marketplace while the dashboard itself only presents information. Read the feature description and determine whether it documents a data view, transaction workflow, or both.
4. **Trace any handoff to another site.** If a dashboard or page links to a different service for listing or trading, treat that destination as a separate product unless authoritative materials establish a relationship.
5. **Check freshness and scope.** Product descriptions can change. Prefer current official materials and make sure they refer to the specific product, feature, and network being discussed. An old article or third-party summary may not describe present capabilities.
6. **Keep unresolved claims unresolved.** If the official documentation does not say whether a product supports a particular function, do not infer the answer from a name, a chart, or a link. Say that the function could not be confirmed from the evidence reviewed.

This method separates two questions that are easy to blur: whether Dune or another named product provides analytics about NFT marketplaces, and whether that same service operates a marketplace. Evidence for one does not automatically establish the other. When describing a dashboard, say what its documentation shows; when describing a trading venue, identify the documentation that supports that separate claim.

## What the distinction means for digital ownership

A marketplace or analytics interface is not the same thing as the token record it displays. In the ERC-721 standard, an NFT is uniquely identified on an Ethereum chain by the combination of its smart-contract address and token ID. This helps distinguish the token from a product page, collection label, or image displayed in an interface.

The distinction also limits what a listing or dashboard can prove about rights. Ethereum’s general NFT description states that an NFT contract records ownership by associating a unique token identifier with an Ethereum address; token ownership does not automatically convey copyright, physical title, or every offchain right. A marketplace listing or analytics display therefore should not be treated as proof of the full rights connected to a work or asset.

For a particular token, the relevant evidence may include the token’s contract and identifier, the marketplace’s own documentation, and any associated rights or license terms. These sources answer different questions: the token record concerns its onchain state, the interface explains what the product displays or allows, and rights documentation addresses linked permissions or claims. A screen that presents data should not be treated as a substitute for those distinct records and terms.
