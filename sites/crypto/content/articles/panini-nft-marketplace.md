## Separate the collectible, token, and rights

A digital collectible may involve three different things: the content, a token record, and rights or permissions described in a license or other terms. The content might be media; the token is a record associated with a blockchain contract; and the governing terms describe what uses, if any, are permitted. Token ownership alone does not establish copyright ownership or transfer intellectual-property rights in the underlying work. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)

For context, ERC-721 is one Ethereum token standard. Under that standard, a token is identified within an Ethereum chain by the combination of its smart-contract address and token ID. The standard’s optional metadata interface can associate a token ID with a URI that refers to metadata for that token. These are general ERC-721 facts, not evidence that Panini uses ERC-721 or Ethereum. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)[source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)

A metadata URI is a reference, not a guarantee about the media it points to. The URI and referenced material may be stored onchain, use content addressing, or depend on mutable offchain hosting. The ERC-721 standard itself does not guarantee that referenced content will remain available, permanent, accurate, or licensed to a token holder. The specific arrangement for a Panini collectible would need to be confirmed from its contract, metadata, and platform documentation. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)[source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)

Rights require separate evidence. Read the applicable marketplace terms and any collectible-specific license to determine whether a holder is granted display, access, or other permissions, and whether conditions or reserved rights apply. A token label or standard does not by itself establish the scope of a license. The available evidence does not provide Panini’s terms or a license for any specific collectible, so no particular permission can be asserted here. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)

## How blockchain records fit into digital ownership

A blockchain record can identify a token under the relevant network and contract rules. It is not a complete account of the collectible’s content, the rights attached to it, or the systems required to view that content. In particular, a token’s metadata reference may lead to media hosted outside the blockchain, and the relevant standard alone does not guarantee that the media remains accessible. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)[source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)

Wallet control is another distinct concept. A crypto wallet generally stores or uses private keys or passcodes that allow a person to access and authorize crypto assets; the wallet does not store the assets themselves. Even where a wallet can interact with a token, that fact does not establish legal title to related media or guarantee access to a platform or referenced file. This is general technical context, not a statement about Panini’s account or custody arrangements. [source](<https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/crypto-asset-custody-basics-retail-investors-investor-bulletin-0>)

The blockchain network and token representation also matter. Support for a particular asset can depend on the specific chain and token representation; a shared symbol does not prove that two representations are equivalent or supported in the same way. The evidence provided here does not identify Panini’s network or supported token representations. Verify those details for the specific item in current official documentation rather than treating “NFT” as a complete technical description. [source](<https://developers.circle.com/circle-mint/supported-chains-and-currencies>)

This distinction also limits what can be concluded from a transaction record. A record may help establish token-related activity on the relevant chain, but it does not alone show that a reader has copyright, a particular license, or enduring access to associated content. For those questions, the token record and the governing terms answer different parts of the inquiry. [source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>)
