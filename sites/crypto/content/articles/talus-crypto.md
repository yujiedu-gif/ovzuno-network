## How to verify a Talus-related NFT claim

Begin by separating what the claim says is involved. An NFT-related statement might refer to a token record, content linked from the record, an access or usage function, or a legal right. These are different claims; evidence for one does not automatically establish the others.

If a claim identifies an Ethereum ERC-721 token, the token’s specific identity is the combination of its smart-contract address and token ID on an Ethereum chain. That combination can distinguish the referenced token from another with a similar name. It does not, on its own, confirm that the contract belongs to a particular project or that the token provides a claimed benefit. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>))

Next, check whether primary documentation explicitly names the relevant project or asset and describes the asserted function. For linked content, look for documentation explaining what is linked and what the link is meant to represent. For a claimed access or usage function, look for technical documentation or terms that specify that function. Do not infer either from the word “NFT,” a token name, or a visual similarity.

Keep the identity question separate from the affiliation question. A token address and ID can identify a token under the ERC-721 definition, but project affiliation still requires evidence tying that contract or token to the named project. Likewise, a project description is not itself evidence that a particular token provides a particular right. If a source does not connect the name, token, and claimed function, preserve the uncertainty.

Third-party mentions can point toward material worth checking, but should not be treated as proof of project affiliation, legal ownership, or function. A careful identification should state what the primary documentation actually says and avoid adding implications that it does not support.

## What an NFT ownership record establishes

An NFT record concerns the token and the ownership information represented under its contract’s rules. It should not be treated as proof, by itself, of copyright, ownership of an associated physical or digital work, control of linked content, or endorsement by a named project. Those conclusions require evidence beyond the token record.

This distinction matters when a token refers to content or describes a benefit. The record and the referenced material are not interchangeable: the token may identify an on-chain record, while separate documentation and terms are needed to understand what linked content or a claimed benefit means. A statement that a token conveys a right needs supporting terms and evidence about what the right is; the token record alone does not establish that broader conclusion.

A usage role can also be distinct from token ownership. ERC-4907, for example, adds a time-limited user role separate from the NFT owner; the role includes an expiration time that can end usage permission without transferring ownership. Applications must recognize and enforce that user role, and the standard does not by itself create a legal lease. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4907>)) This illustrates why a claim about use or access should be checked separately from a claim about who holds the token.

Do not treat an address or token record as identifying a human or organization unless separate evidence supports that attribution. Even if a record identifies a token or address, that is narrower than establishing who stands behind a project or what rights a holder has.
