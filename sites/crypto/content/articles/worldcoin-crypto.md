## How its infrastructure differs from card spending

Crypto-related infrastructure and card spending answer different questions. Infrastructure describes the technical or service components used to handle digital assets. Card spending concerns a payment product and the parties and processes that enable transactions through that product. A project may be described in terms of crypto infrastructure without that description proving that it issues a card or participates in a card-payment arrangement.

A useful boundary example is a convertible virtual currency kiosk. In the United States, FinCEN describes such kiosks as allowing customers to exchange fiat currency for virtual currency and, at supported two-way kiosks, virtual currency for fiat currency. That is an exchange function, not evidence of a payment card or of any Worldcoin relationship. The example helps separate access to conversion from card functionality, but it does not describe Worldcoin. ([source](<https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2025-08/FinCEN-Notice-CVCKIOSK.pdf>))

For a Worldcoin-specific card claim, the evidence would need to identify the product and the parties involved. For example, it would need to attribute the claimed card or integration to Worldcoin or to a named service provider, and clarify whether the relationship is an official product, a direct integration, or an independent third-party offering. The evidence available here does not document a Worldcoin card issuer, payment-network relationship, card-purchase conversion arrangement, or settlement process.

Do not turn a broad statement such as “crypto can be used for payments” into a claim that a particular asset or project is supported by a particular card service. General capability in one context does not verify a specific product, asset, network, or provider relationship.

## Is there a documented connection to crypto cards?

No direct connection is established by the allowed evidence for this article. When assessing a claim, separate three possibilities:

- **A project card product:** current project documentation names a card and explains the project’s role.
- **A direct integration:** attributable documentation from the project and the relevant payment-service parties describes a specific connection.
- **A third-party service:** another provider may offer a way to use or convert an asset, but that alone does not make the service a Worldcoin product or establish an official integration.

The evidence available here does not verify any of those possibilities for Worldcoin. A specific claim that a Worldcoin-related asset works with a card would need service-specific documentation that identifies the provider, the supported asset and network, and the nature of the relationship. Without it, the careful conclusion is that a direct connection remains unverified.

This distinction also prevents a common evidence error: treating a statement about one provider’s capabilities as proof of a wider project feature. Documentation should be read for its stated subject and scope. If it describes a third party’s service, it does not automatically establish that Worldcoin operates, endorses, or directly integrates with that service.

## What readers should verify before relying on a payment claim

First, trace the claim to current official materials. Look for documentation that names the Worldcoin component at issue and describes what it does. If the claim concerns a card or integration, look for materials from the project and the named card or payment provider that confirm the relationship. A social post, an asset ticker, or a general statement about crypto spending is not a substitute for product-specific evidence.

Next, identify exactly what the claimed service supports. A payment claim should name the provider and the relevant asset and network, rather than referring only to a project name or a familiar token label. It should also make clear whether the provider is offering its own service or documenting a direct project integration. The allowed evidence here does not establish Worldcoin-specific support, availability, or eligibility in any jurisdiction.

Then consult the relevant service’s own documentation for its actual operating conditions. If a specific provider claims to convert an asset or use it in a card-payment flow, verify the service’s stated mechanics, fees, and settlement conditions there. The evidence available for this article does not support claims about Worldcoin-specific conversion, card fees, settlement timing, or payment availability. Those details should not be filled in by analogy to another project or service.

Finally, keep security, recovery, compliance, and tax statements tied to the specific service and jurisdiction they describe. The evidence available here does not establish Worldcoin-specific security controls, recovery processes, or jurisdiction-specific regulatory or tax treatment. A disclosure about another provider cannot be assumed to apply to Worldcoin, and no legal or tax conclusion follows from the information presented here.
