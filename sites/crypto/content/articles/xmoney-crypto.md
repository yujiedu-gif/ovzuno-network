## Assess security, custody, and recovery evidence

First determine whether the proposed service involves custody. A third-party crypto custodian manages access to private keys on customers’ behalf. However, custody structure, customer rights, segregation, insurance, regulation, and insolvency treatment vary by provider and jurisdiction. These are matters to verify for the specific arrangement, not protections to assume. [source](<https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins/crypto-asset-custody-basics-retail-investors-investor-bulletin-0>)

If custody is involved, ask which entity controls the keys, what assets or accounts the arrangement covers, and how the contract describes customer access and rights. If the service is not custodial, clarify which credentials or assets the business controls directly and what responsibilities remain with the provider. The available evidence does not establish XMoney’s custody model, key-management controls, or recovery design.

Request service-specific documentation about access controls, key management, incident response, and recovery procedures where relevant. Clarify who can initiate or approve sensitive actions, how the provider communicates interruptions or incidents, and what process applies if authorized personnel lose access. Ask what evidence supports each claim and whether it is a provider description or independent verification. Do not equate a security statement with proof of a particular control or outcome.

Recovery questions should be concrete.
