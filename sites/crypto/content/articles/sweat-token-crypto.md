Information about one does not automatically establish the characteristics or operation of the other. ## How token mechanics should be verified

Claims about issuance, transfers, or other on-chain behavior require a more specific verification path than a general project description. First establish the relevant network and the token’s current contract or other authoritative identifier. Then consult documentation that matches that network and implementation. If those identifiers are not known, a reader cannot confidently connect a technical record to the SWEAT being discussed.

Keep recorded activity distinct from product meaning. A blockchain record can support a claim that a transaction or other specified activity was recorded under a network’s rules. It does not, by itself, establish that a related app feature worked as advertised, that a user earned a reward, or that a token grants a particular off-chain right. A project’s technical and product documentation must support those additional conclusions.

A disciplined review should therefore ask: Is the source authoritative and dated? Does it identify the network and the relevant token implementation? Does it describe a current mechanism or only an intended one? Are any conditions or limitations stated? If a source answers only some of these questions, keep the conclusion equally narrow.
