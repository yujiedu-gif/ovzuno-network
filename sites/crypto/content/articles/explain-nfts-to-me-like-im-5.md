An NFT is a distinct token record on a blockchain. Think of it like a numbered entry in a shared record book: it can identify a particular token, but it is not necessarily the picture or video associated with it. Owning the token does not automatically mean owning the content’s copyright or every right to use it.

## What is an NFT?

NFT stands for **non-fungible token**. “Non-fungible” means individually distinguishable rather than interchangeable one-for-one. A token can have its own identity under the rules of the blockchain and the contract that manages it. On Ethereum, for example, an NFT is identified by the combination of its contract address and token ID.

The NFT is the token record, not necessarily the thing people see on a screen. It may be linked to an image, video, or other content, but the record and that content are not automatically the same object. Minting an NFT creates a token record and initial ownership under a smart contract’s rules; minting alone does not create, authenticate, store, or transfer rights in the referenced work.

## A simple analogy for how NFTs work

Imagine a shared record book with an entry that says, “Token 27 is recorded to this account.” The number helps distinguish that token from others. The record describes the token according to the system’s rules, much as a numbered entry helps someone find one item in a list.

The analogy has limits. An NFT is not a physical certificate, and a blockchain entry alone does not prove that the linked work is authentic or that the token holder has particular rights to it. It records information about the token according to the relevant contract; it does not answer every question about the content, its creator, or the legal terms around it.

## How an NFT differs from a digital copy

A digital file and an NFT record are different things. The file is the content; the NFT is a distinct token record that may be associated with that content. A token record does not necessarily make the linked file unique or exclusive. Nor does the record itself necessarily prevent other people from viewing or copying the content.

The content may also be stored separately from the token record. The way a token refers to its content, and where that content is stored, can vary by design. Some NFT-associated uses may depend on additional systems, such as storage services, registries, identity systems, or issuers. The token record by itself does not guarantee that those external systems or the referenced content will remain available.

A helpful way to keep the distinction clear is to ask two separate questions: “What token does the blockchain record?” and “Where is the content, and what does the link point to?” The first concerns the token record. The second concerns associated material and the systems that provide it. The answers may be related, but they are not the same fact.

## What owning an NFT does—and does not—mean

In everyday language, owning an NFT usually means that the token is recorded to an address under the relevant blockchain and contract rules. On Ethereum, an NFT contract records ownership by associating a unique token identifier with an Ethereum address. That describes the token’s on-chain ownership record, not every right or control a person might have outside the blockchain.

In particular, a token transfer does not necessarily transfer copyright or rights in the associated physical or digital work. Copyright, licensing, commercial use, and other rights depend on applicable terms and law. The NFT record alone does not settle what a holder may do with the linked content. This is a technical explanation, not a conclusion about any particular NFT’s legal status or terms.

It is also important not to confuse the token record with authentication of a physical item. A standard for linking NFTs to physical assets or devices describes an interface for that connection, but the security of such a link depends on the device, key protection, issuer or manufacturer, firmware, and the process that binds the physical item to the on-chain record. A token record by itself does not prove that a physical object is genuine.

So, when someone says “I own the NFT,” the clearest follow-up is: which token is recorded to which address, and what do the associated terms say about the content and rights? The first part concerns the blockchain record. The second cannot be answered from the token record alone.

## The idea in one sentence

An NFT is a distinct blockchain token record that may be linked to digital or physical content; the record, the content, and rights in that content are not necessarily the same thing.
