Bitcoin is a peer-to-peer electronic cash system whose protocol rules govern how transactions are authorized and recorded. “Bitcoin” commonly refers to the protocol and network; “bitcoin” refers to the native unit transferred through that system. The network, rules, unit, and ledger are related, but they are not interchangeable terms.

## What bitcoin means in crypto

Bitcoin names a particular system for transferring and recording its native digital unit. In technical terms, cryptocurrency is a digital asset or unit that can be cryptographically transferred between users of a blockchain network. Bitcoin is one example of that category, not a synonym for every cryptocurrency.

The name is used in more than one way. “Bitcoin” often means the overall protocol or network, while “bitcoin” often means the unit that the system records and transfers. This capitalization distinction is a useful convention rather than a separate protocol rule. The ticker “BTC” is also commonly used for the unit; it is a label, not a different asset or network.

These terms describe different parts of one system. The protocol establishes the rules. Network participants use those rules to check transaction data. The ledger records transactions accepted under the rules, and bitcoin is the unit represented in those transactions. This is a technical definition, not guidance about acquiring or evaluating the asset.

## Bitcoin network, protocol, and native unit

The **network** is the peer-to-peer system through which participants exchange transaction information. The **protocol** is the set of rules that determines how transactions authorize transfers and what conditions a transaction must satisfy. Neither term means the asset itself.

The **native unit**, bitcoin, is represented through Bitcoin’s transaction model. A transaction input refers to a specific output from an earlier transaction. The transaction creates new outputs, which can be spent by later valid inputs. An input is validated by checking that it references an unspent output and satisfies that output’s spending conditions, including the required scripts and signatures.

This model differs from treating an asset as a single balance entry detached from transaction history. To understand a Bitcoin transfer, the relevant technical relation is between the earlier output being spent and the new outputs created. The protocol determines whether that relation meets its transaction rules.

The network, protocol, unit, and ledger therefore have distinct roles: participants exchange information over the network; protocol rules govern valid transactions; transactions represent transfers of bitcoin; and the ledger is the resulting record. Calling all four “Bitcoin” may be convenient in casual conversation, but it can obscure which part is being discussed.

## How transactions enter Bitcoin’s ledger

Bitcoin transactions use digital signatures to authorize transfers through a chain of signed ownership records. A transaction input points to an earlier output and must satisfy the spending conditions associated with it. A signature supports authorization under the system’s rules; it does not, by itself, describe every step involved in checking or recording a transaction.

Once transaction data is shared among network participants, it must be assessed under the applicable protocol rules. A transaction’s presence in the network is not the same as its place in the ledger: the ledger is the record of transactions accepted under those rules. The distinction is useful when describing what a transaction is versus what the system has recorded.

Bitcoin’s ledger is also not, by definition, a directory of verified real-world identities. The protocol-level description concerns transaction authorization and records; it does not establish who a person or organization is in the world outside the system. Technical terms such as “signature,” “address,” and “transaction” should not be treated as proof of a named person’s identity.

## Bitcoin and related crypto terms

Bitcoin can refer to the named protocol and network, while bitcoin refers to the native unit those rules govern. The ticker BTC commonly labels that unit. These conventions help distinguish ideas, but the surrounding context matters more than capitalization alone.

A native unit is also distinct from a token issued under a separate standard or platform. Both may be called crypto assets in broad conversation, but that shared category does not make their technical accounting models identical. Bitcoin’s transaction model represents its native unit through outputs that later inputs may spend.

“Bit coin” may appear as an alternate spelling or search variant. It does not identify a distinct technical category in this definition.

## What this definition does not establish

This definition describes Bitcoin as a protocol, peer-to-peer network, native unit, and transaction ledger. It does not establish legal or tax treatment, service availability, fees, or compliance duties; those matters require separate evidence suited to the relevant place and circumstances.

It also does not establish that a wallet, custodian, application, or user process is secure, recoverable, or capable of reversing a transaction. Those are separate questions from what Bitcoin’s protocol and transaction model mean. Price, investment suitability, acquisition guidance, and service comparisons are outside this article’s scope.
