ERC-721 NFT examples include tokens associated with digital art, collectibles, game items, access passes, event records, virtual objects, and physical items. Each represents a distinct token record that a project may link to an asset or use. The link does not, by itself, establish copyright, ownership of a physical object, or guaranteed access.

## What counts as an ERC721 NFT example?

A useful example identifies both the token and the particular item, record, or function associated with it. ERC-721 supports standardized ownership and transfer records for individually distinguishable token IDs that applications can associate with digital or physical subjects. On an Ethereum chain, a particular ERC-721 token is uniquely identified by the combination of its smart-contract address and token ID—not by its image, collection name, or marketplace label. [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets] [claim_erc721_identity]

The token record and its associated subject are not the same thing. An NFT contract records ownership by associating a unique token identifier with an Ethereum address. That record does not automatically convey copyright, title to a physical item, or every other right someone might connect with the token. [claim_eth_nft_ownership] [claim_nft_ip_separation]

The categories below are illustrative. They describe ways projects may associate tokens with things or services, not a guarantee that every project in a category offers the same function. A named use must be checked against that project's own documentation and terms; a category alone cannot verify a particular collection or token.

## ERC721 NFT example categories

- **Digital art:** A token may be associated with a particular artwork or media file. It records the token and its association, not necessarily the media itself or rights in the work. NFT technology may support intellectual-property management, transfer, or licensing workflows when valid terms and authorized rights holders are present. The token alone does not create those rights. [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets] [claim_nft_ip_support_role] [claim_nft_ip_separation]

- **Collectibles:** A token may represent an individually distinguishable collectible or an entry in a series. Its contract address and token ID distinguish that token from others. A series name or visual design can describe how a project presents the collectible, but those labels are not the token's unique identifier. [claim_erc721_identity] [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets]

- **Game items:** A token may be associated with an in-game object that a specific game or application recognizes. The token record alone does not show that a particular game supports the item, what the item does, or whether the application will continue to recognize it. Those details depend on the application and its terms. [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets]

- **Membership or access passes:** A project may use a token as an eligibility signal for a service. The token does not independently guarantee admission or continuing access: the service must recognize it, and the relevant terms and technical design determine what access, if any, it provides. [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets]

- **Event tickets or certificates:** A token may be associated with an event-entry record or another certificate-like record. Its existence alone does not establish that an event organizer accepts it, that it can be transferred, or that it grants entry. Those conditions depend on the issuer and the systems used to check the record. [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets]

- **Virtual land or objects:** A token may be linked to a parcel or object within a specific virtual environment. The token identifies an on-chain record; the environment's rules and systems determine how the associated item is treated there. An example of a token linked to virtual property should not be read as a claim about ownership or rights outside that environment. [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets] [claim_nft_ip_separation]

- **Real-world item records:** A token may be associated with a physical object, such as a collectible or product. The association alone does not prove possession, authenticity, or legal title to the object. The off-chain link and any legal effect depend on external systems and agreements. [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets] [claim_nft_ip_separation]

Across these categories, the token's role can be limited to identifying a record, or a project may connect it to an application or a rights-management process. Those are different claims. For instance, a token associated with a work does not establish the same thing as a valid license to use that work; a token associated with an entry does not establish the same thing as an organizer's acceptance. In each case, the application, issuer, and governing terms supply context beyond the token record. [claim_nft_ip_support_role] [claim_nft_ip_separation]

## How to read an ERC721 NFT example accurately

Begin with the contract address and token ID. Together, they identify a particular ERC-721 token on an Ethereum chain. A collection title, image, or marketplace description may help explain how the project presents the token, but it is not a replacement for that identifier. [claim_erc721_identity]

Next, determine what the project says the token is associated with. Is it linked to a digital work, a game object, an event record, a physical item, or something else? That description tells you the project's intended association; it does not establish that the associated content is permanently available or that the holder receives every related right. [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets] [claim_nft_ip_separation]

Then separate the stated purpose from what must happen for it to work. For an access pass or game item, the service or application must recognize the token. For an event record, the organizer's process must accept it. The token's existence alone does not ensure any of those off-chain actions. The project’s terms and technical design are therefore part of the example, not details that can be inferred from the token type. [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets]

For intellectual-property claims, look for valid contracts, licenses, or other governing terms and whether the relevant rights holder authorized them. NFT technology may support a workflow for managing, transferring, or licensing rights, but the token does not itself create copyright or establish that a minter owns the rights being referenced. [claim_nft_ip_support_role] [claim_nft_ip_separation]

For a physical item, ask how the project links the token record to the object and what agreements govern that link. The token is an on-chain record; the physical-item connection depends on external systems and agreements. Do not treat the token record alone as proof that the object exists, is authentic, or belongs to the token holder. [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets] [claim_nft_ip_separation]

## What these examples do not prove

These categories describe possible associations between ERC-721 tokens and assets, records, or applications. They do not verify that a particular project implements the stated use, that a service will honor a token, or that an off-chain claim has legal effect. The token's unique identifier establishes which token is being discussed; it does not, by itself, establish copyright or ownership of an associated physical item. [claim_erc721_identity] [claim_nft_distinguishable_assets] [claim_eth_nft_ownership] [claim_nft_ip_separation]

Treat the examples as descriptive categories, not endorsements, rankings, or investment guidance. To understand a particular token, distinguish its on-chain identity from what a project associates with it, and consider the separate application, external systems, and terms that may govern that association.
