## What people mean by a Skrill crypto transfer

The phrase can describe different actions. One possibility is buying, selling, or otherwise changing a crypto-denominated balance inside a service. Another is sending cryptocurrency to, or receiving it from, an external wallet. These actions may have different transaction paths, and the evidence available here does not establish which ones Skrill currently supports.

The key distinction is where the activity is recorded and where the value is meant to go. An internal balance movement may be recorded in a provider’s own account system. An external wallet transfer, if available, would involve a specified destination and could involve a blockchain network. A display showing a crypto amount does not by itself tell you which path applies.

A blockchain transfer uses digital signatures to authorize a transfer between users. A valid signature indicates control of the relevant signing key under the protocol; it does not establish the human identity of the key holder or prove that a particular provider offers a transfer feature. ## How to identify the transaction path

Start by identifying the destination. Is it another balance or account within Skrill, or an address for a wallet outside the service? If it is an external address, look for explicit instructions identifying the asset and network. The recipient’s instructions also matter: asset and network combinations are not interchangeable, and compatibility must be checked with the recipient or service before authorization.

Next, distinguish custody from transaction routing. In a self-custody arrangement, the user controls the private keys and recovery material needed to access the crypto assets. That is different from a third-party account, where access depends on the provider’s custody arrangement and terms.
