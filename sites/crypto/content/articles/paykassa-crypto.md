## How cryptocurrency payment processing works

A generic cryptocurrency payment can be understood as a sequence: a payer initiates a transfer, a transaction is submitted to a blockchain network, the network validates and records it under its protocol rules, and the merchant or a service applies its own processing and settlement conditions. These steps describe a general conceptual flow; they do not establish the exact workflow of Paykassa or any other provider.

A blockchain record and a provider’s internal processing decision are distinct. A recorded transaction does not, by itself, establish that a particular service has credited a merchant’s account, converted an asset, or made funds available. Those outcomes depend on the provider’s documented workflow and terms. Bitcoin illustrates one part of the general mechanism: its transactions use digital signatures to authorize transfers through a chain of signed ownership records. Signatures are not the whole payment system; transaction-validation and double-spend-prevention rules also matter.
