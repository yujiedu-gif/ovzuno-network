## What should users verify before using Skrill cryptocurrency?

Start with official, current information for the specific service and the country where the account is used. A general description or a feature mentioned for another market is not enough to establish your eligibility or the terms that apply to you.

Use this checklist:

- **Availability and eligibility:** Is the feature currently offered in your jurisdiction and for your account type? Are there user or transaction conditions that apply?
- **Contracting entity:** Which legal entity provides each part of the service, and which terms govern the account and the crypto-related activity?
- **Asset and transaction scope:** Which assets and transaction types are expressly supported? Are destination types or network requirements specified?
- **Limits:** What transaction or account limits apply, and do the terms explain when a transaction may be declined or delayed?
- **Funding and withdrawal:** How is the activity funded? Can value be withdrawn or transferred, and does that mean another provider account or a blockchain address?
- **Completion and settlement:** What status counts as complete under the service terms? If a network transaction is involved, is its status tracked separately from the provider’s account record?
- **Account checks and restrictions:** What identity checks or other account requirements apply? What conditions could restrict access or prevent a transaction?
- **Support and complaints:** Which party handles questions or complaints about the account, payment method, crypto-related feature, or network transfer?

These are verification questions, not assertions that any particular feature or option exists. If the terms do not identify the asset, destination, or transaction type clearly, pause and seek clarification from the responsible service before proceeding. Recheck at the time of use because current terms and availability, rather than historical descriptions, govern the decision.
