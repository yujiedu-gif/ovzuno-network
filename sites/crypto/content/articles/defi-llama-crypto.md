DeFiLlama in crypto refers to a data and analytics platform used to view information about DeFi protocols—not to a DeFi protocol itself. Treat its figures as research inputs: check what each metric measures, which chains or records it covers, and when it was updated before drawing conclusions about a protocol.

## What “DeFi Llama crypto” refers to

“DeFi Llama crypto” is a search phrase for DeFiLlama and its role in crypto research. The key distinction is between a platform that presents information and the protocols, networks, or assets that the information concerns. A dashboard or analytics service does not itself become the protocol it describes, and its data display should not be read as an endorsement.

A useful general model is to separate a DeFi application into layers. One framework distinguishes settlement, application, and interface layers, each with different technical dependencies. That distinction helps explain why an analytics view is not the same thing as an application or the underlying settlement network: each represents a different part of the system being examined. ## How to interpret DeFiLlama metrics

Read a displayed value as a reported measurement with a defined scope, not as a complete statement about protocol health. Start by identifying the exact metric name and its definition. Then check its units and reporting period: a value without those details can be difficult to compare meaningfully with another figure.

Next, look for notes about included chains, data sources, and methodology. Figures can depend on how the platform categorizes underlying records and which records are included. As a general example of why methodology matters, public transaction visibility and execution ordering can affect an automated-market-maker swap before a block includes it; that observation concerns public-blockchain AMM execution and does not describe DeFiLlama’s data process. [source](<https://www.bis.org/publications/trading-defi-era-automated-market-maker>)

Keep the conclusion no broader than the metric. A reported figure by itself does not establish liquidity under all conditions, protocol safety, revenue, user activity, or investment quality. Those are separate questions requiring evidence appropriate to each one.

## Limits and verification before relying on a figure

For a consequential decision, use a short verification workflow:

1. **Confirm the definition.** Find the platform’s current explanation of the field, including units, reporting period, and methodology.
2. **Check the scope.** Identify the chains, assets, protocols, or records included—and note any stated exclusions.
3. **Check timing.** Look for the displayed timestamp and whether the figure is subject to updates or revisions. The evidence available for this article does not establish DeFiLlama’s refresh interval.
4. **Compare with relevant primary information.** When the figure matters, consult the protocol’s own documentation or other relevant primary records. A data platform’s categorization and a protocol’s own account of its behavior answer different questions.
5. **Limit the inference.** Do not turn a measurement into a claim about security, compliance, or future performance unless independent evidence supports that claim.

An analytics display may omit information, use a particular categorization, or reflect data at a particular point in time. The evidence available here does not establish a completeness or accuracy guarantee for DeFiLlama. Treat uncertainty about coverage or method as a reason to verify, not as permission to fill gaps with assumptions.

## When DeFiLlama is useful—and what it cannot answer

DeFiLlama can be approached as a starting point for locating and reviewing protocol-related data, subject to checking its current definitions and sources. Its role is informational: a dashboard figure is not, on its own, a security audit, a guarantee of available liquidity, or an evaluation of whether a protocol is suitable for a particular person or business.

Questions about current fees, yields, security incidents, recovery options, legal or tax treatment, and compliance duties require current evidence specific to the protocol, service, and—where relevant—the applicable jurisdiction. Those questions are outside what a generic analytics figure can settle. This definition is educational, not personalized financial, legal, or tax advice.
