## Protocol basics and the native asset

At a high level, a blockchain protocol establishes how transactions are handled and how a shared history is maintained. Bitcoin’s original proof-of-work design, for example, represents the selected history as the chain with the largest pool of cumulative proof of work; replacing accepted history requires rebuilding work and catching up. That is evidence about Bitcoin’s original design, not a basis for assuming that Bitcoin SV uses identical current rules or that this description fully characterizes its protocol.

A transaction model also illustrates why the protocol and asset should not be conflated. In Bitcoin’s UTXO model, a transaction input spends a specific earlier output, identified by transaction ID and output index; newly created outputs remain unspent until later inputs spend them.
