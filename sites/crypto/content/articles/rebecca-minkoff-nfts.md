## Separate the NFT token from its digital content

A token and the digital content associated with it are distinct things. For ERC-721, an optional metadata interface can expose a token URI referring to metadata for a specific token ID. That metadata may include or point to descriptive information and media, but the standard does not guarantee the referenced content’s availability, permanence, accuracy, or licensing. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-721>))

This is why a statement such as “the NFT is the image” needs careful interpretation. A token record can identify a token and refer to metadata, while the referenced image or other content may be supplied through a separate system. The token and its reference do not, by themselves, show how that content is hosted or whether it will remain accessible. For a specific Rebecca Minkoff item, verifying what the token points to would require the relevant token and metadata records; those records are not established in the available evidence.

Minting creates a token record and initial ownership under the smart contract’s rules. It does not by itself create, authenticate, store, or transfer rights in the referenced work or asset. ([source](<https://ethereum.org/nft/>)) In practical terms, the existence of a token cannot alone demonstrate that its holder owns copyright, the underlying creative work, or a physical item. Those are separate claims requiring their own supporting documentation.

The same distinction applies to access. A reference to content is not evidence that a holder has a continuing access benefit, membership, or entitlement. Such a benefit would need to be described in the applicable release terms or other documentation. No Rebecca Minkoff-specific access terms are established here.

## Check what ownership rights are actually granted

A blockchain record can describe token ownership under a contract’s rules, but readers asking what they may do with a work need to locate the relevant license or release terms. The documentation should state the scope of any permission, such as whether a use is permitted or restricted. The evidence available for this article contains no Rebecca Minkoff-specific license or terms, so it does not establish personal-use permissions, commercial-use rights, membership, event access, or other benefits.

Keep each question separate: who is recorded as holding the token, what content the token references, and what rights or benefits the terms grant. A positive answer to one does not settle the others. If the terms are unavailable or do not address a claimed benefit, that benefit remains unverified rather than implied by the word “ownership.”

Token standards can also cover technical functions without resolving these rights questions. For example, ERC-2981 standardizes retrieval of a royalty recipient and amount for a stated sale price. Payment remains voluntary and depends on marketplace or participant implementation. That mechanism does not establish what license, access, or other rights a Rebecca Minkoff NFT holder might receive. ([source](<https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-2981>))

A separate, narrowly scoped example shows why labels should not substitute for examining an arrangement: some third-party tokenized-security models may represent a custodial entitlement or synthetic instrument rather than direct ownership of the referenced security. This is not evidence about Rebecca Minkoff NFTs; it simply illustrates that the meaning of a token depends on the specific structure and terms.
