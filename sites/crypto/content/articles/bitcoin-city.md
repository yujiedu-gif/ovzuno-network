## What is confirmed—and what remains a claim

Readers can assess project status by checking distinct milestones rather than treating “the project” as a single yes-or-no fact:

- **Authorization:** Look for a current, attributable record from the relevant authority. The available evidence does not establish whether authorization was sought or granted.
- **Financing:** Seek documentation identifying the funding arrangement and its status. No financing is confirmed by the evidence here.
- **Construction:** Look for verifiable records tied to the named project and a specific site. The evidence does not establish that construction began or was completed.
- **Operation:** Verify any claimed services directly, including who provides them and whether they are available. No operating services are documented here.
- **Completion:** Require evidence that identifies what was completed and when. A project name or announced target does not confirm completion.

These are evidence questions, not findings that the proposal has failed or succeeded. If records are missing, conflicting, or outdated, report that uncertainty instead of resolving it by assumption. Current official records and independently verifiable material are more useful for checking status than unattributed summaries. A repeated description may show that a claim has circulated; it does not independently show that a milestone occurred.

It is also important not to collapse milestones. Evidence of an announcement would support that an announcement was made, not that a project was approved. An authorization record would not alone establish financing or construction. Evidence that a site is under construction would not, by itself, establish that services are operating or that the planned development is complete. Each conclusion needs evidence appropriate to that particular stage.
