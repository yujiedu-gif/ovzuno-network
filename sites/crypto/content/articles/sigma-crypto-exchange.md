## What evidence can establish exchange operations?

Exchange-related labels do not establish what a service actually does. To assess an operating claim, look for documentation that describes the relevant function and identifies who is responsible for it. For example, a claim that a service handles trades should be supported by materials explaining whether it receives orders, matches them, routes them elsewhere, or provides only an interface to another venue.

The distinction matters because a user-facing interface and the underlying trading venue need not be the same entity. Exchange-facing access can connect participants with a market-facing trading platform through direct platform interaction or through cryptoasset service providers. That high-level distinction does not specify a particular interface technology, provider, or Sigma arrangement.

Documentation should identify the service’s role rather than leaving readers to infer it from terms such as “exchange,” “platform,” or “trading.” If materials do not specify who handles orders or where execution takes place, that part of the operating model remains unverified. A broad description that a venue may involve trading or related activities such as custody, settlement, surveillance, or intermediation should not be read as proof that every venue performs every listed function.

A similar standard applies to custody and settlement. Seek official documents that describe whether assets are held by the service or another provider, and how the service describes completion of a trade or transfer. Do not infer custody from the presence of an account screen, or infer settlement arrangements from a product description alone. Exchange-related functions can be combined or separated across different firms and services, so the responsible party for each activity should be identified separately.
