## What an NFT purchase may—and may not—establish as ownership

Minting an NFT creates a token record and initial ownership under the smart contract’s rules. That technical record should not be treated as proof of copyright, ownership of a physical item, or unrestricted rights to use associated media. A token, the content displayed alongside it, and the rights attached to that content are separate things to examine.

In the United States, a joint report from the U.S. Copyright Office and USPTO explains that transferring an NFT does not necessarily transfer copyright or rights in the associated physical or digital asset. The applicable law and valid contracts, licenses, or other governing terms matter. This general point is not a determination about any specific VeVe collectible or its governing terms.

For a specific item, review the current terms and any license or content-use conditions that apply. Look for answers to questions such as: What does holding the collectible permit? Are display or other uses authorized? Are commercial uses addressed? Do permissions depend on continued account access or platform services? The evidence available for this assessment does not answer those VeVe-specific questions.

Check, too, how the displayed item relates to any token record and associated content. A description or image shown in an application does not by itself establish what a token represents, whether referenced content will remain available, or what rights a holder has. Request documentation that explains the connection and the terms governing use. If those details are not clear, note the uncertainty rather than treating the screen presentation as a complete account of ownership.
