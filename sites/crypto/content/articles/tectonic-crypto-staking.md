## How to distinguish validator staking from lending or token deposits

For this question, the useful distinction is the documented role of the asset. Validator staking is tied to a proof-of-stake network’s consensus participation. Delegation is one possible arrangement, but the details depend on the particular protocol. For example, Solana’s documented native model allows a delegator to assign stake to a validator, increasing that validator’s consensus weight while the delegator retains token control under the stake-account model. That example illustrates what validator delegation can look like; it does not establish that Tectonic uses this design.

A lending or supply activity has a different protocol function. Do not classify an activity as validator staking merely because a description uses the word “staking,” mentions rewards, or refers to assets being deposited or unavailable for a period. Those terms alone do not show that funds contribute to validator participation.

Instead, look for evidence that specifies what the protocol does with the asset and which role receives or uses it. Does the documentation describe delegation to a validator or participation in consensus? Or does it describe supplying an asset to another protocol function? If the documentation does not make the role clear, the appropriate classification is “not established,” rather than a guess based on terminology.

Withdrawal and penalty conditions can help describe a product, but they do not replace this classification test. That is a Tezos-specific distinction, not evidence about Tectonic.
